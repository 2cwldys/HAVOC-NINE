PRIVACY AND THE GDPR: WHAT THE SITE STORES, WHAT IS DONE, WHAT THE OWNER STILL HAS TO DO
=======================================================================================

This is an engineering note, not legal advice. It says what havoc9.org and h9web store and how that was checked against the GDPR's
main duties. The notice visitors read is havoc9.org/privacy/index.html; keep the two in step.

WHAT IS STORED (from h9web's database, lib/db.js and the modules)
  accounts            username, a salted hash of the password. No email, no real name, no birth date.
  sessions            a random token's hash; 30 days at most, 14 idle (config.session).
  forum               posts, threads, wall comments, signatures, profile pictures, votes, reports: all content the user wrote.
  game link, presence a hashed game-installation id, the name played under, ranked results (rating, career level and XP, matches), playtime,
                      online state, for servers enrolled with havoc9.org (docs/H9_PRESENCE.txt, docs/H9_COMPETITIVE.txt).
  discord             Discord user id and display name, only when the user links it.
  audit_log           what admins and moderators did to accounts; 90 days (config.admin.auditRetentionDays).
  logs                nginx's access logs (IP address, browser): Debian's logrotate keeps 14 days; the journal keeps 30 days
                      (vps/linux/hardening/journald-havoc9.conf). Cloudflare also sees every request.
  backups             daily, 14 kept locally; the encrypted off-site copy keeps 7 (vps/linux/systemd).

DONE IN THE REPOSITORY
  * The pages no longer load fonts from Google (that sends every visitor's IP address to a third party): the three typefaces are served from
    havoc9.org/assets/fonts/ (SIL OFL, licenses beside them and in THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES/), and the pages' CSP meta tags no longer allow
    fonts.googleapis.com or fonts.gstatic.com.
  * havoc9.org/privacy/index.html says what is stored, why, on what basis, who else sees it, how long, and the user's rights; every page's
    footer links to it.
  * The only cookie is the session cookie (needed to sign in): no consent banner is needed for it, and there is no analytics or advertising.
  * Data minimisation was already the design: no email address is collected.

WHAT THE OWNER STILL HAS TO DO
  1. The notice names the maintainers and gives the Discord and the GitHub issues as the contact. The GDPR wants the controller's name and a
     contact address: put your name (or the project's legal name) and an email address in the "WHO IS RESPONSIBLE" paragraph of
     privacy/index.html. A country's law may also want an imprint.
  2. Name the hosting provider in "WHO ELSE SEES IT", and accept the data processing terms of Cloudflare and of the provider (both offer a
     data processing addendum in the account settings).
  3. After the VPS is installed, check the retention the notice states: cat /etc/logrotate.d/nginx (daily, rotate 14) and
     journalctl --disk-usage with the journald file in place.
  4. Answering a user's request (within one month):
       delete   the account:  node bin/h9web-admin.js delete <username> --yes   (or the admin panel, ACCOUNTS). An account's posts stay, shown
                as from a deleted account (docs/H9WEB_ADMIN.txt, section 11): when the user asks for erasure, remove their posts first, one by one
                in the forum moderation tools, then delete the account. Backups age out within 14 days.
       copy     (access/portability): there is no self-service export yet. Read the account's rows from h9web.db (accounts, forum_posts,
                forum_signatures, forum_walls, forum_wall_comments, discord_links, presence_*, compete_*) and send them as JSON or CSV.
  5. Images in forum posts come from a few allowed hosts and the media page embeds YouTube: the notice says so. If you want none of that,
     remove the hosts from the pages' img-src and the media page's frame-src.
  6. Keep a short written record of what is processed and why (the notice and this file are most of it) and, if the site ever has a
     data breach, tell the supervisory authority within 72 hours.
