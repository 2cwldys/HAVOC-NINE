===============================================================================
 TEXT TO SPEECH: WHAT PLAYERS SAY IN CHAT, SPOKEN TO THE PLAYERS NEAR THEM
===============================================================================

 Who this is for: whoever runs an H9 game server, whoever plays on one, and
 whoever builds the client or the web page.

 The short version
   * What it is: when a player types in chat (say or say_team), the players
     near him HEAR it, at once, in a voice for his model: a man's, a woman's,
     a Mech's or a bot's, quieter the farther away he is, and muffled when his
     model wears a gas mask. With h9_tts_global 1 every player who has text to
     speech hears it instead, wherever he is, at full volume (section 2). It is
     not voice chat: no sound travels on the network, only the text the chat
     already shows. Each listener's own machine speaks it, with the voices
     installed on that machine, and speakers who talk at the same moment are
     heard together, mixed (section 5).
   * It is off. A server turns it on with h9_texttospeech 1 (default 0).
   * It needs H9's client.dll or the H9 web client (Windows SAPI on a native
     game, the browser's speech synthesis in the web client; a native game
     can also use the Kokoro neural voices, which each player turns on for
     himself, section 3e). A game without it is sent nothing and notices
     nothing. A player turns it off for himself with cl_h9_tts 0.
   * The server is h9_tts.amxx; its settings are configs\h9_tts.cfg and the
     list of gas-masked models configs\h9_tts_gasmask.cfg. COMMANDS.txt has the
     cvars (TEXT TO SPEECH); this file is the rest.

 1. TURNING IT ON (a server)
 ---------------------------

   * h9_tts.amxx goes in plugins.ini AFTER every plugin that can stop a chat
     line (a gag, a mute) and after h9_presence, and BEFORE ARP_Core: AMX
     Mod X runs the say handlers in plugins.ini order, and a handler that
     returns PLUGIN_HANDLED keeps the ones after it from running.
     ARP_TalkArea (Apollo RP) answers every chat line itself and returns
     PLUGIN_HANDLED, so on a roleplay map (h9_tsrp_*) a say handler listed
     after it is never called and nothing is spoken. h9_tts also asks
     h9_presence itself whether the speaker's account is muted (h9p_muted),
     when h9_presence is loaded.
   * build.bat: add h9_tts to TARGETS, and to the presets' plugins.*.ini
     (H9XASH\presets) so the plugin is in every preset.
   * h9_tts.cfg (the plugin executes it on every map) holds:

       h9_texttospeech   0     1: chat is spoken. 0: nothing is sent
       h9_tts_range      800   units from the speaker out to which a line is
                               heard
       h9_tts_maxchars   96    the most characters spoken of a line (8 to 150)
       h9_tts_gap        1.5   seconds a speaker waits between two spoken
                               lines
       h9_tts_self       1     1: the speaker hears his own line too, so one
                               player alone can tell it works. 0: only the
                               others hear it
       h9_tts_global     0     1: a line is heard by every player with text to
                               speech, wherever he is, at full volume and from
                               no direction; never by more players than the
                               text reaches (section 2). 0: only the players
                               within h9_tts_range

     The first and the last line are commented out in the shipped file; remove
     the // or put the cvar in server.cfg. A change of h9_texttospeech or
     h9_tts_global takes effect with the next line anyone says, no restart.

     From a player's console, h9_texttospeech (or h9_tts) prints the state: the
     server's switch, whether this game can speak (_h9tts) and whether he hears
     his own lines. h9_texttospeech 1 or 0 (an admin with the cvar flag) switches
     it. The cvar lives in the server, so a game that is not the server's own
     does not see it; the plugin answers the same name as a command.

 2. WHO HEARS WHAT
 -----------------

   The speaker: a living player, not muted (h9_presence), drawn (not hidden
   with EF_NODRAW and not fully transparent: an eliminated player, a
   spectator and a player waiting out a freeze are not), whose line is not a
   command (it does not start with . / ! or @) and has a letter, a digit or a
   non-ASCII character in it. Bots speak too: a bot's chat is spoken like
   anyone's. His line is cleaned (control characters become spaces, a run of
   spaces one, the ends are trimmed) and cut to h9_tts_maxchars, at a space if
   one is in the last twelve characters, never in the middle of a UTF-8
   character. One line per h9_tts_gap seconds counts; the ones in between are
   only written.

   A listener, with h9_tts_global 0: a living human whose game says it speaks
   (the userinfo key _h9tts is 1), within h9_tts_range units of the speaker,
   who is himself one while h9_tts_self is 1, walls or no walls. For
   say_team, only a player of the speaker's team (get_user_team). The volume
   sent is 100 within a quarter of the range (200 units at 800) and falls in a
   straight line to 0 at the range; under 4 is not sent. A listener is sent up
   to 4 lines at once and then one every 0.3 s, from all speakers together
   (the reliable channel is spared), so speakers who talk at the same moment
   are all heard.

   With h9_tts_global 1 the speaker's rules above are the same and the
   listener is every connected human whose game speaks, wherever he is, the
   speaker too while h9_tts_self is 1. The line goes out at volume 100, pan 0
   and flag bit 2 (section 4), so it is heard at full volume from no direction.
   It changes whom the SPEECH is sent to and nothing about the chat text. Case
   by case:

       a line from ...                      with h9_tts_global 1
       say                                  every human with _h9tts 1, alive or not
       say_team                             only the speaker's team (the text goes no further), alive or not
       a dead, hidden or muted speaker      not spoken, as with global 0
       a gagged player                      not spoken: the line never reaches the plugin (plugins.ini order)
       a command (. / ! @), or no words     not spoken, as with global 0
       a listener with cl_h9_tts 0          nothing is sent to him: his own switch decides
       any line on a roleplay map           global 0 applies: the talk area decides (below)

   A roleplay map is one whose name starts rp_ or tsrp_ or has _rp_ or _tsrp_
   in it (the rule ts_matchmode and ARP_Core use). There ARP_TalkArea answers
   every line after this plugin has run and shows it only to the living
   players within its talk range (300 units, times arp_message_range, and 450
   for a line that ends with !; a plain line said on the phone goes to the
   other end only), so a global line could only reach further than the text.
   On a roleplay map h9_tts_global therefore does nothing and the range rule
   above applies, as before. A line that starts with / (/shout, /w, /ooc, /me,
   /com, //) is a command and is never spoken, on any map.

   Match mode, classes, perks and teams of the match play no part: the plugin
   uses no tsm_ native. It behaves the same on a plain TS server.

 3. THE VOICE
 ------------

   The voice is decided by the speaker's MODEL NAME and nothing else (the
   userinfo key "model", in any case):

       the name has mech or ed209 in it     a Mech's voice
       else droid, robo or _bot             a bot's voice
       else fem, woman, women, girl, lady   a woman's voice
       else                                 a man's voice

   Of the 148 folders in models\player: the Mech's are mech_msr_bot,
   mech_ins_bot and ed209_ts; the bot's msrdroid_bot and insdroid_bot; the
   woman's the 32 with fem in the name (fem_nh_*, bfem_nh_*, prole_fem,
   prole_bfem, ins_prole_fem, ins_medic_fem, ins_medic_bfem, repuheavy_fem*,
   repuheavy_bfem_*, ...); the rest, 111, a man's. The tables are at the top
   of h9_tts.sma. A model with droid in its name is a bot's voice for every
   engine; the server also sends it as a droid (section 4) so that the Kokoro
   voices (3e) can speak it with the droid sound they have.

   What each platform does with it:

     Windows (SAPI)   the best installed voice of the gender: a Natural or
                      Neural voice ahead of a plain one, a voice without
                      "Desktop" in its name ahead of one with, English of the
                      US, then the UK, ahead of other languages (section 3d
                      says how to get better ones). A speaker who asked for a
                      voice variant (3c) gets the n-th of those instead, and a
                      voice the listener picked himself (3b) beats both. A
                      machine with no female voice speaks the woman as the
                      man's, higher; with no voice at all the game says
                      _h9tts 0 and is sent nothing. A Mech is the man's voice
                      at 0.80 of its speed and pitch, a bot's at 1.22, and both
                      through a robot filter (ring modulation,
                      cl_h9_tts_robot 1).
     web client       the browser's own voices, a man's or a woman's picked
                      by name (the Web Speech API says nothing of gender),
                      a Natural or Neural one ahead of the rest, and by
                      language; without a match the default voice, a woman's
                      raised. A Mech is the man's at pitch 0.5, rate 0.8, a
                      bot's at pitch 1.7, rate 1.25.
     elsewhere        nothing. A native Linux client has no speech backend
                      yet (it would be speech-dispatcher or espeak-ng behind
                      h9_tts_platform.h): it says _h9tts 0 and is sent
                      nothing. A Windows client under Wine or Proton speaks
                      only if Wine offers it a SAPI voice, which it usually
                      does not (untested). A Linux player hears the speech in
                      the web client, with the browser's voices.

 3a. THE GAS MASKS
 -----------------

   A model on the list in configs\h9_tts_gasmask.cfg sounds as if its wearer
   spoke through a mask. It is a man's or a woman's voice that is muffled: a
   Mech's and a bot's never is, whatever is listed.

   HOW TO EDIT IT. One model name a line, as the model's folder is called, in
   any case. A line that starts with // is a comment, a blank line is
   skipped, anything after the name on its line is ignored. A name that ends
   in * is every model whose name starts so (repuheavy* is the whole
   repuheavy family), and a name with a ! before it takes a model out again,
   whatever else lists it. Put a name in to
   put a mask on that model, take it out to give the voice back. TSM keeps the
   master copy (build.bat copies it over the server's on every build); the
   plugin reads it as each map loads. Nothing else decides it: not a class, a
   team or the match mode, and the plugin does not read ts_matchmode.cfg.

   WHAT IS ON IT TO BEGIN WITH: every MSR-side model of ts_matchmode.cfg's
   tsm_class1 to tsm_class9 (the names left of the | in each line's fourth
   field; the master and the three presets agree) -- 46 names: repuheavy_pack,
   repuheavy_fem_pack, repuheavy_soot_blood, repuheavy_blooded, RepuHeavyGld
   and its 7 variants, repughillie and its 7, HeavyTengu, RepuHeavyW and its 7,
   RepuHeavyM and its 7, RepuTengu and its 7, repuheavy_recon -- without the
   droid msrdroid_bot and the Mech mech_msr_bot, plus repuheavy, the MSR team
   model of server.cfg's mp_teammodels, which a plain server hands out. No
   insurgent model and no stock TS model is on it. The other MSR-looking
   folders that no class uses (repuheavy_dust, RepuTenguG, rh_agent_soot, ...)
   are not by name; add the ones a server hands out. Of these, repuheavy_fem_pack is a
   woman's voice, so it is muffled as a woman's; the other 46 as a man's.
   Since 2026-11-09 the list ends with the wildcards repuheavy*, reputengu*,
   repughillie*, rh_agent_* and rh_laur_* and heavytengu, so every skin and every
   woman's model of those families is masked (repuheavy_fem and its variants had no
   line before), and takes the two models without a helmet out again,
   !repuheavy_fem_nhelm and !repuheavy_bfem_nhelm. Type h9_tts at the console to see
   whether your own model is masked: it names your model and says yes or no.

   How it sounds: Windows runs the rendered voice through two low-passes in a
   row (a fourth-order roll-off at 1800 Hz / (1 + cl_h9_tts_muffle): 900 Hz by
   default) and a resonance at 700 Hz, then brings the line back to 85 percent
   of the loudness it had, so the mask is heard as a dull, boxy voice and not
   as a quiet one. cl_h9_tts_muffle (0 to 2, saved, default 1) is the strength:
   0 takes the mask off, 2 is heavy. The browser exposes no audio data and so
   cannot filter: the web client approximates it with a voice at (1 - 0.3 s)
   of the volume, (1 - 0.1 s) of the pitch and (1 - 0.05 s) of the rate, s being
   cl_h9_tts_muffle.

 3b. CHOOSING THE VOICES
 -----------------------

   There are four kinds of speaker, from the model's name (section 3): a man, a woman, a Mech and a bot; and a mask on top of a man or a
   woman (3a). The game picks a voice for each from the ones installed on the machine, and a player can pick his own:

       exec ttsactors.cfg          lists the voices on this machine, numbered, and says how to pick and try them
       cl_h9_tts_voices            the same list (Windows: SAPI's voices, e.g. David and Zira; the web client: the browser's)
       cl_h9_tts_voice_man N       a voice's NUMBER for a man, a woman, a Mech and a bot; 0 (the default) leaves it to the game,
       cl_h9_tts_voice_woman N     which is the best Male voice for a man and the best Female voice for a woman; a Mech and a
       cl_h9_tts_voice_mech N      bot with no pick of their own use the man's
       cl_h9_tts_voice_bot N
       cl_h9_tts_test man Hello    speaks the words as man, woman, mech or bot, with the picks in force; add the word muffled
                                   after the kind to hear the mask: cl_h9_tts_test woman muffled Over here
       cl_h9_tts_robot 1           the robot sound of a Mech and a bot (0 turns it off)

   The robot sound is a ring modulator over the rendered voice (a 38 Hz hum for a Mech, 62 Hz for a bot, 65 percent of the line), on
   top of the lower slower Mech and higher faster bot of section 3. The web client cannot do it (the browser gives no audio to
   filter): there a Mech and a bot differ only in pitch and speed. A pick is a number in the machine's own list, so it means a
   different voice on another machine; the picks are saved with the player's config.

   How what you hear is set (all saved, all on your own machine):

       cl_h9_tts_volume 1          how loud (0 to 2)
       cl_h9_tts_muffle 1          how strongly a gas mask muffles (0 off, 2 heavy; 3a)
       cl_h9_tts_pan 1             1 puts each voice in the ear it comes from (Windows, stereo); 0 plays them in the middle
       cl_h9_tts_maxvoices 4       how many voices speak at once (1 to 8). A line that comes when that many are playing waits for
                                   a voice (section 5) and no line that is playing is cut off. The value is saved with the
                                   player's config, so a player keeps what he has whatever the default is
       cl_h9_tts_individual 1      a speaker who asked for no voice of his own gets one from his slot: the n-th voice of his
                                   kind and a small step off its pitch, so a room of players does not all sound alike (0: all alike)

 3c. HOW YOU SOUND TO OTHERS
 ---------------------------

       cl_h9_tts_myvoice N         0 (default) leaves your voice to each listener's game (and cl_h9_tts_individual); 1 to 99 asks
                                   for the n-th voice of your kind (man or woman) on THEIR machine, wrapping round when it has fewer
       cl_h9_tts_mydepth N         -10 deeper to 10 higher (each step is 2.5 percent of the speed and pitch)
       cl_h9_tts_test man Hello    speaks as you sound to others: with your myvoice and mydepth, in the middle

   The first time a game runs with this client and its player has never chosen (cl_h9_tts_voice_set 0), it gives him a variant (1 to 99) and, when he has set no
   depth, a step of depth (-2 to 2) at random, writes them to those two cvars (which are saved with his config) and sets cl_h9_tts_voice_set to 1. So the players
   of a server do not all sound alike, and each keeps the voice he was given on every server and in every listener's game. A pick he made is kept as it is, and
   a 0 he sets afterwards stays 0 (each listener's game then chooses for him, as cl_h9_tts_individual says). cl_h9_tts_voice_set 0 makes the game give him
   another at its next start.

   The game sends the two to the server as the client command h9_ttsvoice <variant> <depth> (not through the userinfo,
   which is full): when the server asks as you join (the plugin sends H9Tts with slot 0 and flag bit 1, so a server without
   the plugin is never sent a command it does not have), and again whenever you change either cvar. The server puts them
   in each line it sends about you. h9_ttsvoice with no argument prints what the server holds for you. A listener's own
   pick (3b) still decides which voice his machine uses; your depth applies either way. The voices are the listener's
   machine's, so "voice 3" is a different voice on each; it is the same relative voice, not the same sound.

 3d. BETTER, MORE REALISTIC VOICES
 ---------------------------------

   The game speaks with the voices the machine has; the quality is theirs, and it is the machine that decides. The default
   already prefers a Natural or Neural voice over a plain one, so installing one is all it takes: cl_h9_tts_voices lists
   them, and nothing needs picking.

     Windows   the SAPI voices that come with it (Microsoft David and Zira "Desktop") are the robotic ones. The newer
               Natural voices (Narrator's, Microsoft Edge's) are not SAPI voices, and a SAPI program does not see them. A
               third-party open-source adapter, NaturalVoiceSAPIAdapter (github.com/gexgd0419/NaturalVoiceSAPIAdapter),
               makes them appear as SAPI 5 voices; it is not H9's, not shipped and not reviewed here -- read its source and
               license before installing it. Other SAPI 5 voices (commercial or free) work the same way.
     browser   Edge offers "Microsoft ... Online (Natural)" voices to a page, Chrome the Google ones; the web client
               prefers a Natural or Neural name, then a local voice, in the page's language.
     Kokoro    a neural voice H9 ships itself, run by a helper process, that each player turns on with cl_h9_tts_engine
               (section 3e). It is not a SAPI voice and is not picked from cl_h9_tts_voices.

 3e. THE KOKORO VOICES (Windows, optional, off by default)
 ---------------------------------------------------------

   Kokoro is a neural text to speech model (82 million parameters, Apache 2.0,
   made by hexgrad) that sounds a good deal more like a person than SAPI's
   Desktop voices. H9 ships it with the game in a folder of its own, ts\h9tts
   (135 MB, of which 20 MB are a pack of ready-made lines), and a player who
   wants it turns it on for himself. The model runs in a helper program,
   h9kokoro64.exe (64-bit; client.dll is 32-bit and never loads the model),
   which client.dll starts, talks to over a private pipe, watches and stops
   itself. A player who leaves cl_h9_tts_engine at system gets exactly what
   he had, and nothing starts on a server that does not use text to speech.

       cl_h9_tts_engine system|auto|kokoro   which voices speak (saved)
           system   the default: the voices of section 3, as before; the
                    helper is never started
           auto     Kokoro when it can start the line in time; else the
                    system voice of section 3, at once
           kokoro   Kokoro only: a line it cannot start in time is not spoken
       cl_h9_tts_threads N    the processor threads the helper makes a line
                              with: 0 (default) lets the game choose, as many
                              as the machine has logical processors and 8 at
                              the most (8 on a machine with 8 or more), and
                              none on a machine with fewer than 4 (engine auto
                              then speaks with the system voices; engine
                              kokoro uses 2); 1 to 16 sets it. A new value
                              restarts the helper
       cl_h9_tts_state        prints which voices speak, the state of the
                              helper, the counters and the last line's times
       cl_h9_tts_reload       stops the helper and starts it again and forgets
                              its failures (a helper that gave up, a helper
                              that was replaced)

   A new install starts from the stock config (ts\config.stock.cfg), which
   sets cl_h9_tts_engine auto and cl_h9_tts_threads 8: a new player has the
   Kokoro voices, with 8 threads, without typing anything. A player who
   already has a config.cfg keeps what he has (the stock file is read only
   when there is none). The web client has no Kokoro and ignores both lines.
   On a machine with fewer than 8 logical processors cl_h9_tts_threads 0
   lets the game choose (the machine's count, and none below 4 under auto).

   The helper starts when the server asks the game for its voice picks (a
   server with h9_tts.amxx does, as a player joins), so a player on any other
   server never has it running. It ends with the game. After 20 minutes
   without a line it is let go of (its model takes about 270 MB) and the next
   line starts it again.

   WHAT A LINE DOES

   1. Who speaks. The kinds of section 3 and one more: a model with droid in
      its name is a droid (the server sends it in the tail of the message,
      section 4). The system voices speak a droid as they speak a bot; Kokoro
      has a sound of its own for it. Kokoro has 12 men, 12 women, 3 Mechs,
      3 bots and 3 droids (ts\h9tts\voices.txt): each is a blend of Kokoro's
      voices at a speed, with a depth of its own, and a Mech, a bot or a droid
      has a sound chain (ts\h9tts\chains.txt) on top.
   2. Which one, and how it is shaped. The same cvars as for the system
      voices decide, and reach the Kokoro path like this:
        cl_h9_tts_myvoice N    the speaker's pick (3c, sent by the server in
                               each line about him) is variant ((N - 1) mod
                               the kind's count) + 1 of his kind
        cl_h9_tts_individual   a speaker who asked for none gets a variant
                               and a step of depth from his slot
        cl_h9_tts_mydepth      with the slot's step and the variant's own
                               depth: one sum, limited to -12 to 12, one
                               resample (2.5 percent of pitch and tempo a
                               step), together with the kind's own pitch and
                               tempo
        cl_h9_tts_muffle       a man's or a woman's gas mask is the variant's
                               mask chain at that strength (0 off, 2 heavy)
        cl_h9_tts_robot        a Mech's, bot's or droid's chain; at 0 only its
                               pitch and tempo
        cl_h9_tts_volume, _pan, _maxvoices   in the mixer, after the voice,
                               for every engine
      The listener's own voice picks (cl_h9_tts_voice_man, _woman, _mech,
      _bot) are places in the machine's system voices and do not apply to
      Kokoro: its variants are picked by the speaker and by the slot rule.
      A line is shaped the same whatever made it, so a line of the pack and
      a line the helper made sound alike. (The shaping is tested bit for bit
      against a reference: every variant of every kind, with and without a
      mask or the machine sound, at three depths.)
   3. Where the sound comes from. A line the line pack holds (lines\lines.h9pk:
      the bots' fixed chat, in every variant a bot can get) is played at once,
      without the helper: 1854 clips, 20 MB. Any other line is made by the
      helper phrase by phrase and each phrase is played as soon as it is made,
      so the first words sound after the first phrase (about 1.6 s of speech,
      cut at a pause of the speech when one is near), not after the whole
      line; the later phrases are about as long, so that the next is ready as
      the one before it ends. A line the helper made before comes back from
      its cache (24 MB) in about 15 ms.
   4. The start rule. A line must START to sound within 3 seconds of its
      arrival in the client (kStartBudgetMs, h9_tts_kokoro.h); the time it
      waited for the speech thread counts. When the helper cannot do it:
        * its first piece is not there in the time that is left (less 0.25 s
          for the system voice's own render): the line is cancelled at the
          helper (which stays up: slow is not dead), whatever it still sends
          for the line is thrown away, and the system voice speaks the line
          at once (engine auto; engine kokoro drops it). With less than 1.2 s
          left the helper is not asked at all
        * the helper has worked more than half of the last 10 seconds: it
          declines the line (DONE busy) and the system voice speaks it at once.
          A line that has been started is never paused between its phrases
        * the helper was late with three lines in a row: it is too slow for
          this machine just now, and it is not asked for a minute
          (cl_h9_tts_state says it is resting), so that a slow machine hears
          the system voice at once and not 3 s late. cl_h9_tts_reload ends
          the rest
        * the helper is not ready (loading its model, 2 to 3.5 seconds after
          the server's hello; restarting after a failure): the system voice
        * a line waits for the speech thread, which makes one line at a time:
          while the helper makes a line, the speech thread looks at the
          lines that wait behind it every 10 ms. A line of the pack is played
          at once, and a line that has waited 2.3 seconds goes to the system
          voice (engine auto) without waiting for the helper to be done. Not
          the speaker's own next line: a speaker's lines follow one another
      The system voice itself needs 0.1 to 0.3 seconds for a line (Desktop
      voices; the first line of a voice more), and the lines are rendered one
      after the other, so in a crowd (the queue holds 8) the last line of a
      burst can start later than 3 seconds.

   5. When the helper is not there. Nothing here stops the game or the
      speech: the line is the system voice's (engine auto) or dropped (engine
      kokoro), and cl_h9_tts_state says why.

       no ts\h9tts folder, or a voices.txt that does not parse   system voice
       no helper files (bin\h9kokoro64.exe, manifest.txt)         the pack's
                              lines only; any other line is the system voice's
       a file of the helper changed (its SHA-256 is not the one in
       manifest.txt)          the helper is not started; the state names the file
       the helper is loading its model, or restarting              system voice
       the helper dies, is late after its first piece, or sends what it
       should not             the line ends where it was (its voice is closed,
                              not left waiting), the helper is killed and
                              started again after 4 s, then 8, 16 ... 120 s;
                              after 5 failures in a row it stays off until
                              cl_h9_tts_reload. (A late FIRST piece is not a
                              failure: see the start rule)
       a machine with fewer than 4 logical processors   the helper is not
                              started by engine auto (the pack still plays);
                              engine kokoro starts it with 2 threads
       the web client and every other platform   the browser's voice, or none;
                              nothing of this exists there

   THE HELPER'S RULES (the client never trusts it)

       * it is a program the client runs, so before it starts the client
         checks the SHA-256 of bin\h9kokoro64.exe and bin\onnxruntime.dll (and
         the providers DLL when manifest.txt lists it); the manifest has to be
         made again with every helper build (tools\h9tts\stage_h9tts.py)
       * the pipe is made by the client, named for this run, open to the
         owner and the system only, no remote client; the helper only connects
       * it runs hidden, below normal priority, no CPU pinning, in a job object
         that ends it with the game (kill on close), with a memory limit of
         1.5 GB; ONNX Runtime does not spin its threads while idle, uses the
         given number of threads inside one call and one call at a time
       * every frame it sends is checked: magic, type, size, the line it
         belongs to, the piece's number, the sample rate; a wrong one ends the
         conversation. Deadlines: the first piece within the start budget (a
         miss cancels the line, it does not kill the helper), each next piece
         and the end within 9 s, the connection within 30 s (the helper loads
         its model before it connects: 92 MB from the disk), READY within
         45 s. A helper that misses a later deadline, or does not answer a
         cancel for 3 s, is killed. A start that fails (a first start under an
         antivirus scan, a machine that is busy) is tried again after 4 s,
         and the state says why, with the helper's exit code
       * it makes no new line while it has worked more than half of the last
         10 s (DONE busy); queues at most 3 lines, the oldest pushed out
       * the game's frame time is watched: while the helper makes a line the
         frames are compared with those before it, and when three of the last
         five lines made them more than 10 percent longer the helper is
         started again with one thread fewer (never fewer than 2); the cap and
         the decisions are in cl_h9_tts_state, cl_h9_tts_reload forgets it
       * it ends when the pipe closes, when the game's process is gone (it
         watches it), and on SHUTDOWN

     * A line of the pack starts 2 to 16 ms after it arrives, even while the
       helper is making another line; a line the helper made before comes
       back from its cache 11 to 16 ms after it arrives.
     * The helper loads its model 2 to 3.4 s after it is started (92 MB from
       the disk; a cold disk takes longer). A line that comes before it is
       ready is the system voice's.
     * Speech that stands still: a line made in several phrases stops for a
       moment between two of them when the next is not ready as the one before
       it ends. For lines of 17 to 21 words the silence in a line was, on
       average, 0.00 s at 8 threads, 0.03 s at 6, 0.39 s at 4 (1.26 s at the
       most) and 1.63 s at 2; at 4 threads with the game-like load beside it
       0.86 s. (The first plan of the phrases, a short first phrase and all the
       rest in one, stood still for 1.9 s on average at 4 threads.)
     * CPU: while it makes a line the helper keeps 3.0 of the 16 logical
       processors busy at 4 threads and 4.7 at 8 (5.4 at the most), for about
       as long as the line lasts; between lines it does nothing. It runs below
       normal priority.
     * Memory and disk: about 270 MB of memory while it runs (24 MB more for
       its line cache at most); 135 MB on disk.
     * The game's frame time: next to the game-like load the frames of the
       game-like thread are the same while the helper makes lines as while it
       is idle. At 8 threads: mean 17.96 ms idle and 17.94 ms while it makes
       lines, 0.1 percent of the frames over 20 ms in both; at 4 threads the
       same (17.87 ms in both, 0.2 and 0.3 percent).
     * A busy chat (the whole speech layer, the real helper, 8 threads, a
       volume nobody can hear): six speakers type six lines of ten words, one
       a second: the first sounds come 0.6 to 2.6 s after the lines, three of
       the six made by Kokoro and three given to the system voice. A long line
       and four short ones within 2.5 s: the long line 1.0 s, a line of the
       pack 16 ms, the short lines 1.5 to 2.5 s (two by the system voice, one
       by Kokoro).

   WHAT IT DOES NOT DO

       * It speaks English. Words the lexicon does not have are guessed by
         letter-to-sound trees (about half of them right); numbers, symbols and
         emoticons are read by rules. Other languages are read as English
       * Each call of the model begins and ends with about 0.8 s of near
         silence of the model's own. It is trimmed, but the time to make it is
         spent: a short line takes at least about 0.7 s on this machine at
         8 threads (0.9 s at 4), and a line made in pieces can stand still
         for a moment between two of them when the helper has fewer threads
         or the machine is busy
       * The web client keeps the browser's voice, and so does every other
         platform: the helper is a Windows program. A native Linux build of it
         is not made
       * Lines are made one at a time. A busy chat is partly spoken by the
         system voice (see the start rule), so two speakers in a quick
         exchange can sound like two different engines

 4. THE PROTOCOL
 ---------------

   The plugin registers the user message H9Tts in plugin_precache, whatever
   h9_texttospeech says, with engfunc(EngFunc_RegUserMsg, "H9Tts", -1), the
   way h9vgui_precache registers H9Vgui (include\h9_vgui.inc): every game
   learns it at connect. (The size is -1, variable.) A message goes to one
   listener, begun with engfunc(EngFunc_MessageBegin, MSG_ONE, ...) because
   Metamod-R records only the game library's messages, and RELIABLE since
   2026-11-09: on a big map the engine drops an unreliable message together
   with the datagram that cannot hold it ("Datagram overflowed" in
   ts\logs\h9_overflow.log), and on the roleplay maps that was every line. The
   reliable channel is spared by sending a listener up to 4 lines at once and
   then one every 0.3 s at most, from all speakers together.

       byte     the speaker's slot (1 to 128)
       byte     the voice: 0 man, 1 woman, 2 Mech, 3 bot
       byte     the volume, 0 to 100
       byte     flags: bit 0 (1), a gas mask muffles the voice; bit 2 (4), the line is global (section 2):
                volume 100, pan 0, heard from no direction; bit 1 (2) is the hello message's; the rest 0
       string   the words, at most h9_tts_maxchars (150 at the most), NUL ended
       byte     the variant the speaker asked others to hear him with (0 none, 1 to 99)    } the tail: a client reads
       char     the depth he asked for, -10 to 10                                          } it only when the message
       char     the pan: -100 his voice in the listener's left ear, 100 right, 0 the       } is long enough for it, so
                middle; from the speaker's direction and the listener's yaw, fading to     } an older client takes the
                0 within 160 units                                                         } message as it was
       byte     1 when the bot voice is a droid's (a model with droid in its name),        } one more byte after the pan,
                else 0; only the Kokoro voices (3e) tell a droid from a bot                } read the same way

   About 100 bytes at the default, 159 at the most; the client takes no more
   than 192. A message with slot 0, voice 0, volume 0, flags 2 and no words is
   the server asking this game for its h9_ttsvoice (3c); it is sent once, a few
   seconds after a player joins. A client that does not know flag bit 2 plays a
   global line as what it is, a full-volume line from the middle.

   A game says it can speak with the userinfo key _h9tts, as it says it draws
   H9 VGUI with _h9vgui: "1" while cl_h9_tts is not 0 and the machine has a
   voice (Windows: SAPI could make one; web: speechSynthesis exists), else
   "0". A game without the key (a Steam client, an older H9 client) is sent
   nothing. Were a message to reach a game that does not know it anyway, the
   engine drops a user message nobody hooked, harmlessly.

 5. THE CLIENT (reTS cl_dll\h9_tts.cpp, h9_tts_mix.h, h9_tts_win.cpp, h9_tts_web.cpp)
 ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

   Client cvars, all archived: cl_h9_tts (1; 0 is silence for this player,
   and the game tells servers by _h9tts 0) and cl_h9_tts_volume (1; 0 to 2;
   the distance volume is multiplied by it); the voice picks, cl_h9_tts_voice_man,
   _woman, _mech and _bot, cl_h9_tts_robot, _muffle, _pan, _maxvoices and
   _individual (section 3b), cl_h9_tts_myvoice and _mydepth (3c),
   cl_h9_tts_engine and cl_h9_tts_threads (3e); the commands cl_h9_tts_voices,
   cl_h9_tts_test, cl_h9_tts_state and cl_h9_tts_reload.

   A server is untrusted. Whatever a message says, the client's own limits
   hold: a line is cleaned the same way and cut to 160 bytes; at most 8 lines
   a second from everyone together are read, and no speaker's lines come
   closer than 0.3 s; whom a line is sent to is the server's say (the speaker
   too while h9_tts_self is 1). A speaker has
   ONE voice: his newer line waits until his older one has finished, and
   replaces a line of his that still waits; the one being spoken is never cut
   off for it. cl_h9_tts 0 silences what is playing and what waits at once.

   Windows. Two threads of its own, so the game's own thread only ever queues
   a line and never waits for a voice or the sound card.
   The speech thread (its own COM apartment) takes the queued lines one at a
   time, 8 at most waiting, a speaker's older one replaced and, when it is
   full, the quietest dropped: SAPI renders the line to memory (22050 Hz, 16
   bit, mono), as PLAIN TEXT (SPF_IS_NOT_XML); a Mech, a bot and the speaker's
   depth are resampled; a gas mask runs the filter of 3a over it; the line is
   handed to the mixer. SAPI is called from this thread only (an ISpVoice is
   never shared between threads), and a line is cut to 30 seconds.
   The audio thread owns the sound card: ONE waveOut device, opened when there
   is something to say and let go of after 2.5 s of silence, fed in blocks of
   40 ms (4 queued) from the mixer (h9_tts_mix.h), which sums every voice that
   is playing: the volume is multiplied into the samples, with 4 dB of
   headroom at a volume of 1, a constant-power pan puts each voice in the ear
   it comes from, and the sum goes through a soft limiter (untouched below 80
   percent of full scale, never past it), so several voices do not clip.
   The mixer's rules:
     * voices of different speakers play TOGETHER, up to cl_h9_tts_maxvoices
       (1 to 8, 4 by default). No line that is playing is ever cut off to make
       room for another.
     * a line that comes when that many are playing WAITS for a voice. At most
       4 lines wait, none longer than 8 seconds after it arrived (it is dropped
       then, being too late to mean anything); when the list is full the
       quietest line goes, the oldest of equals, before it was ever heard.
     * a speaker's lines follow one another: a line of his that comes while he
       is speaking waits for his voice to end, and replaces a line of his that
       still waits. This is the choice because a person does not say two
       sentences at once, and because cutting his first sentence short loses
       what he said, while the server's h9_tts_gap already keeps one speaker
       from piling lines up.
     * a voice fades in and out over 5 ms, so a line starts and ends without a
       click; cl_h9_tts 0 fades everything out over 30 ms.
   client.dll links ole32 and winmm and NOT sapi.lib: SAPI
   is reached through COM at run time, so a machine without it (Wine) still loads
   client.dll and says _h9tts 0. SAPI renders one line after another. The Desktop
   voices render far faster than they speak (Microsoft David and Zira: 4.8 seconds of
   speech in 31 to 203 ms, the first line of a voice being the slow one), so two
   simultaneous lines start together. A voice that renders at about its speaking speed
   (some Natural voices) delays the second line by the time the first took to render,
   and the two overlap only while the first is still playing then.

   Kokoro (3e). With cl_h9_tts_engine auto or kokoro the speech thread asks
   the Kokoro layer first: the line pack, then the helper, a second process.
   While the thread waits for the helper's pieces (it looks every 10 ms) it
   serves the lines that queue behind the one being made: a line of the pack
   at once, and, with engine auto, a line that has waited 2.3 s by the system
   voice. A line made in pieces goes to the mixer as an open voice: the first
   piece starts it, the others are appended as they come, a late piece leaves
   it silent for a moment, the last piece closes it, and a line that ends early
   (the helper died) is closed so that its speaker's voice is not left waiting.

   Web client. The same source, compiled for the browser, calls the page's
   speechSynthesis through the one script function installed with
   emscripten_run_script (EM_JS is not resolved in a side module). THE BROWSER
   CANNOT OVERLAP VOICES: a page's speechSynthesis speaks one utterance at a time and
   gives the page no audio to mix, so in the web client two speakers are heard one
   after the other, never together (cl_h9_tts_maxvoices is not read there). The
   game does not cut anyone off for it: lines wait their turn in a queue of 4, none
   longer than 8 seconds after it arrived, the quietest line (the oldest of equals)
   going when it is full; a speaker's newer line replaces his older one that still
   waits, and follows the one being spoken. Nothing is cancelled except by
   cl_h9_tts 0, or a line that has been going for 20 seconds without the browser
   saying it ended. The page keeps a reference to the utterance being spoken, which
   some browsers need so that they do not discard it before its end. No page file
   changes: the code runs in the page that hosts the game, and a browser without
   speechSynthesis makes the game say _h9tts 0.

   Any other platform: the message is hooked and ignored, and _h9tts is 0.

 6. LIMITS, ALL TOGETHER
 -----------------------

     a line         96 characters by default, 150 at the most (server);
                    160 bytes (client)
     a speaker      one spoken line every 1.5 s (server, h9_tts_gap); 0.3 s
                    (client)
     a listener     4 lines at once, then one every 0.3 s, from all speakers
                    together (server)
     the client     8 lines a second in all; cl_h9_tts_maxvoices voices at
                    once on Windows (4 by default, 8 at the most), one at a
                    time in the browser; on Windows 8 lines waiting for the
                    speech thread and 4 for a voice, in the browser 4 waiting;
                    a line that waited more than 8 s is not spoken
     a message      about 100 bytes, reliable, to listeners within range only
                    (every listener with h9_tts_global 1)
     kokoro         a line must start to sound within 3 s of its arrival, else
                    the system voice speaks it (engine kokoro: nothing); one
                    line is made at a time, 3 wait at the helper; no new line
                    while the helper has worked more than half of the last
                    10 s; the helper is let go of after 20 minutes without a
                    line; 192 bytes of a line go to the helper

 7. PRIVACY AND ABUSE
 --------------------

   * What leaves a machine: the text the chat already shows. Nothing is
     recorded, nothing is sent to H9: the Windows voices run on the player's
     own machine. In a browser, a voice that is not local (some browsers'
     "Google" voices) sends the text to its vendor's speech service, as any
     page that uses speechSynthesis does; the web client prefers a local
     voice among those of the same language. A player who minds types cl_h9_tts 0.
   * Spam and shouting: one line per h9_tts_gap seconds and h9_tts_maxchars
     characters, then the client's own limits. Commands are never spoken.
   * Injection: the text is spoken as PLAIN text on Windows (SPF_IS_NOT_XML),
     so nothing typed can be taken for SAPI markup -- no <audio src=...> that
     fetches a file or an address, no <voice>, <pitch> or <rate>. A server
     cannot make a client open a file or an address by this message; the
     most it can do is make the client speak short lines of its choosing, in
     the four voices, at the client's own rate limits.
   * What a server can do about a player who abuses it: mute his account
     (h9web; his chat is not spoken or written), gag or kick him with the
     tools it has; take away what he can reach with h9_tts_range (smaller),
     h9_tts_global 0, h9_tts_gap (longer) and h9_tts_maxchars (shorter); or turn the whole thing
     off with h9_texttospeech 0, which takes effect with the next line. A
     player can always turn it off for himself with cl_h9_tts 0 (and then no
     message is sent to him), or lower cl_h9_tts_volume.
   * Not done: no filter of words, no translation. Voices read what they are
     given in their own language, and badly in another.

 8. TESTING IT LIVE (nothing in this list has been run)
 ------------------------------------------------------

   Set up: a server with h9_tts.amxx loaded and h9_texttospeech 1; two H9
   clients with the new client.dll, A (the speaker) and B (the listener),
   side by side; B types setinfo, with no argument, and sees _h9tts 1.
   Native Windows with at least one SAPI voice (Windows has Microsoft David and
   Microsoft Zira), then the web client in Chrome and in Firefox.

   1. A man. A wears seal (or any model without a word of the tables), says
      "hello there": B hears David within about a second, and the chat line
      is written as ever. The very first line after the game starts is slower
      (SAPI loads the voice).
   2. A woman. A wears prole_fem: B hears Zira.
   3. A Mech and a bot. A wears mech_msr_bot: slow and low. A wears
      msrdroid_bot: fast and high. A real bot (an rcbot fake client) says
      something wearing a human model: it is a man's or a woman's, by its
      model, not a bot's.
   4. Gas masks. A wears repuheavy_pack: a muffled man. repuheavy_fem_pack: a
      muffled woman. XCCPsgts (insurgent), seal and msrdroid_bot and
      mech_msr_bot: clear. Add msrdroid_bot and mech_msr_bot to the list file,
      change the map: still clear. Take repuheavy_pack out, change the map:
      clear. Do all of this once with the match mode off (tsm_enabled 0) on a
      plain map: the same.
   5. Distance. A speaks with B at 100 units (full volume), 500 (about half),
      790 (a whisper), 900 (silence); then with a wall between them at 300

   6. The rate limit. A types two lines within a second: only the first is
      spoken, both are written. Set h9_tts_gap 0.2 and type two lines 0.5 s
      apart: the second waits for the first to end and then speaks. Three
      speakers at once: three voices together (cl_h9_tts_maxvoices 4); a fifth
      waits for a free voice and nobody is cut off.
   7. B's cl_h9_tts 0: silence, and _h9tts reads 0; back to 1 and _h9tts reads
      1. cl_h9_tts_volume 0.3 and 2: quieter, louder.
   8. h9_texttospeech 0 and 1: nothing at 0, speech at 1, from one line to
      the next.
   9. say_team: B on A's team hears; C beside A on the other team does not.
  10. Not spoken: ".ready" and "/me waves"; a line of ":)"; A dead; A muted
      (h9web); a player under the Recon's cloak.
  11. A client without the new code (a Steam client, or the previous
      client.dll): it joins and plays as ever, and nothing is sent to it. To
      see the message reach such a client anyway, give it setinfo _h9tts 1:
      it takes the message, ignores it, and prints nothing.
  12. Wine, or a Windows with no voices, if one is to hand: the game loads and
      says _h9tts 0.
  13. The web client: the first speech may need a click on the page first
      (browsers do not speak before the page has been used); a Mech and a bot
      are lower and slower, higher and faster; a muffled voice is quieter and
      slightly lower; one voice at a time (the browser cannot overlap), the
      lines of two speakers one after the other; a new line from the same
      speaker waits for his old one and cuts nothing off.
  14. A crowd: ten bots chatting. No frame hitch; at most cl_h9_tts_maxvoices
      voices at once, the rest waiting at most 8 s and then not spoken; no line
      cut off mid-word; the server log shows nothing from [TTS] but its start
      lines.
  15. The gas mask. A wears repuheavy_fem (it was not on the list before): A
      types h9_tts at the console and reads that the model is masked; B
      hears a dull boxy woman; cl_h9_tts_muffle 0, 1, 2 on B: clear, muffled,
      heavier. Take repuheavy* out of the list, change the map: clear.
  16. The ears. A and B stand 300 units apart, B facing A: A's voice is in
      the middle; B turns 90 degrees left: A's voice is in B's right ear, and
      the other way round. cl_h9_tts_pan 0: always in the middle. A speaker
      round a corner (no line of sight) is in the right ear still.
  17. The voice others hear. A sets cl_h9_tts_myvoice 2 and cl_h9_tts_mydepth
      -6, B (with two male voices installed) hears A lower and, with the
      second voice, another one than A's first. A types h9_ttsvoice at the
      console and reads variant 2, depth -6. A changes the map: the picks are
      sent again within a few seconds. B's own cl_h9_tts_voice_man N overrides

  18. A server without the plugin: the client never sends h9_ttsvoice, and no

  19. A native Linux client or a Wine client with no SAPI voice: the game

  20. Global. h9_tts_global 1 (rcon or the cfg; being FCVAR_SERVER, the engine

      voice is free and then speaks whole; type cl_h9_tts_maxvoices 2 and
      the third waits instead. One speaker typing two lines 2 s apart: the
      second begins when the first has ended. cl_h9_tts 0 while lines play:
      they fade out and nothing waiting is spoken afterwards.
  22. Kokoro off. With cl_h9_tts_engine system (the default) play a whole
      session with chat on a server that has the plugin: no h9kokoro64.exe is
      ever in the task manager, the voices are SAPI's as before, and
      cl_h9_tts_state says the engine is system. (Tests 22 to 30 are the ones
      only a game can show: the numbers, the start rule, the fallbacks and the
      helper's safety of 3e were run outside the game, with the real helper,
      the client's own code and the speech layer at a volume nobody can hear;
      no voice has been heard in a game.)
  23. Data and start. cl_h9_tts_engine auto, join a server with h9_tts.amxx:
      cl_h9_tts_state says "kokoro: data ok, 33 voice variants, 5 chains,
      1854 lines in the pack" and, within 2 to 4 s of the join, "kokoro helper:
      ready (ready), 8 thread(s) ..." (8 on a machine with 8 or more logical
      processors; h9kokoro64.exe is in the task manager, below normal
      priority, about 270 MB). A wrong result: "not usable (no
      h9tts folder ...)", "failed (... does not match h9tts\manifest.txt)" (a
      file was changed after the manifest was made) or "did not connect".
  24. The pack. A bot says one of its fixed lines (bot_chat.ini): it is
      heard at once, within a fifth of a second of the chat line, in a
      neural voice; cl_h9_tts_state counts it as "from the pack". Say the same
      with engine system for the difference.
  25. The helper. Type a line of 8 to 11 words that is not a bot's: the first
      words sound within about 1 s (1.4 s with 4 threads, 2 s with 2; never
      more than 3), the rest follows with no pause worth the name on 8
      threads (0.4 s on average at 4); type the same line again:
      it sounds within 0.1 s (the helper's cache). A wrong result: nothing for
      3 s and then the system voice (cl_h9_tts_state: "came late"), a line
      spoken twice, a clipped start, or a long silence in the middle of a line.
  26. The start rule. Two players type long lines one right after the other:
      the first is Kokoro, the second is the system voice about 2.5 s after it
      was typed (not 5 s or more). A bot's fixed line during a long line plays
      at once. Look at the speakers: a speaker's own two lines in a row
      follow one another whatever made them.
  27. The cost. While lines are made, the game's frame rate does not change
      noticeably (cl_showfps, or the scoreboard's fps), and cl_h9_tts_state's
      frame watch says "lowered 0 time(s)"; on a slow machine it may lower the
      thread cap ("thread cap 3"), which is shown there too. Task manager:
      h9kokoro64.exe uses up to cl_h9_tts_threads processors for a few seconds
      a line and nothing between lines.
  28. Failure and end. With a line playing, end h9kokoro64.exe in the task
      manager: the line ends where it was (no hang, no click), the next line is
      the system voice's, the helper is back after about 4 s (cl_h9_tts_state:
      "started 2 time(s)"). Close the game: the helper is gone within a second.
      Delete the folder ts\h9tts (or take bin\h9kokoro64.exe out): the game
      speaks with the system voice as it did, and cl_h9_tts_state says why.
  29. The voices. cl_h9_tts_test man|woman|mech|bot|droid Hello there
      sounds like five different speakers (a droid is not a bot); with
      cl_h9_tts_myvoice 1 to 12 on a man, 12 different men; cl_h9_tts_mydepth
      -10 and 10: deeper and higher; a gas-mask model: cl_h9_tts_muffle 0, 1
      and 2 clear, muffled, heavy; cl_h9_tts_robot 0 and 1 for a Mech. The
      listener's cl_h9_tts_voice_man and the others change nothing while the
      engine is kokoro.
  30. Threads. cl_h9_tts_threads 2, then 6, then 0: the helper starts again
      each time (cl_h9_tts_state: "started N time(s)" and the thread count),
      and the time to the first sound changes as the table in 3e says.
