===============================================================================
 TEXT TO SPEECH: WHAT PLAYERS SAY IN CHAT, SPOKEN TO THE PLAYERS NEAR THEM
===============================================================================

 Who this is for: whoever runs an H9 game server, whoever plays on one, and
 whoever builds the client or the web page.

 The short version
   * What it is: when a player types in chat (say or say_team), the players
     near him HEAR it, at once, in a voice for his model: a man's, a woman's,
     a Mech's or a bot's, quieter the farther away he is, and muffled when his
     model wears a gas mask. It is not voice chat: no sound travels on the
     network, only the text the chat already shows. Each listener's own
     machine speaks it, with the voices installed on that machine.
   * It is off. A server turns it on with h9_texttospeech 1 (default 0).
   * It needs H9's client.dll or the H9 web client (Windows SAPI on a native
     game, the browser's speech synthesis in the web client). A game without
     it is sent nothing and notices nothing. A player turns it off for
     himself with cl_h9_tts 0.
   * The server is h9_tts.amxx; its settings are configs\h9_tts.cfg and the
     list of gas-masked models configs\h9_tts_gasmask.cfg. COMMANDS.txt has the
     cvars (TEXT TO SPEECH); this file is the rest.

 1. TURNING IT ON (a server)
 ---------------------------

   * h9_tts.amxx goes in plugins.ini AFTER every plugin that can stop a chat
     line (a gag, a mute) and after h9_presence, and BEFORE ARP_Core: AMX
     Mod X runs the say handlers in plugins.ini order, and a handler that
     returns PLUGIN_HANDLED keeps the ones after it from running.
     ARP_TalkArea (Apollo RP) answers every chat line itself and returns
     PLUGIN_HANDLED, so on a roleplay map (h9_tsrp_*) a say handler listed
     after it is never called and nothing is spoken. h9_tts also asks
     h9_presence itself whether the speaker's account is muted (h9p_muted),
     when h9_presence is loaded.
   * build.bat: add h9_tts to TARGETS, and to the presets' plugins.*.ini
     (H9XASH\presets) so the plugin is in every preset.
   * h9_tts.cfg (the plugin executes it on every map) holds:

       h9_texttospeech   0     1: chat is spoken. 0: nothing is sent
       h9_tts_range      800   units from the speaker out to which a line is
                               heard
       h9_tts_maxchars   96    the most characters spoken of a line (8 to 150)
       h9_tts_gap        1.5   seconds a speaker waits between two spoken
                               lines
       h9_tts_self       1     1: the speaker hears his own line too, so one
                               player alone can tell it works. 0: only the
                               others hear it

     The first line is commented out in the shipped file; remove the // or put
     the cvar in server.cfg. A change of h9_texttospeech takes effect with
     the next line anyone says, no restart.

     From a player's console, h9_texttospeech (or h9_tts) prints the state: the
     server's switch, whether this game can speak (_h9tts) and whether he hears
     his own lines. h9_texttospeech 1 or 0 (an admin with the cvar flag) switches
     it. The cvar lives in the server, so a game that is not the server's own
     does not see it; the plugin answers the same name as a command.

 2. WHO HEARS WHAT
 -----------------

   The speaker: a living player, not muted (h9_presence), drawn (not hidden
   with EF_NODRAW and not fully transparent: an eliminated player, a
   spectator and a player waiting out a freeze are not), whose line is not a
   command (it does not start with . / ! or @) and has a letter, a digit or a
   non-ASCII character in it. Bots speak too: a bot's chat is spoken like
   anyone's. His line is cleaned (control characters become spaces, a run of
   spaces one, the ends are trimmed) and cut to h9_tts_maxchars, at a space if
   one is in the last twelve characters, never in the middle of a UTF-8
   character. One line per h9_tts_gap seconds counts; the ones in between are
   only written.

   A listener: a living human whose game says it speaks (the userinfo key
   _h9tts is 1), within h9_tts_range units of the speaker, who is himself one
   while h9_tts_self is 1,
   walls or no walls. For say_team, only a player of the speaker's team
   (get_user_team). The volume sent is 100 within a quarter of the range
   (200 units at 800) and falls in a straight line to 0 at the range; under
   4 is not sent. A listener is sent one line every 0.3 s at the most, from
   all speakers together (the reliable channel is spared; the client then
   plays the nearest voices first, section 5).

   Match mode, classes, perks and teams of the match play no part: the plugin
   uses no tsm_ native. It behaves the same on a plain TS server.

 3. THE VOICE
 ------------

   The voice is decided by the speaker's MODEL NAME and nothing else (the
   userinfo key "model", in any case):

       the name has mech or ed209 in it     a Mech's voice
       else droid, robo or _bot             a bot's voice
       else fem, woman, women, girl, lady   a woman's voice
       else                                 a man's voice

   Of the 148 folders in models\player: the Mech's are mech_msr_bot,
   mech_ins_bot and ed209_ts; the bot's msrdroid_bot and insdroid_bot; the
   woman's the 32 with fem in the name (fem_nh_*, bfem_nh_*, prole_fem,
   prole_bfem, ins_prole_fem, ins_medic_fem, ins_medic_bfem, repuheavy_fem*,
   repuheavy_bfem_*, ...); the rest, 111, a man's. The tables are at the top
   of h9_tts.sma.

   What each platform does with it:

     Windows (SAPI)   the best installed voice of the gender: a Natural or
                      Neural voice ahead of a plain one, a voice without
                      "Desktop" in its name ahead of one with, English of the
                      US, then the UK, ahead of other languages (section 3d
                      says how to get better ones). A speaker who asked for a
                      voice variant (3c) gets the n-th of those instead, and a
                      voice the listener picked himself (3b) beats both. A
                      machine with no female voice speaks the woman as the
                      man's, higher; with no voice at all the game says
                      _h9tts 0 and is sent nothing. A Mech is the man's voice
                      at 0.80 of its speed and pitch, a bot's at 1.22, and both
                      through a robot filter (ring modulation,
                      cl_h9_tts_robot 1).
     web client       the browser's own voices, a man's or a woman's picked
                      by name (the Web Speech API says nothing of gender),
                      a Natural or Neural one ahead of the rest, and by
                      language; without a match the default voice, a woman's
                      raised. A Mech is the man's at pitch 0.5, rate 0.8, a
                      bot's at pitch 1.7, rate 1.25.
     elsewhere        nothing. A native Linux client has no speech backend
                      yet (it would be speech-dispatcher or espeak-ng behind
                      h9_tts_platform.h): it says _h9tts 0 and is sent
                      nothing. A Windows client under Wine or Proton speaks
                      only if Wine offers it a SAPI voice, which it usually
                      does not (untested). A Linux player hears the speech in
                      the web client, with the browser's voices.

 3a. THE GAS MASKS
 -----------------

   A model on the list in configs\h9_tts_gasmask.cfg sounds as if its wearer
   spoke through a mask. It is a man's or a woman's voice that is muffled: a
   Mech's and a bot's never is, whatever is listed.

   HOW TO EDIT IT. One model name a line, as the model's folder is called, in
   any case. A line that starts with // is a comment, a blank line is
   skipped, anything after the name on its line is ignored. A name that ends
   in * is every model whose name starts so (repuheavy* is the whole
   repuheavy family), and a name with a ! before it takes a model out again,
   whatever else lists it. Put a name in to
   put a mask on that model, take it out to give the voice back. TSM keeps the
   master copy (build.bat copies it over the server's on every build); the
   plugin reads it as each map loads. Nothing else decides it: not a class, a
   team or the match mode, and the plugin does not read ts_matchmode.cfg.

   WHAT IS ON IT TO BEGIN WITH: every MSR-side model of ts_matchmode.cfg's
   tsm_class1 to tsm_class9 (the names left of the | in each line's fourth
   field; the master and the three presets agree) -- 46 names: repuheavy_pack,
   repuheavy_fem_pack, repuheavy_soot_blood, repuheavy_blooded, RepuHeavyGld
   and its 7 variants, repughillie and its 7, HeavyTengu, RepuHeavyW and its 7,
   RepuHeavyM and its 7, RepuTengu and its 7, repuheavy_recon -- without the
   droid msrdroid_bot and the Mech mech_msr_bot, plus repuheavy, the MSR team
   model of server.cfg's mp_teammodels, which a plain server hands out. No
   insurgent model and no stock TS model is on it. The other MSR-looking
   folders that no class uses (repuheavy_dust, RepuTenguG, rh_agent_soot, ...)
   are not by name; add the ones a server hands out. Of these, repuheavy_fem_pack is a
   woman's voice, so it is muffled as a woman's; the other 46 as a man's.
   Since 2026-11-09 the list ends with the wildcards repuheavy*, reputengu*,
   repughillie*, rh_agent_* and rh_laur_* and heavytengu, so every skin and every
   woman's model of those families is masked (repuheavy_fem and its variants had no
   line before), and takes the two models without a helmet out again,
   !repuheavy_fem_nhelm and !repuheavy_bfem_nhelm. Type h9_tts at the console to see
   whether your own model is masked: it names your model and says yes or no.

   How it sounds: Windows runs the rendered voice through two low-passes in a
   row (a fourth-order roll-off at 1800 Hz / (1 + cl_h9_tts_muffle): 900 Hz by
   default) and a resonance at 700 Hz, then brings the line back to 85 percent
   of the loudness it had, so the mask is heard as a dull, boxy voice and not
   as a quiet one. cl_h9_tts_muffle (0 to 2, saved, default 1) is the strength:
   0 takes the mask off, 2 is heavy. The browser exposes no audio data and so
   cannot filter: the web client approximates it with a voice at (1 - 0.3 s)
   of the volume, (1 - 0.1 s) of the pitch and (1 - 0.05 s) of the rate, s being
   cl_h9_tts_muffle.

 3b. CHOOSING THE VOICES
 -----------------------

   There are four kinds of speaker, from the model's name (section 3): a man, a woman, a Mech and a bot; and a mask on top of a man or a
   woman (3a). The game picks a voice for each from the ones installed on the machine, and a player can pick his own:

       exec ttsactors.cfg          lists the voices on this machine, numbered, and says how to pick and try them
       cl_h9_tts_voices            the same list (Windows: SAPI's voices, e.g. David and Zira; the web client: the browser's)
       cl_h9_tts_voice_man N       a voice's NUMBER for a man, a woman, a Mech and a bot; 0 (the default) leaves it to the game,
       cl_h9_tts_voice_woman N     which is the best Male voice for a man and the best Female voice for a woman; a Mech and a
       cl_h9_tts_voice_mech N      bot with no pick of their own use the man's
       cl_h9_tts_voice_bot N
       cl_h9_tts_test man Hello    speaks the words as man, woman, mech or bot, with the picks in force; add the word muffled
                                   after the kind to hear the mask: cl_h9_tts_test woman muffled Over here
       cl_h9_tts_robot 1           the robot sound of a Mech and a bot (0 turns it off)

   The robot sound is a ring modulator over the rendered voice (a 38 Hz hum for a Mech, 62 Hz for a bot, 65 percent of the line), on
   top of the lower slower Mech and higher faster bot of section 3. The web client cannot do it (the browser gives no audio to
   filter): there a Mech and a bot differ only in pitch and speed. A pick is a number in the machine's own list, so it means a
   different voice on another machine; the picks are saved with the player's config.

   How what you hear is set (all saved, all on your own machine):

       cl_h9_tts_volume 1          how loud (0 to 2)
       cl_h9_tts_muffle 1          how strongly a gas mask muffles (0 off, 2 heavy; 3a)
       cl_h9_tts_pan 1             1 puts each voice in the ear it comes from (Windows, stereo); 0 plays them in the middle
       cl_h9_tts_maxvoices 2       how many voices at once (1 to 3). When that many are playing, a voice louder than the
                                   quietest by a fifth (the nearer speaker) takes its place and a quieter one is not spoken
       cl_h9_tts_individual 1      a speaker who asked for no voice of his own gets one from his slot: the n-th voice of his
                                   kind and a small step off its pitch, so a room of players does not all sound alike (0: all alike)

 3c. HOW YOU SOUND TO OTHERS
 ---------------------------

       cl_h9_tts_myvoice N         0 (default) leaves your voice to each listener's game (and cl_h9_tts_individual); 1 to 99 asks
                                   for the n-th voice of your kind (man or woman) on THEIR machine, wrapping round when it has fewer
       cl_h9_tts_mydepth N         -10 deeper to 10 higher (each step is 2.5 percent of the speed and pitch)
       cl_h9_tts_test man Hello    speaks as you sound to others: with your myvoice and mydepth, in the middle

   The game sends the two to the server as the client command h9_ttsvoice <variant> <depth> (not through the userinfo,
   which is full): when the server asks as you join (the plugin sends H9Tts with slot 0 and flag bit 1, so a server without
   the plugin is never sent a command it does not have), and again whenever you change either cvar. The server puts them
   in each line it sends about you. h9_ttsvoice with no argument prints what the server holds for you. A listener's own
   pick (3b) still decides which voice his machine uses; your depth applies either way. The voices are the listener's
   machine's, so "voice 3" is a different voice on each; it is the same relative voice, not the same sound.

 3d. BETTER, MORE REALISTIC VOICES
 ---------------------------------

   The game speaks with the voices the machine has; the quality is theirs, and it is the machine that decides. The default
   already prefers a Natural or Neural voice over a plain one, so installing one is all it takes: cl_h9_tts_voices lists
   them, and nothing needs picking.

     Windows   the SAPI voices that come with it (Microsoft David and Zira "Desktop") are the robotic ones. The newer
               Natural voices (Narrator's, Microsoft Edge's) are not SAPI voices, and a SAPI program does not see them. A
               third-party open-source adapter, NaturalVoiceSAPIAdapter (github.com/gexgd0419/NaturalVoiceSAPIAdapter),
               makes them appear as SAPI 5 voices; it is not H9's, not shipped and not reviewed here -- read its source and
               license before installing it. Other SAPI 5 voices (commercial or free) work the same way.
     browser   Edge offers "Microsoft ... Online (Natural)" voices to a page, Chrome the Google ones; the web client
               prefers a Natural or Neural name, then a local voice, in the page's language.
     later     a voice H9 ships itself (a small neural model such as Piper's, run by a helper process) would need its own
               license check and a few tens of megabytes per voice; it is not done.

 4. THE PROTOCOL
 ---------------

   The plugin registers the user message H9Tts in plugin_precache, whatever
   h9_texttospeech says, with engfunc(EngFunc_RegUserMsg, "H9Tts", -1), the
   way h9vgui_precache registers H9Vgui (include\h9_vgui.inc): every game
   learns it at connect. (The size is -1, variable.) A message goes to one
   listener, begun with engfunc(EngFunc_MessageBegin, MSG_ONE, ...) because
   Metamod-R records only the game library's messages, and RELIABLE since
   2026-11-09: on a big map the engine drops an unreliable message together
   with the datagram that cannot hold it ("Datagram overflowed" in
   ts\logs\h9_overflow.log), and on the roleplay maps that was every line. The
   reliable channel is spared by sending a listener one line every 0.3 s at
   most, from all speakers together.

       byte     the speaker's slot (1 to 128)
       byte     the voice: 0 man, 1 woman, 2 Mech, 3 bot
       byte     the volume, 0 to 100
       byte     flags: bit 0 (1), a gas mask muffles the voice; the rest 0
       string   the words, at most h9_tts_maxchars (150 at the most), NUL ended
       byte     the variant the speaker asked others to hear him with (0 none, 1 to 99)    } the tail: a client reads
       char     the depth he asked for, -10 to 10                                          } it only when the message
       char     the pan: -100 his voice in the listener's left ear, 100 right, 0 the       } is long enough for it, so
                middle; from the speaker's direction and the listener's yaw, fading to     } an older client takes the
                0 within 160 units                                                         } message as it was

   About 100 bytes at the default, 158 at the most; the client takes no more
   than 192. A message with slot 0, voice 0, volume 0, flags 2 and no words is
   the server asking this game for its h9_ttsvoice (3c); it is sent once, a few
   seconds after a player joins.

   A game says it can speak with the userinfo key _h9tts, as it says it draws
   H9 VGUI with _h9vgui: "1" while cl_h9_tts is not 0 and the machine has a
   voice (Windows: SAPI could make one; web: speechSynthesis exists), else
   "0". A game without the key (a Steam client, an older H9 client) is sent
   nothing. Were a message to reach a game that does not know it anyway, the
   engine drops a user message nobody hooked, harmlessly.

 5. THE CLIENT (reTS cl_dll\h9_tts.cpp, h9_tts_win.cpp, h9_tts_web.cpp)
 ----------------------------------------------------------------------

   Client cvars, all archived: cl_h9_tts (1; 0 is silence for this player,
   and the game tells servers by _h9tts 0) and cl_h9_tts_volume (1; 0 to 2;
   the distance volume is multiplied by it); the voice picks, cl_h9_tts_voice_man,
   _woman, _mech and _bot, cl_h9_tts_robot, _muffle, _pan, _maxvoices and
   _individual (section 3b), cl_h9_tts_myvoice and _mydepth (3c); the commands
   cl_h9_tts_voices and cl_h9_tts_test.

   A server is untrusted. Whatever a message says, the client's own limits
   hold: a line is cleaned the same way and cut to 160 bytes; at most 8 lines
   a second from everyone together are read, and no speaker's lines come
   closer than 0.3 s; whom a line is sent to is the server's say (the speaker
   too while h9_tts_self is 1). A speaker has
   ONE line at a time: his newer line replaces his older one, at once.

   Windows. One worker thread of its own (its own COM apartment) does it all,
   so the render thread only ever queues a line (8 waiting at most, the oldest
   dropped): SAPI renders the line to memory (22050 Hz, 16 bit, mono), as
   PLAIN TEXT (SPF_IS_NOT_XML); a Mech, a bot and the speaker's depth are
   resampled; a gas mask runs the filter of 3a over it; the volume is
   multiplied into the samples, with 4 dB of headroom at a volume of 1, and a
   constant-power pan puts the voice in the ear it comes from; and waveOut
   plays it (stereo). At most cl_h9_tts_maxvoices voices play together (1 to
   3): when they are all busy a line louder than the quietest by a fifth takes
   its place, a quieter one is not spoken. A line is cut to 30 seconds.
   client.dll links ole32 and winmm and NOT sapi.lib: SAPI
   is reached through COM at run time, so a machine without it (Wine) still loads
   client.dll and says _h9tts 0.

   Web client. The same source, compiled for the browser, calls the page's
   speechSynthesis through the one script function installed with
   emscripten_run_script (EM_JS is not resolved in a side module); lines wait in
   a queue of three and are spoken one at a time, because speechSynthesis has
   one voice; a speaker's newer line replaces his older one, cancelling it if
   it is the one being spoken. No page file changes: the code runs in the
   page that hosts the game, and a browser without speechSynthesis makes the
   game say _h9tts 0.

   Any other platform: the message is hooked and ignored, and _h9tts is 0.

 6. LIMITS, ALL TOGETHER
 -----------------------

     a line         96 characters by default, 150 at the most (server);
                    160 bytes (client)
     a speaker      one spoken line every 1.5 s (server, h9_tts_gap); 0.3 s
                    (client)
     a listener     one line every 0.3 s from all speakers together (server)
     the client     8 lines a second in all; cl_h9_tts_maxvoices voices at
                    once on Windows (2 by default, 3 at the most), one at a
                    time in the browser; 8 lines waiting on Windows, 3 in the
                    browser
     a message      about 100 bytes, reliable, to listeners within range only

 7. PRIVACY AND ABUSE
 --------------------

   * What leaves a machine: the text the chat already shows. Nothing is
     recorded, nothing is sent to H9: the Windows voices run on the player's
     own machine. In a browser, a voice that is not local (some browsers'
     "Google" voices) sends the text to its vendor's speech service, as any
     page that uses speechSynthesis does; the web client prefers a local
     voice among those of the same language. A player who minds types cl_h9_tts 0.
   * Spam and shouting: one line per h9_tts_gap seconds and h9_tts_maxchars
     characters, then the client's own limits. Commands are never spoken.
   * Injection: the text is spoken as PLAIN text on Windows (SPF_IS_NOT_XML),
     so nothing typed can be taken for SAPI markup -- no <audio src=...> that
     fetches a file or an address, no <voice>, <pitch> or <rate>. A server
     cannot make a client open a file or an address by this message; the
     most it can do is make the client speak short lines of its choosing, in
     the four voices, at the client's own rate limits.
   * What a server can do about a player who abuses it: mute his account
     (h9web; his chat is not spoken or written), gag or kick him with the
     tools it has; take away what he can reach with h9_tts_range (smaller),
     h9_tts_gap (longer) and h9_tts_maxchars (shorter); or turn the whole thing
     off with h9_texttospeech 0, which takes effect with the next line. A
     player can always turn it off for himself with cl_h9_tts 0 (and then no
     message is sent to him), or lower cl_h9_tts_volume.
   * Not done: no filter of words, no translation. Voices read what they are
     given in their own language, and badly in another.

 8. TESTING IT LIVE (nothing in this list has been run)
 ------------------------------------------------------

   Set up: a server with h9_tts.amxx loaded and h9_texttospeech 1; two H9
   clients with the new client.dll, A (the speaker) and B (the listener),
   side by side; B types setinfo, with no argument, and sees _h9tts 1.
   Native Windows with at least one SAPI voice (Windows has Microsoft David and
   Microsoft Zira), then the web client in Chrome and in Firefox.

   1. A man. A wears seal (or any model without a word of the tables), says
      "hello there": B hears David within about a second, and the chat line
      is written as ever. The very first line after the game starts is slower
      (SAPI loads the voice).
   2. A woman. A wears prole_fem: B hears Zira.
   3. A Mech and a bot. A wears mech_msr_bot: slow and low. A wears
      msrdroid_bot: fast and high. A real bot (an rcbot fake client) says
      something wearing a human model: it is a man's or a woman's, by its
      model, not a bot's.
   4. Gas masks. A wears repuheavy_pack: a muffled man. repuheavy_fem_pack: a
      muffled woman. XCCPsgts (insurgent), seal and msrdroid_bot and
      mech_msr_bot: clear. Add msrdroid_bot and mech_msr_bot to the list file,
      change the map: still clear. Take repuheavy_pack out, change the map:
      clear. Do all of this once with the match mode off (tsm_enabled 0) on a
      plain map: the same.
   5. Distance. A speaks with B at 100 units (full volume), 500 (about half),
      790 (a whisper), 900 (silence); then with a wall between them at 300
      (still heard).
   6. The rate limit. A types two lines within a second: only the first is
      spoken, both are written. Set h9_tts_gap 0.2 and type two lines 0.5 s
      apart: the second cuts the first off. Three speakers at once: three
      voices, a fourth takes the oldest.
   7. B's cl_h9_tts 0: silence, and _h9tts reads 0; back to 1 and _h9tts reads
      1. cl_h9_tts_volume 0.3 and 2: quieter, louder.
   8. h9_texttospeech 0 and 1: nothing at 0, speech at 1, from one line to
      the next.
   9. say_team: B on A's team hears; C beside A on the other team does not.
  10. Not spoken: ".ready" and "/me waves"; a line of ":)"; A dead; A muted
      (h9web); a player under the Recon's cloak.
  11. A client without the new code (a Steam client, or the previous
      client.dll): it joins and plays as ever, and nothing is sent to it. To
      see the message reach such a client anyway, give it setinfo _h9tts 1:
      it takes the message, ignores it, and prints nothing.
  12. Wine, or a Windows with no voices, if one is to hand: the game loads and
      says _h9tts 0.
  13. The web client: the first speech may need a click on the page first
      (browsers do not speak before the page has been used); a Mech and a bot
      are lower and slower, higher and faster; a muffled voice is quieter and
      slightly lower; one voice at a time; a new line from the same speaker
      cuts his old one off.
  14. A crowd: ten bots chatting. No frame hitch; at most cl_h9_tts_maxvoices
      voices at once, the nearest speakers' first; the server log shows
      nothing from [TTS] but its start lines.
  15. The gas mask. A wears repuheavy_fem (it was not on the list before): A
      types h9_tts at the console and reads that the model is masked; B
      hears a dull boxy woman; cl_h9_tts_muffle 0, 1, 2 on B: clear, muffled,
      heavier. Take repuheavy* out of the list, change the map: clear.
  16. The ears. A and B stand 300 units apart, B facing A: A's voice is in
      the middle; B turns 90 degrees left: A's voice is in B's right ear, and
      the other way round. cl_h9_tts_pan 0: always in the middle. A speaker
      round a corner (no line of sight) is in the right ear still.
  17. The voice others hear. A sets cl_h9_tts_myvoice 2 and cl_h9_tts_mydepth
      -6, B (with two male voices installed) hears A lower and, with the
      second voice, another one than A's first. A types h9_ttsvoice at the
      console and reads variant 2, depth -6. A changes the map: the picks are
      sent again within a few seconds. B's own cl_h9_tts_voice_man N overrides
      the voice but not the depth.
  18. A server without the plugin: the client never sends h9_ttsvoice, and no
      "Unknown command" line appears at the console.
  19. A native Linux client or a Wine client with no SAPI voice: the game
      loads, _h9tts is 0, nothing is sent.
