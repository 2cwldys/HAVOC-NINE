TESTFLOW -- what to check in the game after the build of 2026-11-13
===================================================================

How to use this: go in order. Each step says what to do and what you should see. If a step fails, stop and tell me the
step number and what you saw: the later steps depend on it. Nothing here was seen in a real game yet: the engineers tested
on private servers with scripted bots. You are the first real player.
The siege is NOT in this build (it is being built). docs\ARP_SIEGE.txt describes it.

PART 0. START
-------------
 0.1 Close the game completely. Start it again from your git copy. (The new graphics DLL and plugins load only at a fresh start.)
 0.2 Create a game on the map h9_tsrp_hive.
 0.3 In the console type  amxx plugins .  EXPECT: eighteen ARP plugins running (ARP_Core first), plus H9 P2P, and no plugin marked
     "bad" or "error".
 0.4 To use admin commands, make yourself an ARP admin first (docs\ARP_ADMINS.txt). The steps below say "as admin" where needed.

PART 1. THE ARP CHANGES
-----------------------
 1.1 The panels. EXPECT a panel at the top left with the city name, your wallet and bank, the payday timer and your salary.
     docs\ARP_HUD_CHECKS.txt has the long list; this is the quick look.
 1.2 The Location line. Go to each town (the portals join them). EXPECT the Location line to say Alexandria, Paradise City or
     Xenon City, never "Unknown".
 1.3 Plaques. Look at a door of a shop or flat: EXPECT its name, FOR SALE and the price, or the owner. Look at an NPC: EXPECT its
     name, its job and how to use it, and NO owner or affiliation row.
 1.4 The new NPCs. Paradise City, the hall under the plaza's east side: the Cafe Avalon cantina sells food. Under the two floating
     BANK signs of Paradise City (one on the plaza level, one in the street of the Ministry of Defense Facility) stand two
     invisible bank terminals: go to the kiosk under a sign, press E, EXPECT the teller's menu.
 1.5 Prices. EXPECT prices at three times the old scale: a storage unit 3,600, a flat 7,200 to 14,000, a shop 29,000, the big
     places up to 110,000. The Paradise Bank's glass doors (the building at the north east of Paradise City) are state property:
     EXPECT the plaque to say so, nobody can buy them, and they open for everyone.
 1.6 Money. As a new character with no job, EXPECT no salary (Unemployed pays 0). Take a job (arp_employ, or as admin arp_setjob).
     EXPECT the salary to appear at the next payday (every 60 seconds). Admin: arp_h9_pay_dump lists what each job pays.
 1.7 Property tax by hand. As admin give yourself money (arp_createbank <you> 15000). Buy a flat (look at its door, say /buy).
     Say /tax: EXPECT a popup with what the property owes and how long you have. Look at the door, say /renew: EXPECT the tax menu,
     press 1 to pay, EXPECT your bank to drop at once. docs\ARP_TAX_CHECKS.txt, part C, has the full list (eviction after
     72 hours unpaid, automatic payment).
 1.8 Needs. EXPECT a Needs panel at the top right with Hunger and Thirst rows (if both are on in arp.cfg). Drink a Soda: thirst goes down. Quit and rejoin: EXPECT your thirst to be
     kept (arp_h9_thirst_keep 1).
 1.9 The use-key menu. Stand next to another player (a friend, or a second game) and press E on him. EXPECT a menu with a cursor:
     Trade, Search, Cuff, Tie, Tape, Fine, Give money, Show license. A row that does not apply is grey and says why ("Cuff him
     first", "You have no Cuffs", "Bots cannot trade"). As police (arp_setjob "<you>" "Cadet") EXPECT Cuff and Fine to work.
     cl_h9_ctxmenu 0 turns it off. This is the first real test of it: tell me anything odd.
 1.10 Solid shelves. Four bookshelves and bookcases in Alexandria now stop you (near x 1588 y 29, x 1588 y -212, x -1178 y 1111
     and x 2814 y 181). EXPECT that you cannot walk through them.
 1.11 Teams. EXPECT the scoreboard teams to follow your job: Ministry jobs blue, Insurrection red, civilians yellow. Accept an
     arp_employ offer for a Ministry job: EXPECT to change team at once. /unemploy: back to Proletarian.
 1.12 Dropped items. Drop an item: EXPECT a purple glow around the pack for about 12 seconds. In the inventory window
     (say /items) EXPECT the picture tiles, and no "Give to": goods change hands by E on a player.
 1.13 The docs. docs\ARP_H9_ADDITIONS.txt is the list of everything above with its switches. If something there does not match
     what you see, tell me the line.

PART 2. THE UPSCALER
--------------------
 2.1 Open the console. Type  r_scene_scale 0.7 . EXPECT the 3D picture to get softer and the frame rate to go up. The HUD
     stays sharp.
 2.2 Type  h9_upscale 1 . EXPECT the picture to get crisper at once, with the same low cost. If the console says it cannot do
     it, copy the line to me: the plain picture stays.
 2.3 Type  h9_upscale_sharp 0.5  then  1 . EXPECT the edges to get stronger. 0.84 is the default. If you see bright halos
     around edges at 1, go back down.
 2.4 Type  h9_upscale 0 . EXPECT the plain, softer picture again.
 2.5 Set  r_scene_scale 1  with  h9_upscale 1 . EXPECT at most a light sharpening and no scaling (docs\H9_UPSCALE.txt says what to expect).
 2.6 Quit and start again. EXPECT both settings to be remembered (they are saved with the graphics settings).
 docs\H9_UPSCALE.txt has a quick start and the full list of commands.

PART 3. P2P: A FRIEND JOINS WITHOUT A FORWARDED PORT
----------------------------------------------------
Needs Node.js 20 or newer on your PC (type  node --version  in a console). docs\P2P_RELAY.txt is the overview. You can test all
of it on one PC.
 3.1 Run a relay of your own. Copy the p2p folder from the repository to a scratch folder of your own (call it relaytest). In it run  run.bat . It makes two
     files and stops. Edit relay.config.json: set publicUdp host to 127.0.0.1, bind http and udp hosts to 127.0.0.1, and add
     "allowPrivatePeers": true (this is for a test on one PC only). Run  run.bat token Mike : it prints a token once. Run
     run.bat --check : EXPECT "fine". Run  run.bat  and leave the window open.
 3.2 Copy p2p.ini.example (in the game folder) to p2p.ini. Put the relay's address after service= (http://127.0.0.1:28247) and the
     token after token=.
 3.3 In the game's console type  h9_p2p 1  and then  h9_p2p_status . Then run  h9_p2p_host.bat  in a second window. EXPECT
     "the path is open" in that window. Type  h9_p2p_status  again: EXPECT "helper: running".
 3.4 Type  h9_p2p_invite Mike . EXPECT a code like H9-7KQMX-2PDRT in the console.
 3.5 In a third window run  h9_p2p_join.bat <the code> --no-game  (the game is already open). EXPECT a line "ready: connect
     127.101.x.y:27015". Type that line in the console of a second game window (a second copy on this PC works) or on a second PC. EXPECT to join, and
     the host console to say "Mike joined by the relay". Play a minute. The same code again: EXPECT "not valid".
 3.6 Type  h9_p2p_list  then  h9_p2p_kick 1 . EXPECT the friend to be dropped.
 3.7 Public list: type  h9_p2p_public 1 . Within half a minute  http://127.0.0.1:28247/v1/public  (in a browser) shows your
     game: name, map, players, no address. The website's servers page shows it under "P2P games" when the site runs
     (docs\P2P_HOSTING.txt). EXPECT the game NOT to be in the game's own Find Servers list: that is the known limit.
 3.8 Type  h9_p2p 0 . EXPECT the helper window to say the path is closed, and nothing else to run. Type  h9_p2p 1  again:
     EXPECT it to come back.
 The engineers' fuller lists: docs\P2P_JOIN.txt (the guide) and docs\P2P_HOSTING.txt (the relay).
