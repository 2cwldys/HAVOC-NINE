===============================================================================
 PLAYER COMMANDS  (typed in chat, available to everyone)
===============================================================================

 .menu
     Opens the menu of menus: pick a side, pick a class, match status.
     The way back to anything dismissed by accident.

 .team
     Opens the side menu (MSR / Insurrectionists / Spectate).
     During warmup this also RELEASES your current side first, so you can
     switch. Once a match is running it refuses -- sides are locked -- unless
     tsm_latejoin is on for the mode (every mode by default): a person who
     arrived late, or leaves the spectators, then picks a side as usual and is
     let in at his side's next respawn (the next wave, else the next round);
     until then he watches, and .team lets him change the side he waits on.
     Whoever already plays a side keeps it.

     Choosing Spectate is not a black screen: you get the same follow
     camera an eliminated player does. Your view stands with a living
     player and follows them, ATTACK (or attack2) moves you on to the
     next one, and because you have no side the cycle runs over BOTH
     teams -- an eliminated ghost is held to their own team, you watch
     everybody. If nobody is left alive to watch you are parked out of
     the way until someone stands up again. The click is a camera
     control, not a shot: it never reaches TS as one.

 .class
     Opens the class menu, DURING THE FREEZE COUNTDOWN BEFORE A ROUND and at no
     other time. That is when the loadout is handed out, so it is the only
     moment a pick does anything.

     Outside it you are told why rather than shown a menu. A class chosen in
     warmup or ready-up used to be accepted, confirmed, and then thrown away by
     the match starting -- the menu worked and the choice did not.

     Note that a match HALTED for an uneven side sits in ready-up with the score
     intact, so "we are in ready-up" does not mean no match is running. Picks
     are refused there too.

     tsm_classchange (on in every mode by default; 2 is Wave Survival alone) lets you ask for
     another class at any time: .class opens the menu again, your class marked
     (yours) and a pick marked (next). A pick made outside the freeze countdown
     is held, and goes on at your NEXT RESPAWN -- the revive of a wave after a
     death, else the start of the next round -- with that class's kit, health,
     armour, speed, height, model and lives; you keep playing your class until
     then, and are told so. The pick takes its class's slot at once (a full
     class is refused as always) and your old class's slot is freed only when
     it goes on; picking again replaces the pick, and picking the class you
     play drops it. In the freeze countdown a pick is the class at once, as a
     first pick is. A late joiner (tsm_latejoin) picks his first class the same
     way: it goes on when he is let in. With tsm_classchange 0 a class is
     locked once picked.

     Four classes have a job of their own:

       Combat Engineer   +USE on a teammate resupplies them, every 30 seconds
                         (tsm_eng_cooldown). They glow YELLOW while it lands
                         and it is audible to anyone nearby. .turret stands
                         a sentry turret; +USE on one of your side's repairs
                         it.
       Breacher          +USE forces a DOOR open -- and shows "Breached." --
                         that part is still one press, and doors only respond
                         when they were actually LOCKED ("Use Only" spawnflag)
                         or whose targetname is on one of the map's
                         tsm_breachdoor_# lists; an everyday unlocked door
                         gets stock TS behaviour and no message. On siege
                         objectives the Breacher is the only PLANTER: hold
                         +USE for the ten-second plant (a bar fills at the
                         centre of their screen), the satchel burns its fuse,
                         and the explosion is what takes the objective --
                         nothing falls on a single press of E. No other
                         class may plant; they can only destroy bodies that
                         CAN break, by gunfire and grenades. A same-side
                         player can never breach their own objective.
                         Carries the Benelli M3.
       Officer           Commands bots with .follow and .unfollow, and .scan
                         exposes cloaked enemies nearby. Carries the Laser
                         Rifle (H9's reTS), the Glock 18 and a Katana (the M4A1
                         in the Rifle's place on a game library without it, or
                         with h9_customweapons 0).
                         When an Officer is slain, both teams hear it
                         (tsm_officerslain_sound).
       Medic             +USE on a teammate heals them 50hp on a 15s cooldown,
                         capped at that teammate's own class health. They glow
                         RED while it lands, also audible nearby. Sidearm only,
                         no primary.

     Both of those sounds come off the player being worked on rather than being
     sent to the two involved, so the squad standing around them hears it too --
     that is the point of them.

     Two classes do something you do not press a key for:

       Marksman          Barrett M82 and a Glock 20. On MSR they wear the
                         repughillie suit; an Insurrectionist marksman falls
                         back to that side's Assault model.
       Heavy             200hp and noticeably slower, with one frag grenade, and
                         recovers 10hp every 15 seconds on his own.

                         THAT RECOVERY STOPS THE MOMENT HE IS HIT and does not
                         start again until 15 seconds after the last hit --
                         enemy fire, a fall, a grenade, all of it. It is what
                         lets a Heavy who breaks contact come back, not
                         something that keeps one alive in a firefight. It stops
                         at his own 200 and never climbs past it.

     Each perk does one thing. A Breacher cannot heal or resupply, a Medic cannot
     breach, and so on.

 .ready
     Mark yourself ready. Requires a side and requires being spawned in.
     Refused with a reason if either is missing.

 .unready
     Withdraw your ready state.

 .stim    (or bind a key to:  stim)
     Use your stimpack: 25 to 50 health to yourself, ONCE A ROUND, whatever class
     you are. How much is rolled when you use it, so spending it early may buy little.

     Capped at your own class health, so a Heavy stops at 200 and a Marksman at 85.

     Using it at full health is refused AND KEEPS IT -- you are told so and can still
     use it later in the round.

     You glow red while it lands, as a medic's heal does (an engineer's resupply glows
     yellow), and it makes the medic's healing noise from your own body so the
     people around you know you have spent it.

     It comes back at the start of the next round. It does NOT come back if you
     respawn in the middle of one. In Wave Survival every new wave is a new round
     for it, as for .ammo, the class abilities and the Mech's rockets.

 .cloak    /    .uncloak
     Recon only, in a live round: shimmer near-invisible and back. The model stays
     drawn, very faint -- not a true vanish, a close or careful look still finds
     you. You cannot see it on yourself: your own client draws you at its own
     opacity, whatever the server sends. One of two sounds plays either way (picked
     at random), heard up close and not across the map, plus a blue flash anyone
     nearby can see.

     Bots neither see nor target you while it is up. It is not damage immunity:
     shots and blasts still hurt you.

     TAKING OR DEALING ANY DAMAGE BREAKS IT, with the uncloak sound and flash. It is
     for moving unseen, not for fighting from inside it.

     CARRYING AN OBJECTIVE BREAKS IT TOO. A cloaked Recon who takes the flag in
     Capture the Flag, or the uplink in Capture the Uplink, is uncloaked on the
     spot, with the same sound and flash, and .cloak is refused ("You cannot cloak
     while you carry the objective.") for as long as he carries it: until it is
     dropped, returned or captured, or he dies or leaves. The carrier is marked to
     both sides anyway, so a cloak would hide nothing.

     Expires on its own after 30 seconds (tsm_cloak_duration) if nothing else ends it
     first -- same sound and flash as typing .uncloak. .uncloak any time before that.
     After any end, .cloak waits 30 seconds (tsm_cloak_cooldown) before it works again.
     If the match takes you out of the round some other way (eliminated, benched, the
     warmup hold) it ends on its own, silently.

 .scan    (or bind a key to:  say .scan)
     Officer only, in a live round: pulse the scanner and every cloaked ENEMY within
     1024 units (tsm_scan_radius) of you is exposed to your WHOLE SIDE for 10 seconds
     (tsm_scan_time) -- not just to you, to everyone wearing your colour. You are
     told how many were caught, and your side is told with you.

     The mark is a bright blue glow shell around each caught man, and for those ten
     seconds his body is also drawn solid to your side, so the cloak is no use to
     him until it fades. It is the medic's glow shell, which TS draws the same on
     both engines, in line of sight. During sudden death on H9XASH the x-ray's
     through-wall shell takes its place, over a body still drawn solid. Your side's
     bots see him too and may open fire.
     His own side sees none of that.

     The mark is TIME, not state: set when the scan fires, out on its own ten
     seconds later. Uncloak inside the window and the blue stops mattering; recloak
     and it is still live and finds you again.

     Once per 30 seconds (tsm_scan_cooldown, measured from use, like the cloak's
     cooldown), and it re-arms at the start of every round. The scan plays
     scan.wav (tsm_scan_sound; empty makes it silent), heard by whoever is near
     the Officer -- BOTH sides -- and flashes the same blue point light the
     Recon's cloak uses, so an enemy who saw a blue pulse knows either a Recon
     just slipped away or an Officer just read the area.

     Bots cannot use it: no bot types chat commands.

 .reinforce  (or bind a key to:  say .reinforce)
     Officer only, while alive: call the team's waiting dead back now. Every
     teammate waiting for a wave comes back at once, whatever the wave timer
     says -- bots included, exactly like a natural wave. Once per
     tsm_reinforce_cooldown seconds (120); a call with nobody waiting costs
     nothing. Needs tsm_respawns 1. While anyone is waiting, your screen also
     lists them on the right.

 .forfeit
     Vote to concede the match for your side. EVERY human on that side has to type
     it -- one person saying it is an opinion, all of them saying it is a decision.

     Each vote is announced with how many have agreed and who is still holding out,
     so the side knows it is being asked.

     BOTS DO NOT COUNT. A side of one human and five bots could otherwise never
     concede, because five of its six members can never type anything.

     The other side takes the match, scored and announced exactly as it is when a
     side is left empty. Votes are cleared at the start of every round.

 .status
     Print the current match state to chat (to your console with tsm_verbosity
     0).

 .advertise  /  .ad          (ts_advertise -- a separate plugin)
     Post to Discord that a match is being organised. The bot posts an embed with
     the map, the mode as the server browser names it, how many are on, how many of those are bots, what the match is doing
     and a connect line.

     ONE EVERY THIRTY MINUTES FOR THE WHOLE SERVER, not per player, and it survives
     a map change -- the next map does not hand anybody a fresh one. Anyone refused
     is told how long is left.

     It pings a Discord role, so it is deliberately hard to do often.

     The clock only starts once Discord has ACCEPTED the post. A failed advert does
     not cost anyone the next half hour.

     Needs tsm_ad_token and tsm_ad_channel set in ts_advertise.cfg and the
     AmxxEasyHttp module installed; without them it says so in chat and posts
     nothing.

     THE DISCORD BOT DOES NOT HAVE TO BE RUNNING for this. A bot token is a
     credential rather than a connection, so the game server posts by itself.
     Running discord-bot\ts_bot.py adds what has to come back the other way:
     the bot showing online, the /who /status /advertise slash commands, and the
     RSVP buttons on an advert.

     Setup, layout and what the failures mean: discord-bot\README.md

 .roam
     Toggle free movement. Lifts every hold -- warmup, freeze, spectator -- and
     gives you normal movement so you can walk the map.

     This is how you place spawn points. The warmup hold clamps maxspeed to 1, so
     without it you cannot leave the spot you spawned on; doing it during a live
     round instead means doing it under fire.

         .roam
         walk to a position, amx_tsm_spawn_add 1   (or 2)
         repeat
         amx_tsm_spawn_save
         .roam                                     (back to normal)

     You get your WEAPONS while roaming, and lose them again when you switch it
     off. Whatever hold you were under before will have disarmed you, which left a
     roamer with nothing to shoot and nothing to open a door with -- and trying a
     doorway is half of what walking a map is for. They are handed back on the way in
     and taken on the way out, so you never carry them into the match.

     Attack, +use and the impulse keys all work while roaming. They used to be wiped
     along with everything else by the warmup and freeze holds, which is why .roam
     gave you movement and nothing else.

     The hold itself is not saved and restored: it is recomputed from the match state
     every frame, so switching roaming off simply lets that take over again.

 .follow   /   .squad          (Officer class only, during a round)
     Take every free bot on your side under command -- the nearest first, no
     cap. They hold on you until you dismiss them or the round ends.

     A bot already commanded by another Officer is left alone and you are told
     so. It is never taken off him: two Officers steering one bot would drag it
     between them every frame.

     They are held as a LOOP, and it stays a loop for the whole round: beyond
     tsm_squad_dist (160 units) the plugin pulls a bot towards you, a bot that
     STAYS out there for 3 seconds is LEASHED back to a clear spot beside you
     (never onto you, never into a body), and one that wanders beyond
     tsm_squad_warp (1200) is warped to you at once. tsm_squad_leash (50) is how
     far out counts as gone: it sits inside tsm_squad_dist, so the 3-second
     clock starts the moment a bot leaves the formation. Inside tsm_squad_dist
     the bot is rcbot's own to fight, path and roam with.

     An Officer is told about both commands in chat when the round starts, so
     the perk is not invisible.

 .unfollow /   .dismiss        (Officer class only)
     Stand the whole squad down. Releases only YOUR bots.

 .unstick                      (any live player)
     Pushes bots IN YOUR SQUAD within ~56 units off you, and lifts you +24
     units if you are genuinely embedded in solid. Bots are moved to a clear
     ring spot (8 directions tried) and zeroed. 3-second cooldown. Bots only
     -- never affects humans. Only affects bots currently following YOU.

 .turret                       (Combat Engineer only, live round, alive)
     Stand a sentry turret on the floor 64 units in front of you, facing the
     way you face: blue for MSR, red for the Insurgents. It deploys for a
     second, then shoots the OTHER side's players in its sight -- never your
     side, never a spectator, never a cloaked Recon. ONE A ROUND per engineer
     (one a wave in Wave Survival), even once it is destroyed, and two standing
     per side (tsm_turret_per_team). +USE on your
     side's turret puts 100 health back, once every 30 seconds per turret.
     Enemy fire destroys it; every turret goes at the start of each round and
     when its engineer leaves. Refused, with the reason, on a wall, a ledge or
     a player. Also the console command turret.

 .pickup                       (Combat Engineer only, live round, alive)
     Pack up YOUR turret -- the one you are looking at, within +USE reach
     (tsm_eng_range) -- and carry it. Your next .turret sets it down again
     with the health it had and its repair wait still running, even though
     the round's one turret is already used. A carried turret is lost at the
     next round (wave in Wave Survival). Also the console command pickup.
     With no turret in view it packs up the transporter pad of yours that
     stands nearest, within reach, and gives its place back.

 .transporter                  (Combat Engineer only, live round, alive)
     Set a transporter pad on the floor where you stand, in your side's
     light: tsm_transporter_per_engineer of them a round (two). Two pads of
     one engineer are a pair; once they are, an arrow on each pad points at
     the other, and anyone of your side who steps on one is carried to the
     other (then waits tsm_transporter_cooldown seconds). A lone pad does
     nothing. The pads, their effects and arrows go with the round. A pad
     can be shot to pieces by the other side (tsm_transporter_hp), and blows
     up when its engineer dies, as a turret does. .pickup packs one up. Also
     the console command transporter.

 .rocket                       (Mech class only, while alive)
     Fire a rocket at whatever your crosshair is on, up to 2000 units away: a
     white trail to the spot and a blast there with splash damage, 65 at the
     centre. Four a life, 30 seconds apart; every spawn hands all four back, and
     so does every new wave in Wave Survival.
     Where the game library has the Mech's rocket launcher (weapon 41,
     h9_mechrocket, with h9_customweapons on) the Mech carries that as a weapon
     instead: select it and fire, and .rocket only says so. On a library
     without it, .rocket works exactly as above.

 .light
     Toggle your flashlight on or off, whatever weapon you are holding. Only
     while playing in a live round.

 .ammo
     +25 spare rounds for every gun you carry, ONCE A ROUND (once a wave in Wave
     Survival), while playing in a live round. Guns that share a calibre share the 25. If everything is
     already at TS's own cap nothing lands, you are told your guns are full,
     and it is not spent. A Mech whose reserve is at the cap puts the 25 in its
     ammo bay (tsm_mech_ammo_mult) instead, while the bay has room. Same yellow
     glow and ammo sound as an engineer's resupply.

 .mechtest    (or in the console:  tsm_mechtest)
     The mech sound bench. Prints to your console whether the server takes you
     for a Mech -- your model path, your resolved class, whether the mech kit
     recognises the body -- then plays mechwalk, mechloop and one clang on
     your own client, so "not detected" can be told apart from "not audible".

 tsm_music 0|1                 (console)
     Ambient music on or off, for you alone; on by default, and with no number
     it flips. Remembered through setinfo, so it survives a reconnect. Off also
     silences the round-start anthems. The queue plays every track once before
     any plays again, never one twice in a row, and keeps its place from map to
     map; an admin's tsm_music_skip moves it on for everyone. A roleplay
     map plays the queue too (tsm_music_rp), and tsm_music 0 mutes it there as
     anywhere else.

 .rtv
     Rock the vote: you want a new map. Once 60% of the people on the server
     have said it, everyone gets a menu of maps to vote on (ts_rtv.amxx; ROCK
     THE VOTE, below). Not while a match is in progress, unless the server
     sets tsm_rtv_in_match 1, and not on a roleplay map, unless it sets
     tsm_rtv_roleplay 1. While a vote runs, .rtv brings its menu back.

 .nominate [part of a map's name]
     Puts a map on the next vote. One map that matches is nominated at once,
     several are offered in a menu, and .nominate alone lists every map that
     can be nominated here. One nomination each: a second replaces the first.

 .unnominate
     Takes your nomination back.

 .unrtv
     Takes your .rtv back, while the vote has not started.

 tsm_model                     (console)
     Which player model the gamemode picked for you: class, side, the model
     chosen, whether it is on disk, the droid hand set that model gives you (or
     none), and the list it was drawn from with "pick n of m". An m smaller than
     the names in the config means the list is being cut short somewhere.

 .link
     Links your game to your havoc9.org account from in game (h9_link.amxx):
     your game shows a code, you approve it on havoc9.org/link while signed
     in, and from then on the game plays as your account. H9XASH only; the
     web client is signed in already. Link your Account in the game's main
     menu does the same. docs\CUSTOM_XASH_USERID.txt section 2a.

 .rank [name]   (also /rank, !rank, /elo, !elo)
     Your havoc9.org rank (h9_rank.amxx), to you alone: your level, your
     rating and place on the H9 ladder and on this server's table, and this
     season's matches, wins and losses, kills, deaths, assists (where a
     server counted them) and K/D; one chat line, and the whole readout in
     your console. With a name: that player's here (part of the name will
     do), else the havoc9.org account of that name. A bot, or a player not
     signed in to a havoc9.org account here, has no rank; no switch on an
     account hides a rank. docs\H9_COMPETITIVE.txt.

 .relay <text>
     Says <text> to the whole community this server is in: every one of its
     servers and its page on havoc9.org show "[Tag] YourUsername: text", and
     lines written on the site come as "[Web] Name: text". Needs a havoc9.org
     account linked to your game (docs\H9_PRESENCE.txt) and proven, which takes
     a second or two after you join; an older H9XASH, a Steam client and a
     server with h9_presence_challenge 0 cannot prove it, and are told why,
     unless the community's owner switched CHAT NEEDS PROVEN PLAYERS off. One
     line every 2 seconds. The server sets the prefix (h9_community_prefix).

 /find <name>, /where <name>
     Where a havoc9.org player is, as far as they show it: the server and the
     address to type after connect, or on havoc9.org, in the H9 web client, or
     offline. This community's players come first. .find and .where too. A
     private community's server shows only to its members; to anyone else
     its players are just "in game". A player is found only where their game
     proved who they are.

 Members-only servers
     A community can keep its servers for its members. Anyone else is kicked
     with "This server is for members of <name>. Join at
     havoc9.org/community/?c=<short name>", and a Steam client always is: join
     there, with the havoc9.org account your game uses, and come back.

 Portals
     A ring on some maps leads to another server of the community; its name
     and players show as you come near. Press E (or walk in, where it says
     so) and pick 1. Go. H9XASH and the web client then ask you themselves:
     "Travel to <name>?" with YES (Y) and NO (N); only your YES connects
     (cl_h9_travel 0 turns these offers off). Elsewhere the server cannot
     move you, so it gives you the address: open the console and type
     connect <address>.


===============================================================================
 MATCH CONTROL  (admin, ADMIN_CFG)
===============================================================================

 WHO IS AN ADMIN. The admin commands here and below need AMX Mod X access
 flags -- ADMIN_CFG is the "h" flag -- which AMX Mod X gives by authid, IP or
 name in addons\amxmodx\configs\users.ini. On H9XASH a player has no SteamID:
 his authid is ID_ followed by a hash his own game supplies (ID_LAN on a LAN
 server), which cannot be trusted with flags, and a havoc9.org account is no
 authid AMX Mod X can match. Never give 127.0.0.1 flags either: web client
 players reach a game server on the same PC through h9web's relay and arrive
 from 127.0.0.1, so that line would make every one of them an admin. Run admin
 commands in the server's own console, or over rcon with an rcon_password set
 on the server. Server cvars such as amx_tsm_forcedoor are set there too:
 typed in a player's own console they never reach a dedicated server.

 amx_match_start
     Force the match live without waiting for ready-up. Warns if a side is
     empty -- rounds will not resolve while one is.

 amx_match_cancel
     FULL reset to warmup. Round counter to 0, score to 0-0, ready flags
     cleared, classes cleared, late-join lockouts cleared, everyone restored.
     If a match or its ready-up was under way, everyone then goes to the
     spectator team and the humans get the side menu again -- exactly as at
     the end of a match -- so sides are picked fresh.
     This is the "put everything back" command.

 amx_match_restart
     Score and round counter to 0, then start round 1 immediately. Skips
     warmup and ready-up.

 amx_match_score
     Print the current score.

 amx_match_continue
     Override a halt and play the match out with the sides as they are.
     Keeps the round counter and the score; amx_match_start would zero both,
     because that one begins a new match.

     A round HALTS when somebody leaves and the sides end up uneven by more
     than tsm_balance_gap. Everyone goes back to ready-up, that round is
     replayed from the start, and the rounds already won are kept. Play
     resumes when the sides are even again and everyone has typed .ready --
     or immediately, with this command.

     Only does anything while a match is halted; it says so otherwise, and
     amx_tsm_debug prints the halt state on its own line. The override holds
     for the REST OF THAT MATCH, so the next person to leave cannot stop a
     match an admin has already said to play out, and it is cleared when the
     match ends.

     Three cvars govern the halt, all documented in ts_matchmode.cfg:
     tsm_halt_on_leave (1 = on), tsm_halt_delay (grace period before the
     sides are counted, so rcbot can refill one first) and tsm_halt_bypass
     (1 = never halt at all).

 tsm_deathsounds 0|1           (console)
     The death voices and the machines' hit sounds of a roleplay map
     (tsm_rp_sounds) on or off, for you alone, as tsm_music does for the music;
     on by default, and with no number it flips. Remembered through setinfo, so
     it survives a reconnect when the game's userinfo has room (it is 256 bytes
     for every key, docs\CUSTOM_XASH_USERID.txt, the budget note; this key is 18
     bytes and is sent only once you have typed the command; with no room it
     holds for the visit). On a roleplay map each of those sounds is played to
     every player separately, quieter the farther the body is and gone past
     tsm_rp_sound_range, which is what lets one player leave them out; where the
     match mode runs they are emitted into the world as ever and this does
     nothing.

 tsm_music_skip
     The ambient music's next track, now, for everyone listening. The queue
     is one for the whole server -- every listener is sent the same track at
     the same moment, and the next one is armed from the length of the one
     playing -- so moving it on is an admin's, from the console or rcon. A
     skip for one player alone would be cut off as soon as the shared queue
     moved on; a player who does not want the music has tsm_music 0.


===============================================================================
 SPAWN POINTS  (admin, ADMIN_CFG)
===============================================================================

 Custom spawns are per map, saved to:
     addons/amxmodx/configs/tsm_spawns_<mapname>.cfg
 and reloaded automatically every time that map loads.

 The full picture, with the file format and how to move spawns onto tiers,
 is in docs/CUSTOM_SPAWNPOINTS.txt.

 Once a map has ANY custom spawn, the map's own spawn entities are no longer
 used at all. They are only a fallback for maps with no custom set.

 amx_tsm_spawn_add <1|2> [tier]
     Add a spawn for that team where you are standing, facing where you look.
     Custom spawns store facing; map entities cannot.
     [tier] makes it a FORWARD spawn. Left off, or 0, it is the team's normal
     spawn. A forward tier is used once that many siege objectives have fallen:
     the team's respawns then land only on its forward points, and the defaults
     are off until the round ends. Living players are never moved to a forward
     point. Nobody respawns on a tier whose objective has not fallen yet.
     On an Assault map the tier (1 to 32) is the respawn set of the capture
     point of that number: a side's waves land there while it holds the point
     (docs/CUSTOM_SPAWNPOINTS.txt, part 5).

     It refuses while you stand on one of the map's own spawn points: that
     position would become a custom spawn, and done for both teams the two
     would share it. Step off it first; tsm_spawn_prune 0 lets it through.

 amx_tsm_spawn_list
     Each point shows its tier. The numbers are the ones amx_tsm_spawn_del takes.
     List every configured spawn with its position and facing.

 amx_tsm_spawn_tp <1|2> <n>
     Teleport to spawn n of that team, to check placement.

 amx_tsm_spawn_del <1|2> <n>
     Remove one spawn. Use this to drop a bad entry without clearing the set.

 amx_tsm_spawn_clear <1|2|0> [tier]
     With a tier, only that tier's points are removed. Use amx_tsm_spawn_del
     to remove one point by its number, forward points included.
     Clear that team's spawns. 0 clears both.

     It clears MEMORY ONLY and says so. To wipe a map's spawns for good:

         amx_tsm_spawn_clear 0
         amx_tsm_spawn_save

     save deletes the old cfg before writing, so what is left is a file with
     nothing in it but its two header lines -- a real wipe, not a stale set
     that comes back on the next map load. Do not leave it that way: with no
     custom spawns the map falls back to its own spawn entities, which carry
     no facing and may not be where a match wants people to start.

 amx_tsm_spawn_save
     Write the current set to the per-map cfg. Nothing is persisted until you
     run this.

 tsm_spawn <team> <x> <y> <z> [pitch] [yaw] [tier]   (server command)
     Defines one spawn. This is what the saved cfg is made of. Hand edits are
     fine: docs/CUSTOM_SPAWNPOINTS.txt, Part 3, shows how.

 DIAGNOSTICS  (admin, ADMIN_CFG)

 amx_tsm_debug

 amx_tsm_teaminfo

 amx_tsm_whystuck [target]
     Dumps everything that can stop a player moving: our hold flag, FL_FROZEN,
     movetype, solid, maxspeed, velocity, origin, effects, and how often
     maxspeed had to be restored.

     along the floor and looks exactly like being frozen.

 amx_tsm_unstick [target]
     Last resort. Clears every hold, forces normal movement, and lifts the
     player clear of the floor in case they were made solid inside geometry.

 tsm_commands
     Every command this plugin has registered, in two groups: server-side
     (console and admin commands, run from the server console or an admin's
     own client) first, then client commands (what an ordinary player's own
     client sends). Read live from AMXX's own command tables.

 tsm_cvars [prefix]
     Every cvar a loaded plugin has registered whose name starts with the
     prefix (tsm_ when none), with its value: tsm_cvars tsm_live shows the
     live-gate settings. A cvar missing from this list is not registered on
     this server: the plugin it belongs to is not loaded, or the server was
     started before that plugin was built (cvars register at server start,
     not on a map change or amx plugins reload).

 WHERE A SETTING COMES FROM (the order it is applied in, the last one wins)
     the plugin's own default, then ts_matchmode.cfg and its preset (run on
     every map load, so a value line there is applied again each time), then
     the map's ts\mapconfigs\<map>.cfg, then rcon or the console. The newer

     so a value set in server.cfg or on the launch line stays: remove the // in

     stays, and a server with no name is called HAVOC 9 TEAM ELIMINATION.

 tsm_fogtest
     Toggles the sudden-death fog on or off, for looking at it without
     waiting for a real sudden death. Needs tsm_logging 1. Refuses outright

 amx_tsm_unfreeze
     Force-release everyone. Diagnostic.

 amx_tsm_fix <target>

 amx_tsm_cloak <target>

 amx_tsm_loadout [target]

 amx_tsm_menu <side|class>

 amx_tsm_ents

===============================================================================
 COMMANDS THIS PLUGIN INTERCEPTS
===============================================================================

 These are not for you to type -- they are TS's own commands that the plugin
 watches or blocks. Listed so the behaviour is not a mystery.

 tkw / tka / tki / rst_cfg / cashala
     TS's buy-menu commands. Refused when tsm_block_buy is 1, in every state,
     warmup included.

     This is only HALF of the block, and not the half that matters. Refusing
     these stops the menu saving a NEW loadout; it does nothing about one
     already saved in a player's config, because TS re-applies that from the
     config at every spawn and sends no command for this to catch. That is
     what tsm_nocash is for -- see NO ECONOMY below. Leave both switched on.

     The plugin's OWN loadout writes use these same commands and are allowed
     through by a bypass flag.

     With tsm_loadoutrework 1 a player whose class the rework covers is not
     refused: while his buy window is open the plugin reads rst_cfg, tkw and tki
     itself (tka is ignored: the weapon's ammunition is part of its price),
     prices them, and keeps the loadout for the match (it is handed over at his
     arm). Outside the window, or once his loadout is final, they are still
     refused, with a line telling him why. See LOADOUT REWORK.

 changeteam
     Refused while tsm_block_teamvgui is 1. It is the only thing that opens
     TS's team panel, and that panel is modal: it swallows every digit, so an
     AMXX menu shown while it is up renders and never receives a keypress.
     The plugin's own side menu replaces it.

 jointeam
     Watched, not blocked. Logged to tsm_debug.log so a failed join can be
     told apart from one that never arrived.

 h9vgui <id> <control> <value>
     The reply of an H9 game's panel (include\h9_vgui.inc), taken for the
     plugin's own panels only, tsm.*. Its side and class panels answer with
     menuselect, as the text menus do, so today it acts on none.

 usepowerup / powerup
     TS's powerup key, and the cheat that grants one. With tsm_no_powerups 1
     they are refused in match mode ("Powerups are off in match mode."), with
     two exceptions while a round is live: an Officer's goes through to TS --
     his kung fu IS a powerup -- and any other class with a perk gets the
     perk's own action instead, silently.

 drop / drop_<number>
     The T key, and the weapon bar's right-click, drop a weapon. Refused in
     match mode in warmup, ready-up, the freeze before a round and the seconds
     of a match's end ("You cannot drop a weapon until the round is live."),
     so nobody litters the map before a round; allowed again once the round is
     live. With tsm_enabled 0 (the game's own rules) they go through always.

 say / say_team
     Parsed for the player commands at the top of this file.


===============================================================================
 CHAT AND CENTRE TEXTS  (tsm_verbosity)
===============================================================================

 Every line the match mode says in chat starts "[MATCH]", and all of them go
 through one switch, tsm_verbosity (0). At 0 none of them is printed: round
 starts and ends, picks, refusals, kill reports, all of it. Players then go by
 what they see -- the HUD, the scoreboard, and the centre texts below. .status
 and tsm_loc answer in the console at 0. The class abilities' own "[TSM]"
 replies are not "[MATCH]" lines and stay.

 THE CENTRE TEXTS. Whatever tsm_verbosity says, an action of a player's own
 that shows nothing by itself is answered in the middle of his screen, in the
 centre style the siege refusals already used ("That is yours to defend.");
 the same text twice within 1.5 seconds shows once. They answer:
   - doors: an engineer door used by the wrong class ("Only a Combat Engineer
     opens this door.") or by the other side's engineer; a breach door that
     stays shut for anyone but the Breacher ("Only a Breacher can force this
     door.") -- a func_door or func_door_rotating in tsm_breach_entities,
     locked Use Only or named in tsm_breachdoor_1..8, looked at again a moment
     after the +USE, so one that opens for him says nothing -- and a Breacher
     at an engineer door ("This door has no lock to break.")
   - an engineer's +USE on the other side's turret
   - the side menu, .team and jointeam: a match in progress, a full side, the
     enemy wave's side, joining, spectating, waiting for the map vote, and a
     late joiner's wait for his side's next respawn
   - the class menu and .class: a full class, one not played on the map,
     already picked or locked, classes off, the pick itself, a pick held for
     the next respawn and the class that goes on, and the class given to
     anyone who picked none
   - .ready, .unready, .forfeit (with the vote's count), joining or being
     benched mid-match, the team lock, a short side filled with you
   - friendly fire on a teammate, a refused powerup, a refused weapon drop,
     .music
   - the medic's heal and the engineer's resupply, to both players
   - the Officer's .follow, .unfollow, .scan (to him) and .reinforce
   - .stim, .rocket, .cloak, .uncloak and the cloak running out, .light's
     refusals, .ammo, .turret, .pickup, .unstick and .roam
 What already had one keeps it: siege's refusals and the satchel, the
 medkit, resupply, scanner and reinforcement timers, a turret repair,
 "Breached.". Announcements -- a round won, a player eliminated, a tier
 raised -- are no player's action and have none.


===============================================================================
 CLASS IDENTIFICATION  (models and icons)
===============================================================================

 MODELS are barely usable for this, and the limits are in the mod, not here:
 a team gets at most 3 entries in mp_teammodels, each capped at 15 usable
 characters (a longer name overruns the next slot), and TS picks among them
 at RANDOM on every userinfo change via CTSGameRules::GetPlayerModel().

 Worse, the handler that does it is reached unconditionally, and writing the
 "model" key is what triggers that handler -- so a write from a pre hook is
 always overwritten. tsm_model_post 1 answers that by writing the class model
 from a POST hook, after TS has had its say, and superceding the re-entry our
 own write causes so the mod cannot answer twice.

 ICONS carry the same information without any of those limits:

     tsm_class_icons 1     a sprite above each head showing their class,
                           visible ONLY to that player's own side
     tsm_icon_height 52.0  units above the player's origin
     tsm_icon_scale  0.25  sprite scale; the sheet is 64x64 per frame

 Per-viewer visibility is done in AddToFullPack, which the engine asks once
 per (viewer, entity) pair -- the only place a thing can be shown to one
 player and withheld from another on the same frame. The hide is written into
 the entity STATE, never into pev_effects, so the real entity is untouched.

 sprites/tsm_class.spr is NOT part of TS. It is built by
 tools/make-class-icons.py and shipped in the package. CLIENTS NEED THE FILE
 -- with sv_allowdownload on they fetch it on connect, or serve it from
 fastdl. A client without it draws nothing where the icon should be.

 One frame per class, in class order, so adding a class to the table means
 adding a frame to the sheet and nothing else.


===============================================================================
 SUDDEN DEATH  (the last minute)
===============================================================================

 tsm_sudden_death 60    the last N seconds of a round go "sudden"; 0 is off

 Three things turn on at once, together, and go off at the round boundary --
 whichever way that round ends:

     1. THE MARKERS. Every player's class icon is shown to the OTHER side,
        in his own side's colour (MSR blue, Insurgents red, as in the X-ray's
        silhouette): the same sprite through the same AddToFullPack hook.
        Cloaked Recons are deliberately missed by this, because the marker is
        exactly the thing that would point at the man the cloak is hiding.

     2. THE X-RAY (H9XASH only). Every living enemy, cloaked or not, is sent
        to the other side carrying renderfx 64 -- a value GoldSrc ignores and
        the patched renderer (engine-h9-xray.patch) draws as a glow shell with
        the depth test off. He is sent wherever he is on the map, not only
        when he is in your view, so even a solid wall cannot hide him. The
        silhouette is in his own side's colour: Insurgents red, MSR blue.
        Teammates are never marked. It lasts the whole last minute;
        tsm_sudden_xray 0 turns it off. On a stock engine there is no X-ray
        and nothing is wrong: the icons carry the round on every engine.

     3. THE TOXIC BOG (H9XASH only). The engine's world fog is driven from
        four server cvars (h9_fog_r / h9_fog_g / h9_fog_b / h9_fog_density,
        see ts_matchmode.cfg), and the plugin holds them at swamp green for
        exactly as long as sudden death runs, zeroing them at every round
        boundary and every end of match. The whole map fogs: skybox included,
        every client, both sides. On a stock engine those cvars are unknown
        and do nothing.

 While sudden death runs every living player also loses 2 health every
 second -- nobody out-lasts the clock on a full bar. That has no cvar: the
 clock was already the thing meant to end the round.

 A SIEGE OBJECTIVE CAN LIFT IT. With tsm_siege_extratime 1, an objective
 that falls short of the last puts time back on the clock (up to
 tsm_addtime, never past tsm_roundtime). If that takes the clock back above
 the tsm_sudden_death mark, sudden death lifts for everyone: the markers,
 the X-ray, the bog and the health drain all stop, and waves run again.
 When the clock comes back down to the mark it starts over, announced as
 ever.

===============================================================================
 RESPAWN WAVES AND LIVES  (every class has lives; Assault has two)
===============================================================================

 Each class has tsm_class_lives lives per round, in class order: Assault 2,
 every other class 1 (ts_matchmode.cfg). A death is still an elimination: the
 voice, the kill report and the ghost are the usual ones. With lives left the
 player waits for their team's next wave, and the wave brings them back with
 the full loadout at one of their team's spawns. Running out of lives is the
 ordinary elimination.

 What the chat and the wave timer count is the lives a player still has to
 come back with. A death says "N lives left -- back in the next wave" (N
 includes the life he is about to play); the line at a revive, "RESPAWNED --
 N lives left", and the NEXT WAVE notice a living player sees
 (tsm_show_reinforce) leave out the life he is playing, so a player on his
 last life reads 0 lives left. The scoreboard's LIVES column, below, counts
 it.

 A mode that keeps ONE pool of lives for a side (Assault, below, which calls
 it REINFORCEMENTS) replaces all of this: a death draws its class's cost
 (tsm_class_cost) from the side's reinforcements, and tsm_class_lives is not
 used.

 A wave comes every tsm_respawn_wave seconds (60) and takes the players who
 have been dead at least tsm_respawn_seconds (5), so a death just before a wave
 waits for the next one. Waiting players see their wave timer. An Officer sees
 their team's wave and the .reinforce cooldown. While anyone is waiting they
 also see a list of them on the right of the screen. Someone out of lives sees
 no timer, because they are fully out.

 tsm_respawns 0 turns it all off: no wave is attempted and every elimination is
 final. tsm_respawn_sudden 0 (the default) means nobody waiting comes back in the
 last minute of a round.

 BIGGER MATCHES. tsm_scale_players lists player counts, each the start of a
 tier ("24,40,56" in ts_matchmode.cfg): a round that starts with 24 or more
 players on the two sides, bots included, is tier 1, with 40 or more tier 2,
 and with 56 or more tier 3. Each tier adds tsm_scale_limits (1) to every
 class's limit a side, a class without a limit staying without one.

 EXTRA LIVES. With tsm_respawns 1 a bigger match gives lives too: a class's
 extra lives at a tier are its tsm_scale_lives number -- one number for every
 class, or a list in class order -- times the tier's tsm_scale_lives_tiers
 number: the first for tier 1, the second for tier 2, the last for any tier
 past the list. A 0 in tsm_scale_lives leaves that class out; with
 tsm_scale_lives_tiers empty, a tier's number is the tier itself, so each
 tier adds tsm_scale_lives once more. The Siege setup (ts_matchmode.cfg and
 the Siege preset) gives every class but the Mech one more life at tiers 1
 and 2 and two at tier 3:
     tsm_scale_lives "1,1,1,1,1,1,1,1,0"
     tsm_scale_lives_tiers "1,1,2"
 Team Elimination's preset plays one life and sets tsm_scale_lives 0.

 PER MAP. tsm_class_limits and tsm_class_lives, as the configs and the map's
 own mapconfig leave them, are the baseline: read again at every round
 start, the tiers on top, so nothing is raised twice and a mapconfig's values
 are what get raised. Every one of these cvars can be set in a map's
 ts\mapconfigs\<map>.cfg for that map alone; the next map's configs set them
 back. A small siege map that should scale sooner, count the Mech too and
 give no extra life before tier 2 would say:
     tsm_scale_players "16,28,40"
     tsm_scale_lives "1"
     tsm_scale_lives_tiers "0,1,2"
 Chat says when the tier changes (tsm_verbosity 1); tsm_scale_tier shows it.
 A mode's own class rules (Mech Warrior, Wave Survival) are not raised.

 THE SCOREBOARD. While a match is under way the H9 client's scoreboard (reTS)
 has a LIVES column: each player's lives left, the one being played included,
 out of his class's lives ("1/2"), "-" on a side that never runs out (Wave
 Survival's mutants), and each team's header row the sum of its players'
 ("9/14"). ts_matchmode sends it in the H9Lives message as values change.

 SCORE. With tsm_score 1 (the default) a player's points for the match are
 his frags: the number every scoreboard already shows in its kill column and
 sorts by -- Steam's TS client and the GoldSrc package's as much as H9XASH's --
 and the server's player list reads too. Each side's points are the total in
 its header row (TS's own TeamScore), and keep what its players earned after
 they leave. TS's own frag for a kill is not added: the points are all there
 is. They are cleared as each match begins and stay up through the warmup
 after it. Every point is a cvar, so a map's ts\mapconfigs\<map>.cfg can set
 its own:
     tsm_score_kill 2         an enemy eliminated
     tsm_score_teamkill -3    a teammate eliminated
     tsm_score_assist 1       an assist (the scoreboard's A)
     tsm_score_heal 1         a medic's heal
     tsm_score_resupply 1     an engineer's resupply that gave rounds
     tsm_score_plant 2        the siege satchel set
     tsm_score_objective 5    a siege objective destroyed
     tsm_score_zone 5         an uplink zone taken (Capture the Uplink)
     tsm_score_carry 1        every tsm_score_carry_secs (15) seconds carrying
                              the uplink
     tsm_score_wave 3         a survival wave cleared while still in play
     tsm_score_capture 10     a flag carried home and captured (Capture the Flag)
     tsm_score_return 3       a dropped flag taken back to its pad
 Points are given in a round under way only. tsm_score 0 leaves TS's own
 frags, its kills, alone. Other plugins read them with tsm_points(id) and
 tsm_points_side(side), and a mode gives its own with tsm_points_award(id,
 TSM_POINTS_ZONE, _CARRY, _WAVE, _CAPTURE or _RETURN) (include\tsm_api.inc). With tsm_logging 1
 every award is a line in the debug log ("points: 3 +2 for an elimination").

 THE H9 SCOREBOARD with points on. The H9 client (reTS) heads the frags column
 SCORE and puts the real kills in a K column beside it: every enemy each
 player eliminated on this map, as A and D count this map too, with each
 team's sum in its header (ts_matchmode sends them in H9Kills, byte slot and
 short kills; slot 0 only says the frags are points). A teamkill is no kill
 there. Other clients show the points under their own kill heading. With
 tsm_score 0 no H9Kills is sent, and the board is the one it always was.
 Plugins read a player's assists with tsm_assists(id), and with
 tsm_assists_ranked(id) those on kills no bot had a part in (ranked play).

 SIEGE: A FALL GIVES THE ROUND BACK. Every objective but the last leaves the
 round going, and gives both sides back what a new round would, in the same
 round:

     tsm_siege_rewardlives 1   everyone's lives SET to their class's count
                               again -- never added to, so a full count stays
                               as it is -- and everyone eliminated, out of
                               lives or already waiting, comes back with their
                               side's next wave. Needs tsm_respawns 1; does
                               nothing in sudden death unless
                               tsm_respawn_sudden is 1 or the time below
                               lifts it first.
     tsm_siege_extratime 1     the clock runs back toward a full
                               tsm_roundtime, by at most tsm_addtime seconds
                               (150, 2:30) a fall, never past the round time.
                               See SUDDEN DEATH for what it does to the last
                               minute.

 The last objective ends the round as it always has and gives nothing back.

 SIEGE: THE ORDER THE OBJECTIVES FALL IN. The attackers take tsm_obj_1, then
 tsm_obj_2 and on, the next one only, unless the map says otherwise:

     tsm_linear_obj 1          numbered order, the default: a charge or a
                               gunshot counts on an objective only when every
                               lower number has fallen ("Objective 2 has to
                               fall first.")
     tsm_linear_obj 0          any order: every objective still standing is a
                               target, whatever its number, and the round is
                               the attackers' when none is left standing

 The cvar is read as the siege uses it, so a map's own
 ts\mapconfigs\<map>.cfg sets it for that map alone, and the next map is back
 on ts_matchmode.cfg's value:

     // ts\mapconfigs\h9_siege_<map>.cfg
     tsm_linear_obj 0

 In any order a fall that leaves objectives standing gives the round back as
 above, and the last objective to fall, whichever number that is, ends the
 round: it is announced as "the last one standing". The markers and the glow
 already show every objective standing, blue to the side that defends it, red
 to the side that attacks it, white to anyone on no side. One satchel burns at
 a time on either setting, and a forward spawn tier N (amx_tsm_spawn_add) is
 used once N objectives have fallen, whichever they were: with objective 3
 down first, tier 1 is in use, and tier 2 once a second one falls.


===============================================================================
 NO ECONOMY  (why weapons cannot be bought)
===============================================================================

 TS's equipment menu is SLOT-limited, not cash-limited, and it is client-side
 VGUI -- there is no server-side "buy" command to refuse. usecash 0 does not
 close it either: with the economy off, CashBased() is false, nothing is ever
 charged, and the menu becomes FREE.

 So the economy is left ON and the wallet is kept empty, and TS does the
 refusing itself with its own message. tsm_nocash 1 sets:

     usecash 1          economy on, so a purchase is refusable at all
     initial_cash 0     no cash at spawn
     cashforwin 0       no cash for the winning side at round end
     cashforlose 0      no cash for the losing side either

 and then holds every player's cash at 0 every frame, which is the part that
 actually guarantees it: a MAP can hand out cash directly through the
 victorycash and defeatcash keyvalues on a ts_wingiver entity, and those are
 baked into the .bsp where no cvar reaches them.

 The class loadout is applied down that same purchase path, so it would be
 refused too. The money is lent for the few frames that takes and taken
 straight back, keyed on the same bypass flag the buy block uses.

 The four cvars above are also set in ts/game.cfg, which TS execs at the top of
 every map load -- before any plugin runs, so the first map of a session is
 covered too.

 On a map that is not H9's (WHICH GAME A MAP PLAYS) the plugin puts TS's own
 values back instead -- usecash 0, initial_cash 1200, cashforwin 10000,
 cashforlose 1200, nopowerup 0 -- and holds nobody's wallet: that map plays as
 the game ships.


===============================================================================
 LOADOUT REWORK  (buying with TS's default equipment menu)
===============================================================================

 tsm_loadoutrework 0 (default) is the plugin as it has always been: every class
 is handed its kit and the buy menu is refused (NO ECONOMY, above). With 1:

 WHO. Every class except the Mech and the Heavy: a class with the Mech perk, the
 regeneration perk (the Heavy) or the M60 in its kit is left as it is and keeps
 its fixed kit. Bots are left as they are too: they take the class kit.

 NO CLASS KIT. A player the rework covers is never handed the class kit: he holds
 what he bought. A player who bought nothing holds TS's own default, his fists,
 and is told so (tsm_lr_kitfallback 1 hands him the class kit instead).

 WHEN. The equipment menu works in warmup and ready-up (on a side or on none:
 what is bought is kept, nothing is handed over yet) and in the freeze before every
 round (the whole of tsm_freezetime; the freeze that opens a match, where the
 class is picked, lasts at least tsm_lr_freeze). What a player holds when the
 round first goes live for him is his loadout for the ENTIRE match
 (tsm_lr_lock 1, the default): the arm and every respawn wave hand it over again,
 and later freezes and waves sell him nothing. A player who gets a side after
 the match has begun (through a halted match) and has no loadout can still buy
 until his own first go-live. With tsm_lr_lock 0 a player can re-buy in every
 freeze and, for ONE change, for tsm_lr_buy_seconds after a respawn wave puts
 him back on his feet. A weapon cannot be dropped while a live window is open,
 which keeps buy-then-drop shut as before.

 THE MENU. A player on no side is a spectator, and TS's own client opens the
 equipment menu for him (the first open of a map comes up on a random loadout:
 4 = Reset Config first). On a side the player is alive, which TS's client does
 not allow. An H9 client (the reTS client of this build; it says so with
 _h9vgui 4) is told by the server when his window opens and when it ends: the
 buy key (B by default, the command "buy") opens and closes the menu whenever a
 window is open, and the menu comes up by itself when a freeze begins -- except
 for a player who has bought a loadout already, and in the first freeze of a
 match until he has picked his class (the pick brings it up). When the window
 ends the menu goes down by itself. cl_h9_buyauto 0 stops the menu coming up by
 itself. Any other client is sent nothing: it buys before it joins a side, and
 not afterwards, and its chat lines say so.

 WHAT. The menu is TS's own: Buy, Remove Item, Reset Config, Random. What it
 sends is priced by the plugin with the menu's own prices (weapon, attachments,
 kevlar 1900; the stealth shoes are never sold) against the class's cash:
 tsm_lr_cash, or tsm_lr_cash_<n> for class n (the default class's until he has
 picked one). A loadout that costs more than that, holds a weapon on
 tsm_lr_deny that his class does not carry, weighs more than tsm_lr_slotcap
 slots or has more than 8 entries is refused AS A WHOLE and the player keeps
 what he had. Ammunition is part of
 the weapon: tsm_lr_clips spare magazines each (tsm_clip_bonus and tsm_clip_max
 apply as for a class kit). The menu's own money bar is 30000, not the plugin's:
 the menu will let a player build a loadout the plugin then turns down for its
 price, and says so in chat. A weapon on tsm_lr_deny is a class weapon: only a
 class whose own kit carries it may buy it (the Marksman's Barrett), and that
 class's cash is raised by its price, so the Barrett does not eat what he has
 for the rest. The laser rifle and the katana are on sale to every class.

 THE SLOTS. A covered class carries at most tsm_lr_slotcap slots (81, which is
 TS's own limit; 0 is no limit), and handing its loadout over does not raise the
 room TS allows a player. The Heavy, the Mech, bots and a class kit are given
 room past 81 as they always were. (If more than 81 is what you want for the
 covered classes, tsm_lr_slotcap 0 is exactly that.)

 KEPT. The loadout is the player's for the match and follows the player, not a
 class or a side. Picking a class fits it to that class's cash (what does not
 fit is left out, he is told which); the Heavy and the Mech ignore it. What he
 really holds when his first round goes live is what is kept (a weapon the game
 would not give is left out of it, and he is told). The record is cleared when a
 match ends or is cancelled, when the player leaves, and when he reconnects; one
 bought in warmup or ready-up survives the match's start.

 CASH. The figure in the player's TS wallet is what is left of the class's cash
 while his window is open, and 0 otherwise (tsm_nocash 1 holds the wallet).
 The cash is the plugin's own account: the game DLL charges nothing for a
 weapon, so it is not TS that refuses an unaffordable one.

 THE MECH. With tsm_loadoutrework 1 the Mech cannot drop its weapon, cannot take
 a weapon off the floor (the use key does nothing while one is within reach, and
 touching one does nothing), and a disarm is refused by the game library of
 this build and undone by the plugin if it happens: the weapon the game threw
 is removed and the Mech is handed it again at once, with the spare rounds it
 had.

 amx_tsm_loadout [target] prints all of it for one player.

===============================================================================
 RCBOT  (separate Metamod plugin, not part of this one)
===============================================================================

 Full reference: half-life/rcbot/rcbot_commands.txt

 rcbot addbot                         add one bot
 rcbot config min_bots 0              live value; player can always join (kicks a bot if quota > 0)
 rcbot config max_bots 20             live value; 10v10 bots total, +maxplayers 64 leaves 44 human slots
 rcbot config balance_teams 1         rcbot evens its own bots across sides
 rcbot autowaypoint <0|1|2>           drop waypoints as you walk
 rcbot waypoint on|off|add|delete     manual waypointing
 rcbot pathwaypoint ...               path editing
 rcbot debug mech 1|0                 log a Mech bot's weapon choices and pulls, with the reason (server console)

 Waypoints live in  half-life/rcbot/waypoints/ts/<mapname>.rcw  and are
 REQUIRED -- bots will not navigate a map without them. 45 TS maps ship with
 rcbot's own; the H9 maps carry theirs (h9_te_neuropolis, h9_te_meridian,
 h9_tsrp_hive and the three drainage maps).
 h9_tsrp_newbyzantium carries roam waypoints, laid in its public spaces only
 for the roaming citizens (ROAMING CITIZENS, below).

 ROAM MODE (rcbot-patch CHANGE 8, rcbot-ts-roam.patch). While
 tsrp_roambots.amxx runs citizens it holds tsrp_roambots_active at 1, and
 then every bot roams: random waypoints for goals, at a walk
 (tsrp_roambots_speed), a pause now and then (tsrp_roambots_pause), never a
 jump or a crouch, no pickups, nothing bought, and weapons only with
 tsrp_roambots_weapons 1. Its only enemy is whoever hurt it
 (tsrp_roambots_defend), fought with its hands until he dies or leaves or
 has not hurt it for tsrp_roambots_defend_time seconds, and, in an
 insurgency (tsrp_roambots_insurgency), the players
 tsrp_roambots_insurgent_team names, fought with the weapon in its hands.
 It is judged by how far it gets, not how fast it moves: about 5 seconds
 without getting anywhere and it picks another goal, about 12 and it asks
 the plugin to move it (tsrp_roambots_unstick); RCBot's own stuck check never
 kills it. While the plugin holds it (FL_FROZEN, two citizens talking) it
 stands and leaves its view alone. RCBot reads those cvars once a second;
 there is no rcbot command or config setting for it. The same patch lets
 waypoints reach +/-16384 across the map (stock RCBot drops any past +4096),
 for H9's big-world maps.

 ROAM TEAMS (rcbot-patch CHANGE 14, rcbot-ts-roam-teams.patch). With the
 teams in use an insurgent roamer's enemies are every player and every bot
 that is not on its own team, and never one that is: RoamIsEnemy compares
 the team field (pev->team) of the roamer with the other's. The teams are
 in use when the server gives the roamer a team: tsrp_roambots puts every
 citizen on team 2 while an insurgency lasts (team 3 otherwise) and ARP_TS
 puts a player on the team of his job (2 for an insurgent's). So the
 insurgents set on the Proletarians and the Middle Sea Republic's players, and
 on a bot of the Republic's team (the guards a later job adds), and leave
 the Insurrection's players and the other insurgents alone; a client no
 team is set on yet (a player ARP has not placed) is nobody's enemy until
 it is. With none in use the roamer's team reads 0 and nothing changes:
 only players are its enemies, as in ROAM MODE. tsrp_roambots_insurgent_team
 narrows the hunt to one team's players and bots (0: no narrowing). Its
 attacker stays an enemy whatever team he is on, so a citizen that a
 Proletarian player hurts (friendly fire is on in team play) still fights
 back. RoamIsEnemy is the one test of an enemy a roamer makes; a guard's
 own rule goes in it ahead of the insurgents'.

 POWERUPS (rcbot-patch CHANGE 10, rcbot-ts-powerups.patch). A bot walks to a
 powerup it can see, and sets off the one it carries when it sights an
 enemy. While the match mode is on, tsm_bot_powerups 0 stops both, whatever
 nopowerup says: RCBot reads it once a second, as a bot's own "usepowerup"
 never reaches a plugin, and ts_matchmode refuses a bot's touch of a powerup
 too. The plugin's default is 0: the bots are off powerups unless a server
 sets 1, which leaves them to nopowerup and tsm_no_powerups like everyone
 else. ts_matchmode.cfg and its presets keep the line commented out, so a
 value set in server.cfg or on the launch line stays (and a 1 in a map's
 mapconfig is not set back by the next map). A powerup the plugin would
 grant a bot is held back as well: an Officer bot gets no kung fu, and
 tsm_give_powerup gives a bot nothing (Wave Survival's mutants get their
 late-wave kung fu only with 1). nopowerup 1 (game.cfg) already keeps
 everyone off the pickups; this is for a server that gives its players
 powerups but not its bots. A powerup a bot already carries when this goes
 to 0 stays until it ends or the bot spawns again. TS's own kill awards come
 from the game library (mp.dll, TSBotKeptOffPowerups) and are kept from a
 bot too while the match mode is on and tsm_bot_powerups is 0: slowmatch's
 held slow motion, the Specialist's kung fu, super jump and held slow motion
 (it keeps the 150 health), and the slow-motion bubble and the killer's
 ghost-trail blur that a spectacular kill leaves, so a bot's kill shows no
 slow motion. A human's kill still slows what it can see, bots included. The
 One mode (mp_theonemode, 0 in the shipped cfgs) is not covered. Off the
 match mode (ts_ maps, roleplay, h9_dm_, h9_tdm_, ctb_ and ctf_) bots take
 powerups as the game ships. Needs rcbot_mm.dll built from all eight
 patches and an mp.dll with change 16.

 TS'S CAPTURE GAME (rcbot-patch CHANGE 12, rcbot-ts-briefcase.patch). TS has a
 capture game of its own, the briefcase, on a map named ctb_ or ctf_ (ctb_deadheat):
 a base for each of two teams, and a flag to take from the other's base to one's own.
 No plugin runs there (tsm_enabled 0), so rcbot reads what the game itself gives out:
 the TSFlag and ScoreInfo messages, the ts_briefcase bases and the loose ts_flag. With
 tsm_bot_stockobjectives 1 (the default; ts_matchmode registers it, rcbot reads it
 once a second) the bots of both teams fetch the other team's briefcase, the one that
 has it carries it to the middle of its own base, two escort him, two guard their own
 briefcase at its base, the nearest returns it when it is dropped, three hunt the
 carrier of theirs, with the restraint of the other modes (a quarter of a side is left
 to fight, a bot with an enemy keeps what it has). A bot too heavy to carry (TS asks for
 20 free inventory slots) is never sent for one. A bot cannot drop what it carries: it
 lets go by dying. It takes a map of exactly two bases, ownerteam 1 and 2, and rcbot's
 waypoints for it; it plays only while no match mode runs the map (tsm_enabled 0), and
 the bots must be put on the map: tsm_briefcase_bots N, or a tsm_bots line in the map's
 own mapconfig. tsm_bot_stockobjectives 0 puts the bots back as rcbot's stock game has
 them, which on these maps means every player an enemy.

 MECH WEAPONS (rcbot-patch CHANGE 13, rcbot-ts-mech.patch). A bot that carries the M60 and the
 Mech rocket launcher (weapon 41 of the game library) alternates between them in a fight; every
 bot of Mech Warrior is one. The M60 is the default. The launcher comes out for a target that is
 far (1.35 times tsm_bot_mech_near, 400 units) and in clear sight of a rocket, nearer for a group
 of enemies within a blast of each other. It fires its salvo: one press of the trigger, aimed at
 the target's feet and led, never at a wall in front of the bot or with a teammate in the line
 (nor with one near the blast when friendly fire is on). The button is pressed once, never held:
 the rest of the salvo leaves by itself, the bot changes no weapon while it does, and it pulls
 again only when the launcher's own delay (h9_mechrocket_rate, from the last rocket) and its
 reload are over; h9_mechrocket_salvo, _stagger, _rate and _reload are read for those waits.
 Then the M60 takes over, and has the near targets, the targets a rocket is no use against (a
 door), and the fight while the launcher is empty, spent or refused. The game reloads a
 launcher only while it is in hand, so an emptied one is reloaded in a quiet moment (no target
 for two seconds) and put away loaded; it stays in hand for a target too far for the M60 (1500
 units). A bot keeps a weapon for tsm_bot_mech_dwell seconds (3, and up to half again). RCBot
 reads the cvars once a second and learns what its bot carries from the game's own messages
 (WStatus, WeaponInfo, ResetHUD), so no plugin is needed for the play itself. The plugin side
 is three register_cvar lines beside tsm_bot_stockobjectives (rcbot-patch README, CHANGE 13);
 until they are in, the defaults apply and the console cannot set the cvars.
 tsm_bot_mech_rocket 0 puts the bots back to stock play (a launcher that is out goes back to
 the M60). "rcbot debug mech 1" logs every switch and pull to the server log. The same patch
 gives weapons 37 to 63 a place in rcbot's weapon tables: stock rcbot writes a bot's weapon 39,
 40 or 41 past the end of them. Needs rcbot_mm.dll built from all eleven patches and a game
 library with weapon 41.

 tsm_bots IS A COUNT: the plugin keeps that many bots on the server, adding one
 every two seconds through rcbot (rcbot's own fill is switched off, max_bots -1)
 and taking one away the same way when there are too many. It also sets
 balance_teams 1, because only rcbot can move its own bots in a way TS agrees
 with -- moving them plugin-side made bots shoot their own teammates and use the
 wrong spawn points.

 THE BOT FLOW. A bot is put on its side, renamed and given its class one at a
 time, through a queue: tsm_bot_flow_step bots (1) every tsm_bot_flow_interval
 seconds (0: 0.35 up to 32 player slots, 0.6 above, so 63 bots on a 64-slot
 server are placed in about 38 seconds). The first human to pick a
 side used to move every waiting bot in the same second -- with 63 bots that
 burst of team changes, name changes and respawns froze a 64-slot server and
 threw its clients off. A bot that already has its side, its name and its
 class is never queued, a bot that leaves is taken out, and a round does not
 go live while bots are still queued, for as long as their pace needs and at
 most 45 seconds ("Waiting for N bot(s) to take their places").

 tsm_bots 0 zeroes rcbot's quota and kicks every bot, a quarter second apart.
 A change in the console lasts for the map: ts_matchmode.cfg runs again as every
 map loads and puts its value back. To keep bots off, set tsm_bots 0 in
 ts_matchmode.cfg, or in a map's mapconfig for that map. On a map that is not
 H9's the plugin sets 0 for that map by itself, except on a map for TS's capture
 game (ctb_ or ctf_), which gets tsm_briefcase_bots (0 unless a server sets it).

 With bots on, the bots sit in SPECTATOR until a human picks a side. Filling
 starts from that point, so the first person to join is not dropped into a server
 whose teams were already settled without them. If nothing is happening and the
 server is all bots, that is why -- join a side.

 WHAT THE PLUGIN CAN AND CANNOT MAKE BOTS DO

 Can:  put them on the right side, name them, give them a class loadout, keep them
       supplied with ammunition, stop them damaging their own team, and order them
       to follow an Officer.

 Can:  tell them what is going on at the objectives. The mode plugins publish the capture
       points, zones, uplink and flags on their entities (docs\BOT_OBJECTIVES.txt), and
       rcbot_mm.dll sends the bots to them while tsm_bot_objectives is 1. Cannot make a
       bot drop what it carries: a fake client sends no client commands.

 Can:  nothing at TS's own capture game, which no plugin runs: rcbot_mm.dll reads
       that game itself (docs\BOT_OBJECTIVES.txt, section 5), and tsm_briefcase_bots
       only puts the bots on the map.

 Can:  stop them TARGETING a teammate, indirectly. rcbot picks its own targets and
       its team support for TS is broken upstream, so there is no way to tell it a
       player is a teammate -- but the line it traces to ask whether it can SEE one
       goes through the engine, and that is reachable. tsm_bot_blind_ff makes those
       traces come back empty, so a teammate is never a candidate.

       The trade: bots shoot through teammates at enemies behind them. Harmless,
       since friendly damage is refused anyway. Set tsm_bot_blind_ff 0 to undo it.

 Cannot:  make them reload or switch weapons sensibly. rcbot never saw the weapons
       we gave them, because the loadout is written through TS buy commands rather
       than picked up off the floor. tsm_bot_resupply works around it by keeping
       them from ever running dry.

 Bots are renamed after the side they land on: Enforcer 1, Insurgent 1 and so on
 (tsm_bot_names, tsm_bot_name1, tsm_bot_name2). Set tsm_bot_names 0 to keep
 rcbot's own profile names.


===============================================================================
 ROAMING CITIZENS  (tsrp_roambots.amxx, configs\tsrp_roambots.cfg)
===============================================================================

 RCBots that walk a roleplay city's public streets, plazas, halls, galleries
 and stairs at the pace of a player's walk key, stop now and then to look
 about or to talk, and never pick anything up or buy, so the city looks lived
 in. They keep out of homes, shops and MSR space because the map's roam
 waypoints do: they are laid in its public spaces only. Players can hurt and
 kill them (tsrp_roambots_god 1 stops that); a citizen someone hurts fights
 that one back with its hands until he dies or leaves, or has not hurt it
 for tsrp_roambots_defend_time seconds (tsrp_roambots_defend); a killed
 citizen is back at once in another district with another face, and its
 body stays where it fell.

 WHICH MAPS. Roleplay maps only -- rp_ or tsrp_ at the start of the name, or
 _rp_ or _tsrp_ in it, the same test that turns the match mode off -- and only
 those whose mapconfig asks for them. On every other map the plugin does
 nothing at all. It also stands down where a mapconfig turns the match mode
 back on (tsm_enabled 1).

 TURNING THEM ON, in the map's ts\mapconfigs\<map>.cfg, as
 h9_tsrp_newbyzantium.cfg does (h9_tsrp_hive.cfg too, with the first two):

  tsrp_roambots 1
  tsrp_roambots_count 32
  tsrp_roambots_weapons 0
  tsrp_roambots_models "proletarian_healthy:m;proletarian:m;prole_fem:f;prole_bfem:f"

 NAMES MATCH FACES. Each model carries its gender after a colon, ":f" or
 ":m"; one without is neither. A citizen's name comes from the list for its
 face's gender -- tsrp_roambots_names_f, tsrp_roambots_names_m, or
 tsrp_roambots_names for a face with none; empty, the plugin's own 40, 40 and
 16 -- one no other citizen has had on the map while the list lasts, and the
 new face it gets after a death is one of the same gender, so the name still
 fits. Citizens are named as they join, one every two seconds.

 The map needs its roam waypoints too, rcbot/waypoints/ts/<map>.rcw, and the
 places its citizens appear, configs\tsrp_roamspots_<map>.cfg (a district and
 a place on each line). New Byzantium's build lays both, from its plan's
 public spaces (export-map\h9_tsrp_newbyzantium\roamwaypoints.mjs). The
 plugin reads the mapconfig's tsrp_roambots lines itself as well, so they
 hold on a server that does not run mapconfigs; configs\tsrp_roambots.cfg
 holds the server's defaults, and every value goes back to them as each
 roleplay map loads.

 SLOTS. tsrp_roambots_reserve slots are always left free for real players.
 When people join, a citizen leaves (a dead one, or the one farthest from
 everyone); they come back one every two seconds as people leave. rcbot's
 own fill is switched off (min_bots -1, max_bots -1) while they run.

 GETTING STUCK. A citizen that gets nowhere for about 5 seconds picks another
 goal; after about 12 it is moved, quietly, as after a death but without one:
 to another district, with a new face -- once no real player near it can see
 it go. RCBot asks for that (the server command tsrp_roambots_unstick); it
 judges by distance, so a citizen walking into a wall counts as stuck.

 TALKING. Two citizens who meet -- within tsrp_roambots_talk_range, level, in
 sight of each other, on the ground and unhurt -- stop with the chance
 tsrp_roambots_talk, face each other and murmur in turns for 3 to 6 seconds,
 then walk on. A pair is asked once as they pass. The murmurs are
 sound\h9\murmur\murmur1.wav to murmur6.wav; each is precached only when it
 is installed, so without them the citizens still stop, silently. A hurt
 talker, or an insurgency, ends a talk at once.

 A WORD TO A PLAYER. Press +use (E) on a citizen within reach and it murmurs
 to you, from where it stands, and turns to face you for a moment
 (tsrp_roambots_use). Then that citizen, and you, wait
 tsrp_roambots_use_cooldown seconds before the next. A citizen that is
 talking, hurt in this life, or an insurgent has nothing to say.

 HOSTILITY ENDS WITH THE LIFE. A citizen fights back the player who hurt it
 (tsrp_roambots_defend). RCBot holds him for its attacker and lets him go when
 he dies, leaves, or has not hurt it for tsrp_roambots_defend_time seconds
 (tsrp_rb_forgive_seconds above 0 sets that time). This is in RCBot, and
 needs an rcbot_mm.dll built from the rcbot-patch source of 2026-11-03 10:04
 or later (rcbot-patch\README.txt, REBUILDING). A build before that, such as
 the one shipped until then (it has no tsrp_roambots_defend_time string),
 holds the attacker for the rest of the citizen's life: the citizens go for
 him again at his next life, and nothing but that citizen's own death or
 kick makes it forget him.
 The plugin keeps a record of the same thing (amx_roamhostile below),
 inferred from a citizen's health and the inflictor of its last damage, and
 ends a row when the player is back on his feet after a death, leaves, or
 changes team or class (only where something writes the entity's team or
 playerclass: the roleplay maps as shipped do not). tsrp_rb_forget_cmd then
 acts on the row: 0, the default, does nothing more; 1 sends "rcbot
 roamforget <player>" (a build of RCBot that has the command: any other
 prints that it does not exist); 2 sends away each citizen that was hostile
 to him as soon as no player has it in sight, and the plugin brings in
 another -- the way to make a citizen of an older RCBot build forget.

 INSURGENCY. .insurgency (below) turns every citizen into an insurgent: the
 insurgent version of its face (tsrp_roambots_insurgent_models,
 "civilian:insurgent" pairs), the weapons of tsrp_roambots_insurgent_weapons
 ("id:magazines" as the class lines), and RCBot sets them on the players --
 every player who is not a bot, or, with tsrp_roambots_insurgent_team set,
 only that team's (1 is the Middle Sea Republic's: its players and guards,
 never the Proletarians); with Apollo RP's team play running (TEAM PLAY,
 below), every player and every bot that is not on the Insurrection team,
 and never one that is.
 They take up arms one at a time, a quarter of a second apart, as the match
 mode arms its players: the first weapon listed is given last, so it is the
 one in their hands and the one they fight with. A killed insurgent comes
 back an insurgent; of everything TS throws down as it dies, only the gun
 in its hands is left (tsrp_roambots_insurgent_drop 0: nothing). .civilians
 puts them down the same way, one at a time, leaving nothing on the ground,
 and they come back as citizens. A mapconfig can start its map in an
 insurgency (tsrp_roambots_insurgency 1) or switch both commands off
 (tsrp_roambots_commands 0).

 TEAM PLAY AND FACTIONS. With Apollo RP's team play running (arp_teamplay 1
 and TS running its team list: arp_teamplay_on 1) a citizen is on the
 Proletarians' team (team 3) and, while an insurgency lasts, on the
 Insurrection team (2), in its team field (what RCBot compares) and in TS.
 A bot joins onto the team with the fewest players (mp_defaultteam 0), so
 the plugin moves each citizen to its team as soon as it sees it, before it
 has spawned; TS keeps it there through every respawn. The plaque of Apollo
 RP reads Civilian and Insurgency from that field. .insurgency sets every
 citizen's team field to 2 at once and moves each citizen to team 2 in TS
 as it takes up arms, a quarter of a second apart; .civilians moves them
 back to team 3. TS takes a bot that changes team out of play for its
 respawn time (5 seconds in
 server.cfg) and RCBot respawns it, so each comes back as after a death:
 in another district, with the new team's face and weapons, and with
 nothing of its old arms on the ground. Without team play none of this
 runs. Every bot the plugin runs has a faction (g_faction[]): a citizen, or
 a guard (the Middle Sea Republic's, team 1), which .insurgency and
 .civilians leave alone. No bot is a guard yet: new_faction() decides what
 a new bot is, and rb_team_of() says each kind's team. An insurgent citizen
 already takes a guard for an enemy (a bot of another team); what a guard
 itself fights is for RoamIsEnemy to say, in rcbot-patch.

 .insurgency  /  .civilians        (chat, roleplay maps; an admin with the
                                   flags of tsrp_roambots_admin_flag, "h" by
                                   default; empty: anyone)
     Start the insurgency, or end it. Everyone is told in chat.

 amx_roambots                      (server console, or an admin)
     What the citizens are doing: the cvars, how many models, names, spots and
     murmurs the plugin has, every counter (ticks, postthink calls, joins
     asked, citizens adopted and sent away, spawns, placements, pickups
     refused, weapons dropped, thrown weapons taken off the ground or left
     because TS had slowed them, stuck citizens asked about, waited for and
     moved, talks, murmurs, insurgents armed and put down, murmurs to
     players on +use), whether the insurgency's pace is running, team play
     (on or off, the moves between teams and those that did not take), and
     each citizen: name, model, alive, district, where and how fast, its
     faction, its team field and TS's team, and whether it is an insurgent,
     talking or facing a player. On a map that is not a roleplay map it
     says it does nothing there.

 amx_roamhostile                   (server console, or an admin)
     Which citizen is hostile to which player, as the plugin records it
     (inferred, not read from RCBot): the settings that govern it (defend,
     defend_time, forgive seconds, insurgency, tsrp_rb_forget_cmd), and one
     line for each pair -- citizen, player, how long since the last blow,
     whether he is alive, and when it ends (his next life, or the seconds
     left of tsrp_roambots_defend_time). amx_roambots counts the marks, ends,
     roamforget commands sent and citizens replaced.


===============================================================================
 APOLLO RP  (ARP_*.amxx; apollo-rp\ in TSM, ORIGIN.txt says from where)
===============================================================================

 Apollo RP, github.com/DouglasSherk/Apollo-RP, copied whole with H9's changes
 (apollo-rp\H9.patch): jobs, money and a bank, items, property and door keys,
 jail, robbing, NPCs, zones and roleplay chat. Its own help is in the game:
 arp_help in the console, /menu in chat.

 WHICH MAPS. Roleplay maps only -- rp_ or tsrp_ at the start of the name, or
 _rp_ or _tsrp_ in it, the match mode's own test. On any other map ARP_Core
 pauses every ARP plugin before they do anything: they stay loaded (amx_plugins
 lists them as paused) and touch nothing, not the map, the database or a
 player.

 ITS DATA. One SQLite database, addons\amxmodx\data\sqlite3\arp.sq3 (arp.ini:
 arp_sql_type, arp_sql_db), opened only on a roleplay map. Players' money, jobs
 and items are shared by every roleplay map; a map's properties, doors and keys
 are its own, in tables named after it: arp_property_<map>, arp_doors_<map>,
 arp_keys_<map>. Its NPCs, jail, rob spots and zones are files in
 configs\arp\maps\<map>\ -- without that folder the map runs ARP with none of
 them, and the core logs "Configuration directory missing".

 FILES. build.bat builds ARP_Core, ARP_Commands, ARP_Config, ARP_HUD,
 ARP_HungerMod, ARP_Items, ARP_JailMod, ARP_NPCs, ARP_RobMod, ARP_TalkArea,
 ARP_Zones, ARP_TS, ARP_H9Menu, ARP_Inventory and ARP_PropertyTax, lists them in plugins.ini with ARP_Core first, and
 installs configs\arp\ (arp.ini, arp.cfg, the maps' folders), models\arp and
 sound\arp. Edit them in apollo-rp\ in TSM: build.bat copies them over the
 game's. "build.bat apollo" builds Apollo RP alone.

 ITS MENUS. A player whose game draws H9 VGUI (h9_vgui 1, cl_h9_vgui 1) sees each of ARP's menus --
 /menu, /items, the shops, the doctor, the gunshop, the bank and ATM, the phone, the jail, the lock
 pick, the fine and job offers, the gun attachments, arp_config -- as a panel on H9's purple art, with
 the same number keys, pages (8 Back, 9 More, 0 Exit) and handlers; any other player gets the TS text
 menu as before. include\ApolloRP_VGui.inc, docs\H9_VGUI.txt 1e lists them. h9_vgui is the server's switch for every H9 panel,
 arp_h9_vgui (default 1) its switch for Apollo RP's menus alone, and cl_h9_arpvgui 0 the player's.

 ITS HUD. The HUD at the left of the screen -- the title with the city's name,
 the wallet, the bank, the payday, the salary, the job, and the lines other
 plugins add (jail status, the zone) -- is an AMX Mod X text HUD for
 every player but one whose game draws H9 VGUI: that player is sent it as
 panels on H9's purple art, in the game's fonts, in a user message, H9ArpHud
 (docs\APOLLO_RP.txt, 2). The second and third HUDs (arp_hud2: the needs, below;
 arp_hud3: the out-of-character chat) and the short notice (arp_hud4: what you
 look at, ammo loaded up; what you look at is the plaque, below) are panels too, each where arp_hudN_x and _y put the
 text HUD, to the nearest corner or side; the top panel of the left column
 stands under TS's weapon select bar. arp_hud_panel is the server's switch:
 1 (the default) sends the panels to the games that draw them, 0 sends everyone
 the text HUD. The player's cl_h9_arphud 0 (or cl_h9_vgui 0) gives that player
 the text HUD whatever the server says; the server's 0 gives it to everyone
 whatever the player says. arp_hud_refresh (10, 5 to 60) is the seconds before
 a HUD that has not changed is sent again; a changed one goes at once.
 arp_hudstate (a console command: the server's, or an ARP admin's, whose job
 has arp_admin_access) lists each player, which HUD he is sent and why (his
 _h9arphud key, the server's switches), how many messages he has had and the
 last lines sent to him, so a server can be checked with nobody at the screen.
 The log has a line when a player's HUD changes between the two and one when
 the first panel update goes to him. A plugin gives a HUD line a kind with
 ARP_AddHudItemKind (include\ApolloRP_HUD.inc: HUDK_TITLE, _WALLET, _BANK,
 _PAYDAY, _SALARY, _JOB, _STATUS; ARP_HUD does; ARP_AddHudItem is HUDK_EXTRA),
 which is what a player's cl_h9_arphud_<kind> switches act on.
 THE NEEDS. A need is a line of kind HUDK_NEED, the text "<label>: <0 to 100>%",
 in the second HUD: a plugin that owns one adds it from its HUD_Render handler
 with ARP_AddNeedItem(id, "Hunger", value). A player with panels sees a panel
 headed Needs, at the top right in the stock arp.cfg, with a row for each: the
 label, a bar (green at 0, yellow, red from 75, marks at 75 and 90) and the
 number; every other player has the line as text, "Hunger: 42%", in the second
 HUD's place, and "Starving" (a HUDK_STATUS line) under it from 90.
 ARP_HungerMod adds hunger while arp_hunger_enabled is 1 and thirst while
 arp_thirst_enabled is 1 (the plugin's defaults, which the shipped arp.cfg
 keeps), a row each, for a living player whose character has loaded. Thirst is a
 meter and nothing more: it rises about twice as fast as hunger (a point about
 every 45 seconds), is kept while the player is in the game and not stored (a
 player who joins starts at 0, and one who dies starts again at 0, as with
 hunger), and only a drink quenches it: Soda by 35 and
 Milk by 30, which still heal their hunger (a thirsty player may drink with no
 hunger to heal). It slows nobody and costs no salary; from 90 a status line
 "Dehydrated" shows under the bars, and hunger's effects at 75 and 90 are
 hunger's alone.

 ITS PLAYERS. A character (wallet, bank, job, items, keys, the property it owns) is kept under a key, the player's
 authid. On H9XASH the authid is made per server (ID_ and an MD5 of the player's id and the server's IPv4 address),
 so it changes with the server's address, and with it the character a player finds. A player whose game is linked to
 a havoc9.org account and proves it (h9_presence 1.9, h9_presence_challenge 1) keeps his: the table arp_ids lists
 each authid with the account it was seen with, the first authid of an account is its character's key, and a join
 under another authid finds it. No row of the other tables is changed: the table is made beside them at the first
 start, and going back is dropping it (apollo-rp\ARP_IDS.txt). The proof comes a second or two after a player is in
 game and up to 20 seconds after a map starts, so a player no row knows waits for it (the HUD says his account is
 loading) for up to arp_account_wait seconds (30; 0 leaves the account out, the authid alone as before) before he
 is made a character of his own. A game that cannot prove an account (a Steam client, an unlinked or older
 H9XASH, a bot) uses its authid.

 ITS JOBS, BY MAP. The jobs are shared by every roleplay map unless a map names a table of its own: configs\arp\maps\<map>\
 arp.ini with the line  arp_table_jobs "arp_jobs_<map>"  (read after the server's configs\arp\arp.ini; H9's two maps do
 this, so the Ministry of Defense ranks are theirs alone). Jobs go into it with arp_addjob <name> <salary> <access> on that
 map or with the map's own SQL file (run by ARP itself at map start, below). arp_joblist [first] (a player's console; 30 jobs to
 a page, then it says which number to type next) lists the map's jobs: number, JOBID, name, salary, access letters; /menu has it
 as Jobs on this map. A character whose job a map does not have is Unemployed there and keeps his saved job for the map that
 has it. docs\APOLLO_RP.txt, section 8.

 ITS MAP SQL. configs\arp\maps\<map>\<map>.sql is run by ARP_Core at every start of that map, after the tables are made and
 before the jobs, properties and doors are read: the rows are there in the same map load, with no admin step and no reload.
   arp_mapsql_autoload  (cvar, default 1; configs\arp\arp.cfg)
       1: run the file at map start. 0: leave it to arp_config (below). The core reads arp.cfg's line itself, because the server
       executes that file after the core has started, so the first map of a server run obeys it as well.
   Only SQLite, and only a file in which every statement is  INSERT OR IGNORE INTO arp_jobs | arp_property | arp_doors VALUES
   (...);  is run: the default table names in lower case, which become the map's own (arp_jobs_<map> where the map's arp.ini names
   one); one statement to a line of at most 1000 characters, at most 4000 of them; nothing after the closing ; (which may be left
   off); no other ; outside a text, no comment, no quoted name; the table's name once. A file with any other line is not run at
   all and the log says which line and why (the upstream maps' files, plain INSERTs, are such files). The rows already there keep
   their place and a start adds only the rows a table lacks, so it never duplicates and never overwrites. The log has one line
   for each start, [ARP] map SQL: N statements, E errors, jobs now J, properties now P, doors now D  (or why the file was not
   run: none, arp_mapsql_autoload is 0, not SQLite, line L ... is not an INSERT OR IGNORE INTO statement), and a line for each
   statement SQLite refuses (line L failed: ...). Players are told nothing. docs\ARP_CONTENT.txt, section 2 has the rule in full.
   arp_config  (a console command of a player who is an ARP admin, or an AMX Mod X admin with the ban flag)
       Opens the config menu. Entry 3, Load Map SQL Data, runs the map's .sql by hand whatever it holds (every line as it stands,
       the default table names swapped for the map's own); what it adds is read at the next map start. Entry 1 makes the admin an
       ARP admin, entry 2 changes the database connection.

 ITS COMMANDS. docs\ARP_COMMANDS.txt lists every Apollo RP command and what it does: chat (/items, /buy, /me, /shout, /cuff, /fine, /sell, /lock and the rest), police,
 property, the admin commands of the console (arp_createmoney, arp_createitems, arp_setjob ...) and the database commands (arp_backup, arp_restore, arp_delete, which DROPS
 TABLES), and the switches of arp.cfg. docs\ARP_ADMINS.txt says how to become an ARP admin and, in section 8, how to give yourself money, items and a job to test with.
 The colour of every H9 panel, ARP's included, is the player's own: cl_h9_vgui_color (and _r, _g, _b), under H9XASH CLIENT below.

 ITS H9 SWITCHES. Every addition H9 made to stock Apollo RP has a server cvar named arp_h9_* (or one of the older ones:
 arp_hud_panel, arp_hover_message, arp_hunger_enabled, arp_thirst_enabled, arp_mapsql_autoload), set in configs\arp\arp.cfg;
 with all of them off the server plays stock ARP. docs\APOLLO_RP.txt has the table of every addition and what its switch off gives.
   arp_h9_vgui  (cvar, default 1; configs\arp\arp.cfg)
       1: Apollo RP's menus are H9 VGUI panels for the games that draw them (ITS MENUS, above), and the inventory and exchange
       windows are available. 0: every Apollo RP menu is the classic AMX Mod X text menu, whatever the player's game and his
       cl_h9_arpvgui. Read by include\ApolloRP_VGui.inc (arpvgui_wanted), so every plugin that shows a menu obeys it.
   arp_h9_itemhover  (cvar, default 1; configs\arp\arp.cfg)
       1: a dropped item (classname arp_item) that a player looks toward from within 120 units is named on the notice HUD, "Soda x3",
       and "Press use (default e) to pick up" from within 50 units; a game of _h9vgui 7 with the HUD as panels is sent the item's
       picture too (a HUDK_ICON line, include\ApolloRP_HUD.inc), which its panel draws above the notice (cl_h9_arphud_icon).

===============================================================================
 ROCK THE VOTE  (ts_rtv.amxx, configs\ts_rtv.cfg)
===============================================================================

 Built on "Rock The Vote" 1.0 by Destroyer7 (AlliedModders, thread 351317),
 whose plugin it is; the original file states no licence.

 .rtv, .unrtv, .nominate [name], .unnominate     (chat; PLAYER COMMANDS, above)

 THE ROCK. Each person says .rtv once. When 60% of the people on the server
 have, rounded up, the map is put to a vote. Bots never count and never vote;
 spectators do, because between matches the match mode parks everyone without
 a side on the spectator team, and a ts_ map plays TS deathmatch, which has no
 teams to tell apart. The 60% is of whoever is here now, worked out again as
 people come and go, and a player who leaves takes his .rtv, his nomination
 and his vote with him. Alone on a server, your .rtv is all it takes.

 NOT DURING A MATCH. With tsm_rtv_in_match 0 (the default), .rtv is refused
 while a match is in progress: a round frozen, live or just ended, or a match
 halted for uneven sides with its score kept. Warmup, the ready-up before a
 match and the seconds after one are open.

 NOT ON A ROLEPLAY MAP. With tsm_rtv_roleplay 0 (the default), a roleplay map
 -- rp_ or tsrp_ at the start of its name, _rp_ or _tsrp_ inside it, the match
 mode's own rule, so h9_tsrp_hive is one -- has no map vote at all: .rtv,
 .unrtv, .nominate and .unnominate are answered "Rock the vote is off on
 roleplay maps.", and no vote starts there, the one after a match included.

 THE VOTE. Everyone gets a menu of tsm_rtv_maps maps (6) for 20 seconds; the
 number keys vote, 0 passes, and .rtv brings the menu back if a TS panel took
 it. Nominations go on first, up to tsm_rtv_nominations (4); the rest are
 drawn at random. Most votes win, a tie is drawn, and the map changes 5
 seconds later. A vote nobody answers keeps the map -- unless tsm_rtv_auto is
 1, when it picks one of the vote's maps at random.

 AFTER A MATCH. With tsm_rtv_auto 1 (the default), a vote starts by itself
 ten seconds after a match is played to its end -- a side reaching the win
 total, or a Wave Survival run ending -- with the map just played kept as the
 last choice, "Keep <map>", for a rematch. A match cancelled, forfeited or
 left with an empty side starts none.

 THE SIDE MENU WAITS. The match mode's side menu comes after the vote: it is
 not offered in the ten seconds before tsm_rtv_auto's vote, nor, while a vote
 runs, to a player who has not answered it with a map or 0, nor to anyone
 while the map changes to the winner. "The map vote comes first; the side
 menu follows it." says so, and the menu comes up once he has answered or
 the vote is over (ts_rtv's rtv_vote_running and rtv_player_voted; without

===============================================================================
 DATAGRAM OVERFLOW  (h9_msgstats.amxx, h9_staticprops.amxx)
===============================================================================

 "Datagram overflowed for <name>" (the server console, and logs\h9_overflow.log
 with the H9XASH engine): one client's update for one frame passed its buffer,
 49152 bytes (3 x the 16384 of MAX_DATAGRAM: engine-h9-framesize.patch; an
 engine without it has 16384). The engine throws that update away whole and
 sends the client nothing, so its world freezes, and the client has no delta
 base to work from: the next update is a full one again, and while the client's
 view stays as it is the same thing happens again, every frame. "Ignoring
 unreliable datagram ... would overflow" is the same frame with no room left
 for the unreliable messages after the entities. The queues the game and the
 plugins write unreliable messages into keep their 16384 bytes.

 What fills an update is the entities the client can see. A client is sent an
 entity it did not hold for about 10 bytes, a player for about 30, so a view of
 1,500 map entities, as in the open parts of New Byzantium, is 15 KB before
 one message has been counted, which a frame of 48 KB holds (the most the
 engine sends is about 31 KB: 2,048 entities and a player in every slot).
 Plugin messages are some hundred bytes an update at most. Rates (rate, cl_updaterate) change how fast updates go, not
 how big one can be.

 h9_msgstats [seconds]                (server console, or an admin: ADMIN_CFG)
     Counts for a while, then prints the report to the console that asked and
     to the AMX Mod X log: the 15 messages that cost the most, by name (a temp
     entity by its TE_ type), with their rate, size, estimated bytes to all
     their recipients, the share that is unreliable, and the busiest second;
     the entities each client was sent per update (the average, the most, how
     many were brush models and players), priced as new to the client against
     the 16384 a frame holds, and how many updates passed it; and the edicts
     with a model, by class. 60 seconds by default, 5 to 600. h9_msgstats 0
     prints what has been counted since the map began, at once. Run it on the
     live map while a client has the overflow.
     The message counting is always on (a few forwards, each a counter); the
     entity counting runs only inside a window.

 h9_staticprops.amxx (configs\h9_staticprops.cfg)
     Hands the map's ts_model props and env_sprite sprites that nothing can
     reach (no targetname, no target, no parent, no owner; a sprite only if it
     is one frame) to the clients once, in the map's sign-on data, through
     EngFunc_MakeStatic. The client draws them and the server stops sending
     them: about half of what a New Byzantium view holds. They are no longer
     entities of the game, which a ts_model, whose box is a point, never was
     in any way that mattered. It runs while the map loads, before the map's
     own config, so the switch is h9_staticprops in its own cfg, not a
     mapconfig's. A client already on the map when it changes sees no props
     until the next map.

 The H9XASH engine's two limits (engine-h9-entbudget.patch; a proposal until
 xash.dll is built with it, and then a server console answers them):
     sv_h9_entbudget <bytes>   default 10000. The entities new to a client in
                               one update are held to this many bytes (about
                               12 each, 40 a player); the farthest are left
                               out and come in on the next updates. Players
                               and what the client holds are never left out.
                               0: no limit.
     sv_h9_entdist <units>     default 0 (off). A map entity that never moves
                               by itself is not sent to a client farther away
                               than this; the eye is taken to a 128 unit grid.

 ORDER OF OPERATIONS FOR A NEW MAP

 0. Name it for its mode (WHICH GAME A MAP PLAYS; docs\GAME_MODES.txt has the

===============================================================================
 WHICH GAME A MAP PLAYS
===============================================================================

 The map's name decides, as it loads: h9_te_ Team Elimination, h9_siege_ Siege,
 h9_up_ Capture the Uplink, h9_ws_ Wave Survival, h9_mw_ Mech Warrior, h9_as_
 Assault, h9_ctf_ Capture the Flag; any other h9_ or tsm_ name plays the match mode too. A stock ts_ map,
 a roleplay map and
 TS's own modes under H9 names (h9_dm_, h9_tdm_, h9_ctb_), and a map
 named for TS's capture game (ctb_ or ctf_ at the start, _ctb_ or _ctf_ anywhere:
 ctb_deadheat) play as the game ships: tsm_enabled 0, tsm_bots 0 (a ctb_ or ctf_
 map: tsm_briefcase_bots), TS's own cash, powerups and weapon
 rules (weaponrestriction 0: the buy menu works), and the
 plugin picks deathmatch or teamplay rules for them before the world spawns. A
 tsm_enabled or mp_teamplay line in the map's own ts\mapconfigs\<map>.cfg
 decides in place of the name. The full list, and how to set up a map for each
 mode, is docs\GAME_MODES.txt.

 The server log says what each map got:

     [TSM] h9_te_neuropolis: an H9 map, the match mode plays, on teamplay rules.
     [TSM] ts_bikini: not an H9 map, the game as it ships, on deathmatch rules.

 A roleplay map -- rp_ or tsrp_ at the start of its name, or _rp_ or _tsrp_
 inside it, h9_tsrp_ among them -- is reported as "Roleplay": the server's
 game description, which havoc9.org's server list, the in-game browser and
 the other lists show in place of TS's "The Specialists (DM)", and the H9
 client's scoreboard heading for its list of players (H9Board), where any
 other map without teams reads "Free for all". The match mode stays off
 there all the same.

 Four things of the match mode reach a roleplay map nonetheless, each with a
 cvar of its own and each on by default:
   - the ambient music queue (tsm_music_rp), which the match mode alone plays
     elsewhere;
   - the death sounds (tsm_rp_sounds): a player or bot taken out of play sounds
     the voice its model calls for (death/male1..6.wav, death/fem1..6.wav; a
     name with robo, droid or bot in it, the Mechs among them, detonates with
     the frag grenade's boom, the droid power-down and an explosion sprite), and a
     machine that is hit clangs on every damaging hit, to its wearer on his own
     client too. No count, no report and no Officer's announcement: those are
     the match's;
   - the hands by the model a player wears (tsm_rp_hands): a name with repu or
     rh_ in it (tsm_rp_hands_msr) gets the msr hands, one with xccp or ins_ in
     it (tsm_rp_hands_ins) the insurgent hands, a droid the metal arms of its
     type (the insurgent droid, insdroid_bot, the tan ones, any other droid the
     msr droid's light white-blue ones; tsm_rp_hands_droid 0 gives every droid
     the msr droid's), and a Mech (mech_msr_bot, mech_ins_bot) the hands of the
     side its name gives and, for its M60, the minigun built into the arm. A
     proletarian model has hands and sleeves of its own, and shoes in the kung
     fu kicks: proletarian (and proletarian_dirty, tsm_rp_hands_prole) a bare
     mottled brown hand, proletarian_healthy (tsm_rp_hands_prolehealthy) a pale
     one, prole_fem and prole_bfem (tsm_rp_hands_prolefem) a dark glove, each in
     the model's navy sleeve with its cuff. A proletarian whose set is not there
     (a list left empty and nothing else naming him, or a file missing) wears
     the default hands, and any other model keeps them. The model is read again every half second, so a job that changes
     it changes the hands with it.
 h9_customhands 0 takes all of the hands away again.

 MECH WARRIOR  (ts_mechwarrior.amxx)
===============================================================================

 amx_tsm_mw                      (admin, ADMIN_CFG)
     The mode's whole state: its build, whether it claimed the map, the
     Officer's, the Mech's and the Combat Engineer's classes with their limits
     and lives, and who is still in the round on each side.

 One Officer a side on two lives, any number of Mechs on three, and the Combat
 Engineer at his class's own limit and lives (tsm_mw_engineer); every other

 below set the counts. The Engineer's .turret, .transporter and resupply work

 any mode.

 any mode. TS holds only 120 spare rounds of the M60's caliber (at the default
 ammocount), so a Mech is armed with those and the rest of the multiple waits
 in its ammo bay, loaded into the reserve as the Mech uses it up ("Ammo bay:
 +100 rounds" on its screen). The laser rifle does tsm_mech_laser_mult times
 its damage to a Mech, before the Mech's armour.

 A Mech's second weapon is the rocket launcher (weapon 41 of the game
 library, behind h9_mechrocket and h9_customweapons): one pull of the trigger
 launches a salvo of four rockets, 0.12 seconds apart, out of a clip of four
 ("Missile" in the ammo box), and each rocket's blast does 200 at the middle
 and falls to nothing 260 units out. The h9_mechrocket_* cvars in the cvar
 reference set it.

===============================================================================
 WHEN SOMETHING IS WRONG -- WHAT TO RUN FIRST
===============================================================================

 Nothing happens at all            amx_tsm_debug
 Cannot join a team                amx_tsm_teaminfo    (check TeamInfo msgs)
 Bots sit in spectator             expected until a human picks a side
 Bots never come back after a match  amx_tsm_debug, check tsm_bot_return
 A Breacher cannot open a door     the door is master-locked; see
                                   tsm_breach_entities in ts_matchmode.cfg
 Cannot move                       amx_tsm_whystuck
 A menu will not appear            amx_tsm_menu side   (check built=)
 Someone is stuck invisible        amx_tsm_unstick <name>

 A model is wrong or clients crash amx_tsm_teaminfo, compare mp_teammodels against
                                   the tsm_classN model fields. Every model a class
                                   can wear must be in mp_teammodels or it is never
                                   precached. tsm_model_mode teamonly disables all
                                   class models at once.
 Everything is in a bad state      amx_match_cancel

 A round stopped, score kept       somebody left and the sides went uneven.
                                   amx_tsm_debug, read the halt line. The way
                                   on is .ready from both sides once they are
                                   even, or amx_match_continue to play it out
                                   as it stands. See tsm_halt_on_leave in
                                   ts_matchmode.cfg

 Bots still shoot their own side   amx_tsm_debug, read the bots block:
                                   seen=0   the traceline hook never sees bots
                                   hidden=0 it sees them but never onto a teammate
                                   both >0  it is working; the cause is elsewhere

 A body vanishes on death          The body is NOT the player entity. TS has its
                                   own `deadcorpse` entity and spawns one during
                                   the death sequence, so the corpse persists by
                                   itself and this plugin must not interfere.
                                   Every attempt to hold the body was the bug --
                                   refusing the respawn delayed TS's corpse by
                                   exactly tsm_death_linger, so it appeared that
                                   late with its animation already over.

 Deeper death detail               tsm_debug_log 2, then read deathtrace lines in
                                   logs/tsm_debug.log. They show solid, nodraw,
                                   rendermode and renderamt every frame after a
                                   death, so whatever hides the body names itself.

 A splash MOTD does not show       the player's console says why ("H9 MOTD:
                                   ..."). The server's AMXX log has an "[H9 MOTD]"
                                   line when it refused an address. A player
                                   with cl_h9_motd 0 always gets the text MOTD

 A client's world freezes          "Datagram overflowed for <name>" in the server
                                   console or logs\h9_overflow.log: run
                                   h9_msgstats 60 while it is on (DATAGRAM
                                   OVERFLOW)
 Presence shows nobody on a server h9_presence_status in the server console
 Community chat or /find is silent h9_community_status in the server console
 A portal's Go shows no prompt     the player's console says why ("H9 TRAVEL:
                                   ..."). Steam and older H9XASH get the
                                   address in chat instead

 Verbose tracing: tsm_debug_log 3 writes per-tick detail to
 addons/amxmodx/logs/tsm_debug.log. Level 4 also echoes to the console.

 -- the Mech's minigun (class 9, weapon 32 the M60)
 The Mech's M60 is drawn as a minigun built into the Mech's own armoured arm (no hands) when
 models/h9/v_mechminigun.mdl exists and h9_customhands is on; the band on the arm is the side's colour
 (msr blue, insurgent green, default hazard yellow); everyone else's M60 stays stock. The Mech's rocket
 launcher (weapon 41) is a pod on the same arm. The Mech sees the one he holds in first person; no weapon is
 drawn in a Mech's hands for the other players, since the guns are part of his model (the pod's p_ model
 shows only in the hands of a player who is not a Mech). An M60 a Mech throws to the floor (tsm_mech_nodrop 0) lies there as the same cannon, models/h9/w_mechminigun.mdl, when that file exists and
 h9_customhands is on; any other player's M60 stays stock. The minigun has no p_ model.
 The ammunition belt on the left of the minigun is fed into the gun as the Mech fires: it slides one round into the feed
 throat at each shot (every shot starts the fire animation again) and rests between bursts, and it is long enough that its end
 stays out of the picture up to fov 120 on any screen shape. Both models come from modelling/mech-arms (its README.txt).
 The Mech's M60 shots have sounds of their own, played by the client from its fire event: sound/h9/mechminigun/start.wav
 on the first shot of a burst (the motor spinning up) and fire.wav on every shot after it; each shot cuts the tail of the
 one before, so while the trigger is held it is one buzz and the last shot's tail plays out as the spin-down. They are
 made by tools/make-mechminigun-sound.py, play at normal speed (the game's own fire sounds are pitched up four times)
 and are optional: without the files the M60's sound plays. Any other player's M60 sounds as before.
 Every shot of a Mech's M60 also sends a large muzzle flash (sprites/h9_minigun_flash.spr, else TS's muzzle flash) at the
 end of the barrels, and flies a bullet from there to where the round lands, in slow motion as the .turret turret's are:
 TS's bullet model (models/bullet.mdl) at 2000 units a second with a bright line along its path (the laser rifle's streak,
 sprites/laserrifle_trail.spr, 1.4 units wide, the turret's bullets have it too; TS's own bullet_trail.spr is too faint to
 show as a line and stays only where the streak is missing), seen by everyone. A shot is the game library's fire event for
 a round of the M60, which it plays for a bot as for a person.
 One Mech can have up to tsm_mech_trail_max bullets in the air at once (each is an entity while it flies): a shot fired
 while all of them are still flying shows its flash and no bullet, and none is made within 64 places of the engine's
 entity limit. Where the game lacks the bullet files the picture is a plain tracer line.

 THE WORDS are what the player said and nothing added: control characters and
 doubled spaces gone, a line typed all in capitals set in sentence case, cut to
 h9_floatchat_maxchars and broken into two lines at most. They rest 6 units
 above the top of the talker's head -- or 6 over the top of his typing bubble
 while it is drawn. The bubble is a sprite with frames of 128 x 96 pixels,
 centred fc_rise over his eyes and drawn at fc_scale, so its top is 48 x
 fc_scale over its centre (9.6 units at the default). The top of the head
 is read from the talker's model, not guessed from the hull: the bounding
 box of the idle sequences in the model's own file
 (models\player\<name>\<name>.mdl), which is 35 to 39 units over the origin
 for a human model standing and 23 to 26 ducking, and 48 and 52 for the Mech
 and the ED-209. His scale stretches it (a Heavy at 1.50); a model the server
 cannot read is taken to be 36 standing and 22 ducking.

 It shows a typing bubble over a player every time his chat line opens, and
 takes it down when the line closes, however it closes: Enter, Escape, the
 console, the menu, a map change. While the line stays open the engine says
 so again every 2 seconds, and a bubble with no word for 5 seconds -- or
 whose player dies, changes team or leaves -- comes down by itself, so one
 lost word can never leave it hanging. No engine tells a server that by itself
 -- messagemode and messagemode2 run on the client and are never sent on --
 so the H9XASH engine sends the client command h9_typing 1 as the line opens
 and h9_typing 0 as it closes (engine-h9-typing.patch), to a server whose
 game registered the H9Typing user message, as ts_floatchat does; any other
 server is sent nothing. A command and not a userinfo key: the engine's
 userinfo flood penalty dropped a key that changed at every chat line, so
 the bubble showed once and then not again for a while. An earlier H9XASH
 that sent the key _h9typing is still understood. A Steam client sends
 nothing and shows no bubble.

 .bubbles                        (chat, while the plugin is loaded)
     Float chat off for you alone, or back on: no typing bubbles and no
     words over heads are sent to you at all. Remembered by your
     h9_clientid or authid (data\ts_floatchat_off.txt), so it holds on the
     next map and the next visit. setinfo _h9bubbles 0 in your
     userconfig.cfg keeps it off on every server and wins over .bubbles
     while it is set; setinfo _h9bubbles 1 keeps it on.

 What the hudmessage sends a viewer who has it on: a talker's words only while the
 talker is within fc_range, in his sight and on his screen, for fc_hold
 seconds after each line, three talkers at most -- up to ten times a second
 while the words move on his screen and about five while they stand still,
 each about 25 bytes plus the line. A 40-character line is some 300 to 650
 bytes a second per talker on screen. A typing player's bubble is an
 ordinary entity, a few bytes a tick while it animates. A viewer who has it
 off is sent neither, and the server keeps no extra per-entity hook running
 unless someone on it has it off.

 WHO IS SHOWN. With the match mode on for the map (tsm_enabled 1), only a
 player it draws as he is: alive and in the match, the round frozen, live or
 just ended, and not cloaked (ts_matchmode's tsm_shown). Nobody eliminated,
 spectating, in warmup or ready-up, at a match's end or under the Recon's
 .cloak gets a bubble or words, and a bubble or a line already up comes down
 the moment its player stops being shown. With the match mode off, every
 living player.

 THE WORDS DRAWN BY THE CLIENT  (H9's reTS client.dll; docs\CHAT_BUBBLES.txt)

 A client with cl_h9_chatbubble 1 says so in its userinfo (_h9chatbubble 1) and
 is sent each line once, as the H9Say user message, in place of the hudmessage.
 It keeps each speaker's latest line and draws it in a box in the H9 panels'
 style (the banner art, the H9 Text fonts, the purple scheme) over the head of
 the speaker its crosshair is on, placed on its own screen every frame, in sight
 only, while the line is fresh; while the speaker's typing bubble shows, 6 units
 over the top of that instead, so the two never overlap. A viewer who has float
 chat off is sent nothing. Every other client is sent the hudmessage above.

 The userinfo _h9chatbubble is 0 for a game that does not draw the words
 (cl_h9_chatbubble 0), 1 for one that does and 1s for one that does and also
 asks for its own line (cl_h9_chatbubble_own 1). An older ts_floatchat tests
 the first character only, so it reads 1s as 1; a missing key, or a value that
 does not start with 1, is a game that cannot draw the words. The own line is
 sent to a game that asks for it and to every game with fc_self 1; the client
 shows an own line it is sent, in a box near the top of its screen.

 Heard by living human players whose game says it can speak (_h9tts 1; see the
 client cvars cl_h9_tts and cl_h9_tts_volume), within h9_tts_range of the
 speaker (with h9_tts_global 1: by every human whose game speaks, wherever he
 is, dead or alive), the speaker himself while h9_tts_self is 1; say_team by
 his team only. Never spoken: a line that starts with .
 / ! or @, the chat of a muted account (h9_presence), of a player who is dead
 or not drawn, and a line with nothing to say in it. The server sends the
 line as an H9Tts user message (byte slot, byte voice, byte volume 0 to 100,
 byte flags (bit 0 a gas mask, bit 2 a global line), string words, byte
 variant, char depth, char pan, byte droid), reliably, to each listener: 4
 lines at once, then one every 0.3 s, from all speakers together.

 THE VOICE is decided by the speaker's model name alone (the userinfo key
 "model"), never by a match mode, a class or a team, so it is the same on a
 plain TS server: a name with mech or ed209 in it is a Mech's (slower, lower);
 droid, robo or _bot a bot's (faster, higher; with droid in the name the last
 byte of the message says so, and the Kokoro voices speak it as a droid);
 fem, woman, women, girl or lady a woman's; anything else a man's.

 h9_tts_gasmask.cfg    the models that wear a gas mask, one name a line in any
                       case (a // line is a comment; name* is every model whose
                       name starts so, !name takes one out again): their man's
                       or woman's voice is muffled. A Mech's and a bot's never is. Read
                       as each map loads; it begins as every MSR-side model of
                       ts_matchmode.cfg's tsm_class1 to tsm_class9 without
                       the droid and the Mech, and repuheavy (mp_teammodels);
                       no insurgent and no stock TS model; it ends with the
                       wildcards repuheavy*, reputengu*, repughillie*, rh_agent_*
                       and rh_laur_* (without the two no-helmet models). The
                       Windows game filters the voice (a fourth-order low-pass
                       and a resonance, loudness kept; cl_h9_tts_muffle sets
                       the strength); the web client can only approximate it
                       with a quieter, lower, slower voice.
