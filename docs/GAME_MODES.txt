GAME MODES -- what each H9 map plays, and how to set a map up for each
=====================================================================

ts_matchmode is the core: rounds, sides, lives and respawn waves, classes, the HUD. Five
mode plugins sit on top of it, each on the maps that are its own: ts_uplink.amxx (Capture
the Uplink), ts_survival.amxx (Wave Survival), ts_mechwarrior.amxx (Mech Warrior),
ts_assault.amxx (Assault) and ts_ctf.amxx (Capture the Flag). They talk to the core through a
small API (include\tsm_api.inc) and do nothing without it.

Everything a mapper needs is here: what a map's NAME does, the entities and configs each
mode reads, and the in-game commands that write those configs for you. Nothing is needed
beyond the map editor and the game itself.


1. WHICH GAME A MAP PLAYS
-------------------------
The map's name decides, as it loads. TS picks its gamerules (teamplay or deathmatch) while
the world spawns; ts_matchmode sets mp_teamplay just before that, so the right rules are in
place from the first second, on a Steam hlds server and on H9XASH alike.

  Name                   Plays                                       Rules
  h9_te_<name>           Team Elimination (the match mode)           teamplay
  h9_siege_<name>        Siege (the match mode with objectives)      teamplay
  h9_up_<name>           Capture the Uplink                          teamplay
  h9_ws_<name>           Wave Survival                               teamplay
  h9_mw_<name>           Mech Warrior                                teamplay
  h9_as_<name>           Assault                                     teamplay
  h9_ctf_<name>          Capture the Flag (H9's own: two flags, two   teamplay
                         pads)
  any other h9_ or       Team Elimination, or Siege when the map     teamplay
  tsm_ name              has objectives
  h9_dm_<name>           TS's own deathmatch, as the game ships      deathmatch
  h9_tdm_<name>          TS's own team deathmatch                    teamplay
  h9_ctb_<name>          TS's own Capture the Briefcase              teamplay
  ctb_<name>, or         the same: ctb_ or ctf_ at the start of the  teamplay
  <x>_ctb_<y>,           name, or _ctb_ or _ctf_ anywhere in it
  ctf_<name>, <x>_ctf_<y>  (the stock ctb_deadheat, ts_ctf_<name>)
  ts_<name>, and any     the game as it ships: TS's deathmatch       deathmatch
  other name
  roleplay names         the game as it ships, whatever else the     deathmatch
                         name says: rp_ or tsrp_ at the start, or
                         _rp_ or _tsrp_ anywhere (h9_tsrp_hive)

CAPTURE THE BRIEFCASE AND CAPTURE THE FLAG are one game in TS. Its flag is the briefcase: a map carries
two ts_briefcase entities, one a team (ownerteam 1 and 2), and whoever carries the enemy's to their own
scores (reTS dlls\ts_briefcase.h: CTSBriefcase, "the CTF base + flag registry"). TS has no second flag
mode, and the stock game decides nothing from a map's name (the one stock map, ctb_deadheat, is teamplay
by its own worldspawn "gamemode 2"). So the name only decides what H9 does around it: a name of the
capture kind above puts the map on teamplay rules (mp_teamplay 1) with the match mode out, and the Discord
card, the website and the server list call it Capture the Briefcase (ctb_) or Capture the Flag (ctf_).

"As the game ships" means the match mode stays out entirely: no warmup or ready-up, no
side or class menus, no bots of its own (tsm_bots is 0 there, or tsm_briefcase_bots on a ctb_ or ctf_ map),
TS's own cash (usecash 0),
powerups and weapon rules (weaponrestriction 0, which H9's server.cfg sets to 1 for the match
mode: the buy menu works, as in TS's own team deathmatch), and the server browser shows TS's
own game name. TS's capture game (ctb_, ctf_) is such a map: team deathmatch with the flags. A map's own worldspawn
"gamemode" key (1 deathmatch, 2 teamplay, 3 last man standing, 4 The One) still wins over
the rules column, as it does in the stock game.

H9's OWN CAPTURE THE FLAG is another game: the h9_ctf_ maps, with the ts_ctf plugin's flags and pads
(section 2). It does not touch TS's briefcase game, and the briefcase game does not touch it: a map
named h9_ctf_ is H9's, and TS's capture game under an H9 name is h9_ctb_ (or a ctf_ name, which is TS's too).

Name a map for what it plays: the part after h9_ is also what the Discord card and the
server list call the mode.

THE MAP'S OWN MAPCONFIG HAS THE LAST WORD (section 4). Two lines in it are read as the map
loads, ahead of the name: "tsm_enabled 1" makes any map a match-mode map on teamplay rules,
"tsm_enabled 0" makes even an h9_ map play as the game ships, and "mp_teamplay 0" or "1"
picks the rules alone. A stock map can be played as a match without renaming it:

    // ts\mapconfigs\ts_bikini.cfg
    tsm_enabled 1

The server log says what each map got: "[TSM] <map>: an H9 map, the match mode plays, on
teamplay rules" or "not an H9 map, the game as it ships, on deathmatch rules".


2. THE MODES
------------

TEAM ELIMINATION  (ts_matchmode.amxx, configs\ts_matchmode.cfg)
  Two sides, MSR and the Insurgents, in elimination rounds: wipe the other side out. Each
  player picks a class at the freeze; every class has lives (tsm_class_lives), and the
  fallen come back with their side's respawn wave (every tsm_respawn_wave, 60, seconds)
  while their lives last. The last minute is sudden death (tsm_sudden_death). A match is
  best of tsm_rounds (9). Everyone readies up with .ready in chat; COMMANDS.txt has every
  command and cvar.

SIEGE  (ts_matchmode.amxx)
  Team elimination with objectives: the attacking side (tsm_obj_team) plants charges on the
  map's objectives in order (tsm_linear_obj 0 in a map's mapconfig: in any order), and the
  last one falling wins the round. Every fall but the last gives lives and time back
  (tsm_siege_rewardlives, tsm_siege_extratime). A map is a siege map because it has
  objectives, whatever its name; no mode may claim one.

CAPTURE THE UPLINK  (ts_uplink.amxx, configs\ts_uplink.cfg)
  Both sides fight over one carried objective, the uplink.
  The uplink   appears each round at the map's uplink point. Walk over it to take it; the
               carrier wears it on their back, keeps every weapon, and is marked to BOTH
               sides. It drops where the carrier falls, and goes home after
               tsm_uplink_return (20) seconds untouched. "dropuplink" in the console (bind
               it) or .drop in chat lets it go. A bot cannot type either: a player who presses the use key
               on a bot of his side that carries it makes it let go (tsm_handoff 1) and keeps that bot off it
               for tsm_handoff_secs (8) seconds, so that the player can take it.
  The zones    up to nine. Only the carrier takes a zone: tsm_cap_time (15) seconds inside
               it with no living enemy in it. Leaving, dropping, dying or a contest starts it
               over. Each zone shows a ring on the floor in its holder's colour (grey, MSR
               blue, Insurgent red; amber while being taken) and a flag over it.
  Lives        a taken zone gives lives back by tsm_uplink_rewardlives: 1 the taking side,
               2 both, 0 none. Lives are SET to the class's count, never added, and the
               fallen come back AT ONCE, not with the next wave. Needs tsm_respawns 1.
  Waves        on an uplink map tsm_uplink_wave (30), not tsm_respawn_wave, sets both sides'
               respawn waves; .reinforce resets to it too.
  Winning      holding every zone wins at once. A side wiped out (no lives, nobody waiting)
               loses at once, whatever it held. When the clock runs out the side holding
               more zones wins; equal is a draw. There is no sudden death.
  Seen         through the walls: every zone (its number, in its holder's colour, with a bar
               while the carrier is taking it) and the uplink (UPLINK at its post, CARRIER over
               whoever has it in his side's colour, DROPPED where it lies) are drawn on every
               screen, as in Assault (cl_h9_marks; docs\H9_VGUI.txt).
  Bots         play it with tsm_bot_objectives 1 (the default): a bot fetches the uplink, its carrier
               takes a zone, the others escort him and clear the zone he is heading for, and the other
               side hunts him. With 0 they fight and leave the uplink alone.
  Switch       tsm_uplink_mode 1 (0 turns it off everywhere). Browser name:
               tsm_gamedesc_uplink "Capture the Uplink".
  Admin        amx_tsm_uplink (the state), amx_tsm_cap_give <n> <0|1|2> (hand a zone over,
               to test).

WAVE SURVIVAL  (ts_survival.amxx, configs\ts_survival.cfg)
  Side 1 are the survivors; side 2 is a wave of bots that never runs out. A person picking
  side 2 is made a spectator. tsm_ws_human_side 2 swaps them: the people play side 2 (the
  Insurgents) and the mutants are side 1's bots, in the MSR models. The map's spawn areas
  follow the roles: the people still start in the survivors' area.
  Waves        a wave is over only when its last mutant is dead. Then a breather of
               tsm_ws_wave (30) seconds, counted down on screen, and the next wave comes all
               at once: the count goes up, xom_laugh plays for everyone, every fallen
               survivor comes back, every survivor's lives are set to their class's count,
               and each wave is a new round for the once-a-round abilities: the stim, .ammo,
               the class abilities and the Mech's rockets are all ready again. No round
               clock, no sudden death; the match bar and the HUD show WAVE n, never a round
               number or a tally of rounds. A fallen survivor who still has a life sees the
               respawn-wave countdown; nobody else is shown one.
  The run      ends when the survivors' side is wiped out: chat says how far they got (and the best on
               this map), the match is over, and it is back to warmup.
  Late joiners a person who arrives while a run is going takes the survivors' side from the side
               menu and comes in with the side's next respawn wave: its own countdown, or the wave that
               comes when every mutant has fallen, whichever is first. Until then he is a ghost, not
               counted as standing or waiting, so he never keeps a lost run open. A player may ask
               for another class with .class at any time and has it from his next respawn. Both are
               on in a survival run with no setting, and by default in every mode (tsm_latejoin 1, tsm_classchange 1; section
               LATE JOINERS AND CLASS CHANGES below).
  It grows     health +tsm_ws_hp_per_wave (5) percent per wave; guns by tsm_ws_gun_odds
               (40% at first, 60% from wave 16, all from wave 25); the kung fu powerup after
               wave tsm_ws_kungfu_after (30).
  Mechs        from wave tsm_ws_mech_after (25) each mutant has a chance of
               (wave - after + 1) * tsm_ws_mech_per_wave (1) percent, 100 at most, to play the
               Mech class instead of tsm_ws_class. It is rolled once as the mutant is put into
               the wave. A Mech keeps its class's health and M60: no wave health, no blade or
               gun, no kung fu.
  Overtime     a wave that runs past tsm_ws_overtime (225) seconds shows OVERTIME once, and
               every living mutant then loses tsm_ws_overtime_hp (2) health a second, with the
               sudden-death fog if tsm_ws_overtime_fog (1), until the wave is cleared.
  A stall      after tsm_ws_stall (120) seconds with three or fewer mutants left and none
               falling, they are taken off the board and the wave counts as cleared.
  Switch       tsm_mutants_mode 1. Browser name: tsm_gamedesc_survival "Wave Survival".
  Admin        amx_tsm_ws (the state), amx_tsm_ws_wave <n> (jump the count, to test),
               amx_tsm_ws_mechs (how many mutants play the Mech class).

MECH WARRIOR  (ts_mechwarrior.amxx, configs\ts_mechwarrior.cfg)
  The match as in team elimination -- rounds, respawn waves, sudden death -- with the
  classes cut down: one Officer a side on two lives, as many Mechs as a side likes on
  three lives each, and the Combat Engineer. Every other class is gone from the class
  menu, and every bot plays a Mech.
  Classes      found by their perks in ts_matchmode.cfg's tsm_classN lines (the Officer's,
               perk 4, the Mech's, perk 7, and the Combat Engineer's, perk 1), so
               reordering the classes does not break it.
               tsm_mw_officers (1, 0 for no Officer), tsm_mw_officer_lives (2), tsm_mw_mechs
               (0, no limit) and tsm_mw_mech_lives (3); a change counts from the next round.
               The lives need tsm_respawns 1.
  Engineer     tsm_mw_engineer (1; 0 leaves him out): the Combat Engineer is played at his
               class's own limit a side (tsm_class_limits) and lives (tsm_class_lives). His
               sentry turret (.turret), transporter pads (.transporter) and ammunition
               resupply (+USE on a teammate) work as in any match, and the resupply is what
               fills a Mech's ammo bay again. Every bot stays a Mech: only people play him.
  Mech ammo    a Mech's spare rounds are tsm_mech_ammo_mult (2.0) times what it is armed
               with. TS lets a gun of the M60's caliber hold only 120 spare rounds, so a Mech
               is armed with those and the rest waits in its ammo bay, loaded into the
               reserve as it uses the reserve up ("Ammo bay: +100 rounds" on its screen);
               .ammo and a resupply fill the bay once the reserve is full. 1.0 turns the bay
               off. In every mode that has a Mech, not only this one.
  Laser rifle  does tsm_mech_laser_mult (1.15) times its damage to a Mech, before the Mech's
               armour takes its share: a marginal bonus, 1.0 turns it off. In every mode.
  Rocket       a Mech's second weapon (weapon 41 of the game library) has two fire modes, which
  launcher     the fire mode key switches (the HUD says Volley or Single): the volley, the
               default, is four rockets 0.12 s apart on one pull of the trigger; Single is one
               rocket a pull. Its clip holds the four and the ammo box reads "Missile". Each
               rocket's blast does 200 at the middle and falls to nothing 260 units out (armour
               takes none of it), and the rockets fly 1100 units a second. He is armed with a
               full clip and eight spare rockets, a reserve of their own (the Barrett's caliber,
               not the M60's belt). In every mode; the h9_mechrocket_* cvars of the game library
               set it (COMMANDS.txt, CVAR REFERENCE).
  M60 shots    every shot of a Mech's M60 sends a large muzzle flash at the end of the barrels and
               flies a bullet in slow motion from there to where the round lands, as the .turret
               turret's are, with a thin white trail that follows it and fades out behind it (the
               turret's bullets keep the laser rifle's streak), whoever pulls the trigger, a bot or
               a person (tsm_mech_flash, tsm_mech_flash_scale, tsm_mech_trails and
               tsm_mech_trail_max, COMMANDS.txt). One Mech can have up to six bullets in the air at
               once.
  Bot Mechs    a bot that carries the M60 and the launcher alternates between them in a fight
               (rcbot_mm.dll, CHANGE 13): the M60 is its default; the launcher comes out for a
               target far off (1.35 x tsm_bot_mech_near, 400 units) and in clear sight of a rocket,
               nearer for a group, and fires one salvo at the target's feet; then the M60 has the
               fight, and the near targets. The emptied launcher is reloaded in a quiet moment.
               tsm_bot_mech_rocket 0 leaves the bots with whatever the game put in their hand
               (COMMANDS.txt, RCBOT and CVAR REFERENCE).
  Switch       tsm_mechwarrior_mode 1. Browser name: tsm_gamedesc_mechwarrior "Mech
               Warrior".
  Admin        amx_tsm_mw (the state).

ASSAULT  (ts_assault.amxx, configs\ts_assault.cfg)
  Both sides fight over the map's capture points, and a side's lives are ONE pool.
  The points   up to 24, each a wide circle on the floor (tsm_as_radius, 384 units, where an
               uplink zone is 128) with a ring in its holder's colour (grey, MSR blue,
               Insurgent red; amber while being taken) and a flag over it. Nothing is
               carried: any player of a side standing in a point with no living enemy in it
               takes it, in tsm_as_time (20) seconds for one player and half again as fast
               for each more (tsm_as_speedup), up to tsm_as_maxcap (6) players. A point the
               other side holds is worn down to neutral first, then taken. Both sides in it
               at once freezes it. A point changes hands as often as the fighting does.
  Seen         through the walls: everyone sees every point, wherever it is. An H9 game
               (H9 VGUI protocol 3) draws a badge with the point's letter in its holder's
               colour and a bar while a side is taking it, pinned to the screen's edge when
               the point is out of view, and the same badges in a row under the top notices
               (cl_h9_marks, cl_h9_marks_strip; docs\H9_VGUI.txt). Any other game gets TS's own
               objective marker at each point, its text the letter and the holder.
  Reinforcements
               what Assault calls the lives, one pool of them to a side: tsm_pool_base (16)
               plus tsm_pool_perplayer (4) for every player on the side, bots included, as
               the round goes live. A death draws its class's cost from the pool
               (tsm_class_cost: the Officer 2, the Mech 3, the others 1) and the player waits
               for the side's next respawn wave; a death with none left is final. The
               per-class lives (tsm_class_lives) are not used, and the match bar, the
               scoreboard (its column is titled REINFORCEMENTS and shows each side's, left
               out of what it started with, on the team's row) and chat say reinforcements,
               where every other mode says lives, and the class menu gives each class's cost
               (cost 2). Taking a point adds
               tsm_as_capture_reinforcements (5) to the taker's. Needs tsm_respawns 1.
  Respawns     the waves run as ever, tsm_as_wave (30) seconds apart for both sides, and a
               wave puts a side's dead back at the points that side holds. Spawn point set N,
               the forward points of tier N in the map's spawn file, belongs to point N: it is
               open to the side that holds the point, and closed again when the point is
               lost, or while an enemy is taking it (tsm_as_spawn_under_attack 0: a wave
               never puts the fallen into a point that is being lost). A wave lands at the held point nearest the enemy's base (the front),
               not spread over every point held, and its places are on the holder's own side
               of the point, out of the enemy's way in. A side holding nothing respawns at its
               own base (tier 0). The side that takes a point gets a wave at once
               (tsm_as_capture_wave 1). Round start and a living player are never moved.
               docs\CUSTOM_SPAWNPOINTS.txt, part 5.
  Winning      holding every point wins at once. A side with nobody alive and nobody waiting
               loses once tsm_roundend_delay (4 seconds) has passed, which is how a drained pool ends the round. When the clock runs
               out the side holding more points wins, then the one with more
               reinforcements; equal is a draw. There is no sudden death and no pool bleed.
  No rounds    Assault is one round: the first round that ends is the whole match. Its winner
               is the match's winner and is rated for it, a draw is a drawn match (rated for
               nobody), and the match bar and the chat say MATCH where the other modes say
               ROUND. h9_assault_rounds 1 plays rounds instead, best of tsm_rounds, as the
               other modes do; a change of the cvar takes effect within a second.
  Bots         play the points with tsm_bot_objectives 1 (the default): they go to the points their side
               does not hold, to the ones being lost and into contested ones, and leave a point that is
               nearly taken. With 0 they fight (a bot that stands in a point still counts as a capturer,
               or as a contester).
  Ranked       on an authenticated server a match counts on the match ladder as any other
               does (docs\H9_COMPETITIVE.txt): Elo for kills and the win, career levelling
               for everything. A match with bots in it rates nobody. The one round is rated as
               the round it is (the match says so to havoc9.org: it asks one round, and the
               five minutes).
  Chat         the mode's chat lines (a point taken, lost, the death's cost) show only with
               tsm_verbosity 1; at 0, the preset's value, the marks, the bars, the banners and
               the match bar carry it all.
  Reference    docs\ASSAULT.txt: every cvar, command and rule.
  Switch       tsm_assault_mode 1 (0 turns it off everywhere), h9_assault_rounds 0 (1: rounds).
               Browser name:
               tsm_gamedesc_assault "Assault". The Discord card reads "Assault - points: MSR
               2, Insurgents 1 of 5".
  Admin        amx_tsm_assault (the state), amx_tsm_as_give <n> <0|1|2> (hand a point over,
               to test); amx_tsm_debug prints the reinforcements and the spawn sets that are open.


CAPTURE THE FLAG  (ts_ctf.amxx, configs\ts_ctf.cfg)
  Each side has a flag standing on its own flag pad. Take the enemy's, carry it to your own pad,
  and it scores.
  The flags    models\tsm_flag.mdl (made with SMAK, export-map\h9_drainage\flag.mjs): a banner on a
               pole, standing on the pad or worn up the carrier's back, and it CHANGES COLOUR WITH
               POSSESSION. At its pad it wears its own side's colour (MSR blue, Insurgent red); the
               enemy that carries it turns it to the carrier's colour; where it lies, dropped, it
               is grey. Walk over the enemy's flag to take it. The carrier keeps every weapon, is
               marked to BOTH sides, and lets it go with "dropflag" in the console (bind it) or
               .drop in chat; it drops where he falls. A dropped flag is taken back at once by its
               own side walking over it (tsm_score_return for him), and flies home on its own after
               tsm_cf_return (20) seconds untouched.
  Scoring      a carrier standing on his own pad (within tsm_cf_pad_radius, 72 units) turns the
               enemy's flag in: a capture (tsm_score_capture, 10, for him), but only while his own
               flag is at its pad too (tsm_cf_needhome 1; 0 scores whatever has become of it). The
               flag goes home, and his side's fallen come back in a wave at once
               (tsm_cf_capture_wave). The first side to tsm_cf_caps (3) captures wins; when the
               clock runs out the side with more wins, equal is a draw. A second carried scores
               1 point (tsm_score_carry) for every tsm_score_carry_secs (15) seconds carried, counted as in
               the uplink.
  Lives        none are counted: both sides respawn in waves (tsm_cf_wave, 20 seconds) and every
               death waits for the next, so nobody is counted out (TSM_MODE_ENDLESS in the API).
               The wait reads RESPAWN WAVE IN 12s, with no count of lives, and the class menu says
               respawns.
  No rounds    one round is the whole match, its winner the match's and rated for it, as in
               Assault (h9_ctf_rounds 0; 1 plays rounds, best of tsm_rounds). A change of the
               cvar takes effect within a second.
  Seen         through the walls: every flag and every pad on every screen, a badge in the colour
               of whoever holds it: FLAG at its pad, CARRIED over the carrier, DROPPED where it
               lies (a bar draining to the moment it flies home), BASE on each pad (cl_h9_marks;
               docs\H9_VGUI.txt). Any other game gets TS's own objective markers.
  The pads     in the map: info_target entities named tsm_flag_1 and tsm_flag_2, at the floor
               where the flag stands. Or in the game, then saved to configs\tsm_ctf_<map>.cfg:
                   amx_tsm_cf_pad <1|2>   that side's pad where you stand
                   amx_tsm_cf_list        the pads, and where each flag is
                   amx_tsm_cf_save        write the file
               A first proposal for a map: node tools/assault-spawns/cli.mjs <map> --pads
               --overview <folder> (flat open floor to the rear of each base, drawn for a look).
  Ranked       on an authenticated server a match counts on the match ladder as any other does
               (docs\H9_COMPETITIVE.txt): Elo for kills and the win, career levelling for
               everything. A match with bots in it rates nobody. The one round is rated as the
               round it is (it asks one round, and the five minutes).
  Bots         play the flags with tsm_bot_objectives 1 (the default): they take the enemy's flag, carry
               it to their pad, bring their own dropped flag back, escort the carrier and guard their
               flag. With 0 they fight; tsm_cf_bots 1 still lets a bot that walks over a flag take it,
               return it and carry it, like anyone.
  Chat         the mode's chat lines (a flag taken, dropped, returned, a capture) show only with
               tsm_verbosity 1; at 0 the news banners, the carrier's status and the marks carry it.
  Reference    docs\CAPTURE_THE_FLAG.txt: every cvar, command and rule.
  Switch       tsm_ctf_mode 1 (0 turns it off everywhere). Browser name: tsm_gamedesc_ctf
               "Capture the Flag". The Discord card reads "Capture the Flag - captures: MSR 1,
               Insurgents 0 of 3".
  Admin        amx_tsm_ctf (the state). To test: amx_tsm_cf_give <flag side> <0|1|2> (hand a
               flag to a side's first player, or put it home with 0) and amx_tsm_cf_tp <side>
               <pad> (a person of that side walks onto a pad).

LATE JOINERS AND CLASS CHANGES  (ts_matchmode.amxx; tsm_latejoin, tsm_classchange)
  Core behaviour in every mode, each behind a cvar read live: 0 is off everywhere (the match
  plays exactly as it always has: a person who arrives mid-match waits for the next one, a class
  is locked once picked), 1 (the default) is on in every mode, 2 is on in the modes that ask for
  it. Wave Survival asks for both (its claim carries TSM_MODE_LATE_JOIN and TSM_MODE_CLASS_CHANGE,
  include\tsm_api.inc); no other mode does, so at 2 every other mode plays as it always did. A
  ranked or competitive server that wants the match as it was set up sets 2, or 0.
  The rule     a person comes in, or comes back as his new class, at the NEXT RESPAWN OF HIS
               SIDE. Where the side has respawn waves (tsm_respawns 1, a side a mode made
               endless: Wave Survival, Capture the Flag, the modes that run waves) that is the
               next wave of any kind: the side's own countdown, the Officer's .reinforce, a
               mode's wave, an admin's amx_tsm_wave. Where it has none (tsm_respawns 0, a mode
               that eliminates) it is the start of the next round. Never mid-round in a mode
               with neither, and never in sudden death: a wave in sudden death lets nobody in,
               he waits for the next round. A mode whose first round, or whose run, is the whole
               match and has no waves has no next round, so a late pick is refused there as before.
  A joiner     picks a side from the side menu (.team, or TS's own team menu: jointeam) like
               anyone before a match. The bots' side of a survival run is still refused, and so is
               a side that would break tsm_balance_gap (tsm_balance_mode block), exactly as in
               warmup. He may change the side he waits on with .team, or spectate. Until he is let
               in he is a ghost on his side: locked out of the fight as every arrival mid-match is,
               watching his side's players, told when he comes in. He counts as neither standing
               nor waiting, so he keeps no round open, and tsm_side_of, tsm_in_play and tsm_side_alive
               know nothing of him: no kill, death or round is his that he did not play, and h9_compete
               counts for him only the rounds he is let into. He leaves nothing behind when he
               disconnects first or the match ends first.
               When he is let in a wave takes him as it takes a fallen player: with his class's
               lives (tsm_class_lives; in Assault, where a side's lives are one pool, one life and
               nothing drawn from the pool or added to it), at the side's spawn points with spawn
               protection, the class's kit, health and armour, and the Officer's kung fu. At a round's
               start he is simply a player of the round, put on his feet, placed and armed by the
               round's own start; TS holds a player who changed team for five seconds before it lets him
               spawn, so a side taken in the five seconds before a round starts is let in at the round
               after (a wave needs no such wait: it asks TS again until TS lets him spawn).
  His class    the class menu is offered to him as soon as he has a side; the pick is held and goes
               on as he is let in. No pick: the default class (tsm_default_class); a class that is
               full by then gives him the first class with room, and he is told. A pick holds its
               class's slot from the moment it is made, so two people cannot both take the last Medic;
               a pick that would break a limit is refused with the usual message.
  A class      a player who has a class types .class for the menu again, at any time during a live
  change       round (and between rounds): his class is marked (yours), a pick already made (next).
               The class he picks goes on at his NEXT RESPAWN: the revive of a wave after a death, or
               the next round's start. Everything follows from the new class as from a first pick: the
               kit, health and armour, speed and height, the model, the perk (an Officer's squad is
               disbanded when he stops being the Officer), the class's lives (where a wave sets them, as
               Wave Survival's does, they are counted from the class he comes back as; a wave that only
               brings back what is waiting leaves the count he has), the scoreboard's class column, and
               the loadout rework's fit of
               what he bought to the new class (lr_fit: what no longer fits is left out, and a class the
               rework leaves out takes its own kit). He keeps playing his class until then. The pick
               holds the new class's slot at once and the old class's slot only until it goes on;
               picking again replaces it and picking the class he plays drops it. In the freeze
               countdown a pick is the class at once, as a first pick is. Bots have no menu and are not
               affected.
  Settings     tsm_latejoin and tsm_classchange, 0, 1 or 2 (ts_matchmode.cfg and the presets; the
               other rows of COMMANDS.txt). amx_tsm_whystuck <target> shows a player's late and
               held-class state (late=, class=, next=).

3. SETTING UP A MAP
-------------------
Build and compile the map as for any TS map, then:

  1. NAME IT for its mode (section 1): h9_te_, h9_siege_, h9_up_, h9_ws_, h9_mw_, h9_as_ or h9_ctf_.

  2. SPAWNS. Place info_player_deathmatch points in the map as for any TS map, then give
     each side its own, at least as many as players it will have (16 a side is a
     comfortable number): stand on each spot in the game, facing the way players should
     face, and use the commands, which write configs\tsm_spawns_<map>.cfg:
         amx_tsm_spawn_add 1 [tier]  (or 2) a point for that side where you stand
         amx_tsm_spawn_list          what is placed
         amx_tsm_spawn_tp <1|2> <n>  go to one, to check it
         amx_tsm_spawn_del <1|2> <n> take one away
         amx_tsm_spawn_save          write the file
     Without that file the match mode splits the map's deathmatch points into two groups
     by where they stand, which is only a guess. Leave 64 units between points: a player is
     never placed on top of somebody standing there. docs\CUSTOM_SPAWNPOINTS.txt has the
     rest (forward tiers for siege maps, pruning).

  3. WHAT THE MODE NEEDS
     Team Elimination  nothing more.
     Siege             the objectives: brush entities named tsm_obj_1 .. tsm_obj_9, in the
                       order they fall, and optionally tsm_obj_N_reset relays and
                       Engineer-only doors named tsm_eng_door. MAP_ENTITIES.txt is the full
                       account; amx_tsm_obj shows what the map has.
     Capture the       the zones and the uplink's point. In the map: any entity named
     Uplink            tsm_cap_1 .. tsm_cap_9 (an info_target's origin is a zone's middle,
                       tsm_cap_radius, 128, units round it; a brush entity's box is its
                       footprint) and an info_target named tsm_uplink. Or in the game, then
                       saved to configs\tsm_uplink_<map>.cfg:
                           amx_tsm_cap_add [n] [radius]   a zone where you stand
                           amx_tsm_cap_del <n>            remove one
                           amx_tsm_cap_list               the zones and who holds them
                           amx_tsm_uplink_set             the uplink's point where you stand
                           amx_tsm_uplink_save            write the file
                       Put the zones where both sides have to fight for them, and the
                       uplink's point between the two sides' starts, the same distance
                       from each.
     Capture the       TS's own game, no plugin: two ts_briefcase brush entities, one a side
     Briefcase or      (the brush is the base and the capture trigger; it is invisible),
     Flag              ownerteam 1 and 2, contents "the red flag" (what its flag is called
                       in the pickup line; the default is "a flag"), message "Red scores!"
                       (shown when it is captured), and a target if a capture should fire
                       something. worldspawn: gamemode 2 (teamplay) and mapteams "Blue;Red",
                       info_player_team1 and info_player_team2 for the sides. A side's flag
                       starts at the middle of its brush; an enemy takes it by touch (one flag
                       at a time) and carries it to his own side's brush, where it scores 10
                       for him and a point for his team, but only while his own flag is home.
                       A dropped flag lies 20 seconds and goes home, and its own side returns
                       it sooner by touching it (5 for the returner). The flag is a
                       briefcase (models\briefcase.mdl, tinted with its side's colour): TS
                       has one such game and no second flag mode. Name the map ctb_<name> or
                       ctf_<name> (or h9_ctb_): the match mode then stays out.
                       The stock ctb_deadheat is the worked example.
                       Bots: tsm_briefcase_bots N puts N on the map and they play the briefcase
                       game (docs\BOT_OBJECTIVES.txt, section 5); give the map rcbot waypoints.
     Wave Survival     the name alone. Spawns: side 1 (the survivors) somewhere they can
                       hold; side 2 (the mutants) where waves should come from, far enough
                       away that a wave arrives rather than appears. How many mutants a wave
                       has is tsm_bots, so set it in the mapconfig.
     Mech Warrior      the name alone. Mechs are slow, so give the round time
                       (tsm_roundtime) in the mapconfig.
     Assault           the points. In the map: any entity named tsm_as_1 .. tsm_as_24 (an
                       info_target's origin is a point's middle, tsm_as_radius, 384, units
                       round it; a brush entity's box is its footprint). Or in the game, then
                       saved to configs\tsm_assault_<map>.cfg:
                           amx_tsm_as_add [n] [radius]   a point where you stand
                           amx_tsm_as_del <n>            remove one
                           amx_tsm_as_radius <n> <r>     how far one reaches
                           amx_tsm_as_list               the points and who holds them
                           amx_tsm_as_save               write the file
                       Then each point's respawn set: stand where a side should come back
                       when it holds point N, and amx_tsm_spawn_add <team> N. At least two
                       points make the map an assault map. Put the points where both sides
                       have to fight for them, more of them than a round can hold at once,
                       and a side's own base outside any of them. A map that is a copy of an
                       Uplink map still carries that map's zones: tsm_uplink_mode 0 in its
                       mapconfig (the shipped h9_as_ maps have it).
     Capture the Flag  the two pads: info_target entities tsm_flag_1 and tsm_flag_2 in the map, or
                       amx_tsm_cf_pad <1|2> where each flag should stand and amx_tsm_cf_save
                       (configs\tsm_ctf_<map>.cfg). Put each at the rear of its side's base, on
                       flat open floor with room round it for the carrier to be seen and
                       to reach. A copy of an Uplink map keeps that map's zones: tsm_uplink_mode 0
                       in its mapconfig (h9_ctf_drainage has it).

  4. A MAPCONFIG (section 4) for anything this map plays differently: respawns, round time,
     how many bots.

  5. BOTS need waypoints to find their way: rcbot\waypoints\ts\<map>.rcw. Make them with
     rcbot's own waypoint editor in the game (its documentation is in the rcbot folder).
     Without them bots stand around.

  6. CONTENT a player may not have -- a sky, sounds, models, sprites, a wad -- goes in a
     <map>.res file beside the .bsp, one path per line relative to ts\ (gfx/env/<sky>rt.tga
     and so on), so the server sends it with the map. Textures can instead be put inside the
     .bsp by the compiler (-wadinclude).

  7. TEST IT: load the map, check the server log's line for which game it plays, and use
     amx_tsm_debug (the core), amx_tsm_obj (siege), amx_tsm_uplink (uplink), amx_tsm_ws
     (survival), amx_tsm_mw (Mech Warrior), amx_tsm_assault (Assault) or amx_tsm_ctf (Capture the Flag) to see the mode's state. tsm_test_solo 1 lets a
     match run with only one side filled.

  8. THE DISCORD CARD shows a picture of the map when the HAVOC 9 Discord application has an
     art asset named after the map (docs\RICH_PRESENCE.txt).


4. PER-MAP CONFIGS
------------------
  ts\mapconfigs\<map>.cfg   any console commands and cvars, run by the H9XASH engine once the
                            map and its plugins are up (h9_autoconfig 1), so they override the
                            plugins' own configs for that map alone; the next map's configs
                            set everything back. Its tsm_enabled and mp_teamplay lines are also
                            read as the map loads (section 1). A Steam hlds server does not run
                            these files.
  configs\tsm_spawns_<map>.cfg    the spawn points (amx_tsm_spawn_save writes it).
  configs\tsm_uplink_<map>.cfg    the uplink's zones and point (amx_tsm_uplink_save):
                                  tsm_cap <n> <x> <y> <z> [radius] and tsm_uplink_at <x> <y> <z>.
  configs\tsm_assault_<map>.cfg   the capture points (amx_tsm_as_save): tsm_as_zone <n> <x> <y>
                                  <z> [radius].
  H9XASH\presets            h9_presets.bat swaps whole profiles: the .Siege copies of
                            ts_uplink.cfg, ts_survival.cfg, ts_mechwarrior.cfg and ts_assault.cfg have their mode
                            on, the .TeamElimination copies have it off, and the .Assault ones have only Assault
                            on (with the siege chain stood down in ts_matchmode.Assault.cfg; AMX_PRESETS.txt).

  A mapconfig, for example, ts\mapconfigs\h9_up_drainage.cfg:

      echo "[h9_autoconfig] h9_up_drainage.cfg applied"
      tsm_respawns 1
      tsm_roundtime 420


5. THE DRAINAGE MAPS  (export-map\h9_drainage)
----------------------------------------------
h9_up_drainage, h9_ws_drainage and h9_mw_drainage are one place: the city's storm-drain
works at night, a canal 704 deep with a basin at each end, two box culverts with open decks
over them, a pumping station in the middle, service galleries inside the walls, a filter
room and a generator room. Rain falls from high over the city's toxic storm sky, dirty haze
lies along the floors, and litter is everywhere. The sky gives little light; the lamps give
the rest, their lit faces shining. Over each basin the city's elevated line crosses on a
steel bridge between two tunnels, and every two minutes or so a train comes through: the
north basin's waits 110 seconds in its tunnel between crossings and the south's 136, so the
two keep no beat, and a crossing takes about 7 seconds. The train makes no sound of its own.
What is heard is the rumble, TS's own sound\ambience\cg_rdmsubway.wav at full volume and
92 percent pitch, from the middle of the bridge, within about 1250 units: it starts a second
and a half before the train leaves its tunnel, and as the train leaves, the ground shakes
under everyone within 1100 units of the bridge's middle.

  h9_up_drainage   MSR on the north basin's platform, the Insurgents on the south's (16
                   each). Zones: 1 the north culvert, 2 the filter room, 3 the south culvert,
                   4 the generator room; the uplink in the station. Mapconfig:
                   tsm_respawns 1, tsm_roundtime 420.
  h9_ws_drainage   16 survivors in the station; mutants from both platforms and the
                   galleries' far ends. Mapconfig: tsm_respawns 1, tsm_bots 12.
  h9_mw_drainage   the sides start as on h9_up_drainage, with no zones. Mapconfig:
                   tsm_respawns 1, tsm_roundtime 420.
  h9_as_drainage   Assault, a copy of h9_up_drainage (tools\make_assault_maps.py): the sides
                   start as there, and four capture points, every one over a thousand units
                   from every other: 1 the north culvert (reach 256), 2 the filter room (192),
                   3 the south culvert (256), 4 the generator room (192)
                   (configs\tsm_assault_h9_as_drainage.cfg). Mapconfig:
                   tsm_uplink_mode 0, tsm_respawns 1, tsm_roundtime 720.
  h9_ctf_drainage  Capture the Flag, a copy of h9_up_drainage (tools\make_assault_maps.py): the sides
                   start as there, and each side's flag stands on a pad at the rear of its base,
                   the north end and the south (configs\tsm_ctf_h9_ctf_drainage.cfg, proposed by
                   node tools/assault-spawns/cli.mjs h9_up_drainage --pads). Mapconfig:
                   tsm_uplink_mode 0, tsm_respawns 1, tsm_roundtime 600.

  Rebuilding them  in export-map\h9_drainage (node, SMAK beside TSM):
    node sky.mjs deploy          the sky (gfx\env\h9drain*.tga)
    node sprites.mjs deploy      the zone, flag, marker, rain and haze sprites
    node uplink.mjs deploy       models\tsm_uplink.mdl
    node flag.mjs deploy         models\tsm_flag.mdl (Capture the Flag's flag: two bodies, three skins)
    node drainage.mjs --compile deploy   all three maps, their .res and spawn configs
    node waypoints.mjs deploy    the bots' waypoints, under all three names
    node preview.mjs             a reel of stills and a flythrough with its sound

6. THE SKYLIFT  (export-map\h9_siege_lift)
------------------------------------------
A skylift car climbing its tether to orbit over the burning city: one car, four maps, as
the drainage works are five. The world outside streams down past the windows (conveyor
cloud cards, the tether, drifting cloud banks) under a still, toxic red sky; red radiated
haze clings to the gantry and the dock; the car groans, creaks and sways, everyone's view
rolling with it.

  h9_siege_lift  the siege. The Insurgents boarded underneath (the pod, with a dock off it)
                 and attack upward; MSR start on the top deck and keep the lift climbing. The
                 drive's fall stops the world outside.
  h9_up_lift     Capture the Uplink: zones 1 the clamp bay, 2 the power room, 3 the drive
                 room, 4 the mess; the uplink on the passenger deck. MSR on the drive deck,
                 the Insurgents in the pod and the cargo deck. Mapconfig: tsm_respawns 1,
                 tsm_roundtime 420.
  h9_ws_lift     Wave Survival: the survivors hold the passenger deck; the hordes come from
                 the pod, the cargo deck and the drive deck. Mapconfig: tsm_respawns 1,
                 tsm_bots 12.
  h9_mw_lift     Mech Warrior: the sides start as on h9_up_lift, with no zones. Mapconfig:
                 tsm_respawns 1, tsm_roundtime 420.
  h9_as_lift     Assault, a copy of h9_up_lift (tools\make_assault_maps.py): the sides start as
                 there, and five capture points of 288 units: 1 the clamp bay, 2 the power
                 room, 3 the drive room, 4 the mess, 5 the uplink room
                 (configs\tsm_assault_h9_as_lift.cfg). Mapconfig: tsm_uplink_mode 0,
                 tsm_respawns 1, tsm_roundtime 720.
  The three mode maps have no siege objectives: the machines stand as scenery.

  Objectives     (the siege) 1 the tether clamps (cargo deck), 2 the power core (passenger
                 deck), 3 the drive (drive deck).
  Rooms          each machine in a walled room, four ways in: a door anyone opens; a door
                 only a Breacher forces (named tsm_breach_*); a door only a Combat Engineer
                 opens (tsm_eng_door); and a crawl vent behind a grate anyone can break.
  Routes         stairs between the decks; two maintenance shafts behind engineers' doors;
                 the gantry outside every deck, by its hatches and ladders (through holes in
                 the grating), open to everyone. Falling off it kills.
  Spawns         (the siege) MSR on the drive deck; Insurgents in the pod, then (forward
                 tiers) the cargo deck once the clamps fall and the passenger deck once the
                 power is cut.
  Mapconfig      (the siege) tsm_obj_team 2, the objective banners, tsm_respawns 1,
                 tsm_roundtime 480.

  Rebuilding it  in export-map\h9_siege_lift (node, SMAK beside TSM):
    node sky.mjs deploy          the sky (gfx\env\h9lift*.tga)
    smak sound sounds.json -o .. ..   its sounds, into TSM\sound\h9lift
    node sprites.mjs deploy      the haze sprite (sprites\h9_lift_haze.spr)
    node lift.mjs --compile deploy    the four maps, their .res and spawn configs
                                 (out\h9_lift.wad holds the scrolling textures, built into
                                 each .bsp; LIFT_ONLY=siege, up, ws or mw does one)
    node lift.mjs --views        pictures from inside, sky and all
    node waypoints.mjs deploy    the bots' waypoints, one walk under all four names
    node tactical.mjs            the tactical map: doors, shafts, hatches, ladders, spawns


7. NEUROPOLIS  (the city; h9_te_neuropolis and its twins)
---------------------------------------------------------
The long east-west city map, MSR at the west end and the Insurgents at the east (36 spawn points
a side), is the match mode's h9_te_neuropolis (and h9_siege_neuropolis, which has no objectives
yet). Two copies of it, made by tools\make_assault_maps.py with the same bsp, spawns and waypoints,
play the other modes:

  h9_up_neuropolis  Capture the Uplink: four zones of 128 units, one near each end and two in the
                    middle crossings, and the uplink's point in the middle
                    (configs\tsm_uplink_h9_up_neuropolis.cfg). Mapconfig: tsm_respawns 1,
                    tsm_roundtime 600.
  h9_as_neuropolis  Assault: seven capture points of 384 units over the city's THREE FLOORS, each
                    reachable on foot from both bases: three on the street (the south-west
                    corner, the south-east corner and the east avenue), two in the undercity
                    (floor -512, the hub under the middle) and two in the shanty (floor -1088,
                    the big room under that), and a spawn set of eight places on each side of
                    each (configs\tsm_assault_h9_as_neuropolis.cfg and
                    tsm_spawns_h9_as_neuropolis.cfg). Mapconfig: tsm_respawns 1, tsm_roundtime 900.

The points were PROPOSED from the map's own geometry (node tools/assault-spawns/cli.mjs
h9_as_neuropolis --suggest 7 --levels auto --overview <folder>: a walk of the map through its doors,
panes and stairs, and down any drop a player would take, finds its floors; open floor with room for
the whole reach on each, spread along the walk, two points of a floor never nearer than a reach and a
quarter) and the sets placed by the same tool (--plan <folder>). They are a start: walk the map,
move a point with amx_tsm_as_add <n> and amx_tsm_as_save (or edit the cfg), and run the tool again
(docs\ai_context\ASSAULT_SPAWNS.txt). The Uplink zones and the uplink's point have not been walked either:
amx_tsm_cap_add, amx_tsm_uplink_set and amx_tsm_uplink_save adjust them.
