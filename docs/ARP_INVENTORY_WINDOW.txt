APOLLO RP INVENTORY AND EXCHANGE, THE WINDOW -- what the player sees and does (the plugin's rules and the protocol: docs\ARP_INVENTORY.txt)
==============================================================================================================

What it is. On an H9 game Apollo RP's inventory is a window of cells (tiles) the player works with the mouse, two players can swap
items and money in a window of their own, and a player can search one who is handcuffed in a third. The server half is the plugin ARP_Inventory (apollo-rp, docs\APOLLO_RP.txt: the rules of the
cells, stacks, a full inventory, the exchange, the switches and the table of what each one turns off); the client half is reTS\cl_dll
h9_arpinv.cpp (the engine's part), h9_arpinv_ui.h (the rules of the mouse and keys), h9_arpinv_draw.h (the drawing) and
h9_arpinv_model.h (the three messages). A game that does not draw it, a player whose cl_h9_arpinv or cl_h9_arpvgui is 0 and a server with
arp_h9_vgui or arp_h9_inv_window 0 keep Apollo RP's text menu exactly as it was: nothing here changes it.


1. HOW IT PLAYS
---------------

Opening. /inventory or /items in chat, or the key bound to cl_h9_inventory (the stock client config binds i; any other game: bind i
cl_h9_inventory). The cursor comes up with the window, as with the buy menu, and is the player's until he closes it: with the X at the
top right of the window, with ESC, or with the key again. The window closes by itself when the player dies or spectates, at an intermission
and at a new map. While it is up the player can still walk; the mouse is not the view.

The window. Eight cells across, four rows (a server may give more cells: the grid scrolls with the wheel or the arrows beside it). Each
tile shows the item's picture (section 4; a monogram where it has none), the number in the stack at the top right, and its name, centred under the picture on as many lines as the tile has room for (section 4). A cell
another exchange holds says HELD and cannot be used or dropped. The title says INVENTORY, how many cells are used of how many
(FULL when none is free), the character's name, job, wallet and bank. The wallet is named CREDITS with the credit sign (CREDITS <sign> 1,250) where the money is credits, as the HUD's
wallet line says it, and WALLET with the dollar sign (WALLET $ 1,250) as stock Apollo RP has it: the server says which (arp_h9_credits). The row under the grid says what the server
answered to the last click (a refusal is yellow).
  * Left-click selects; the panel on the right shows its name, how many are in hand and its description, and the buttons USE, DROP ONE,
    DROP ALL, SPLIT ..., SHOW TO ... Double-click uses it.
  * Right-click a tile: the same as a menu (and INFO, and OFFER while an exchange is open).
  * Drag a tile to another cell: it moves; onto a cell of the same item it COMBINES (up to the stack limit; the rest stays); onto another
    item it swaps. SHIFT while dropping it on an empty cell SPLITS half of the stack off.
  * SPLIT ...: a small box with the number to take off the stack (type it, or - and +; Enter or OK). The new tile is a stack of its own that
    can be used, dropped or offered separately and combined again.
  * Drag a tile out of the window onto the world: it is dropped (the whole stack, one with SHIFT) and lies there as a pack.
  * SHOW TO ...: a list of the players near (within about 170 units, in view); choose one. The item is shown to that player (a license,
    a card, anything): he reads a notice "<NAME> SHOWS YOU / <ITEM> / <description>" for a few seconds and nothing leaves your
    inventory. There is no GIVE TO: goods change hands by an exchange (E on the player), below.
  * TIDY merges the partial stacks and packs the cells to the front. ESC closes a box, then the window.

The exchange. Pressing E on a player of the same server (not a bot; near, in sight, both alive, neither jailed or cuffed) offers him an
exchange. He reads "<NAME> OFFERS YOU AN EXCHANGE" at the top of his screen and has about 12 seconds: E while looking at the one who offered
accepts, or ACCEPT / DECLINE in the banner while his inventory window is open (a banner cannot take the cursor by itself: the
console commands cl_h9_trade_accept and cl_h9_trade_decline do the same and can be bound). Both then see EXCHANGE OF GOODS beside their
inventory: YOUR OFFER and THEIR OFFER, twelve cells each. Drag a tile into your offer (or choose OFFER in its menu); click an offered tile
or drag it away to take it back. The money line is the wallet (never the bank): click it and type an amount and Enter, or +10, +100, ALL,
0. Either side's SEAL EXCHANGE is a promise; ANY change by either side breaks BOTH seals and the button waits 3 seconds. When both are sealed
the server checks everything again at that instant (items still owned, money, range, room in both inventories) and swaps it in one step;
a window says EXCHANGE COMPLETE with what each side received. WITHDRAW, the X and ESC cancel it. Goods in an offer are held: they cannot be
used or dropped until the exchange ends.

The search. Pressing E on a player who is handcuffed or in jail (near, in sight, alive; neither of you in an exchange, and you not handcuffed yourself) does not
offer him an exchange: it opens SEARCHING beside your inventory, for a game that says _h9vgui 8 (an older game is told that player cannot make an
exchange now). He reads "[ARP] <NAME> is searching you." in the chat, once. The window shows his cells exactly as he sees them in his own window: each tile a
picture, a count and a name; OVER CAPACITY beside the title when they hold more than his limit; NOTHING ON HIM when there is nothing. Take things with the mouse:
drag a tile onto your inventory window (SHIFT: one of it), double-click it (all of it), or select it and press TAKE ONE or TAKE ALL under his grid; a right click
offers TAKE ALL, TAKE ONE, TAKE ... (a box asks how many) and INFO. What fits your inventory is taken and the rest stays with him (the row under the grids says
"You took 3 of 5 Soda. Your inventory is full."): no money is taken, and nothing but what his cells hold. His grid scrolls with the wheel or the arrows beside
it. CLOSE, the X and ESC end the search and leave your inventory window as it is (ESC closes a box first). The search ends by itself, with the reason in the row under
the grids, when he is freed, dies or leaves, when you move more than 150 units from him, die or are handcuffed, when your inventory window is closed, at a new
map and when the server switches it off (arp_h9_search 0). A search and an exchange are never open together: E on a free player while you search says "Finish
your search first.".

What the client will not do. It sends at most ten commands a second, reads at most 64 messages a second, believes no count, name or index
past its own limits and never moves an item itself: every click is a request the server may refuse.


2. THE SWITCHES
---------------

Client:  cl_h9_arpinv 1|0        (archived) 0: this game keeps the text inventory (and tells the server so: _h9vgui 5)
         cl_h9_arpvgui 1|0       Apollo RP's menus as panels at all; 0 keeps the text inventory as well
         cl_h9_inventory         opens or closes the window (bind it)
         cl_h9_trade_accept, cl_h9_trade_decline    answer an invitation without the mouse
         cl_h9_arpinv_state      what this game has read, for finding out why a window did not come up
Server:  arp_h9_vgui, arp_h9_inv_window, arp_h9_inv_slots, arp_h9_inv_stack, arp_h9_inv_persist, arp_h9_credits, arp_h9_trade_range, arp_h9_trade_timeout,
         arp_h9_search, arp_h9_search_range
         (docs\APOLLO_RP.txt, COMMANDS.txt). With them off the server plays stock Apollo RP.


3. THE PROTOCOL
---------------

A client says it can draw the window with the userinfo key _h9vgui at 6 or more (5 is H9Look, the aim plaque; 4 the buy window; 7 is 6
and the credit sign, the cent sign Apollo RP shows for money with arp_h9_credits 1; 8 is 7 and the search window, H9Search), and says 5 when cl_h9_arpinv or cl_h9_arpvgui is 0. The server sends the user messages below to such a client only (H9Search to one that says 8), and the client sends the
console command h9inv. The layouts are the plugin's and the client's contract (the byte order is the engine's: little endian).

Three plugin-registered user messages (H9Inv, H9Trade, H9Search), registered with engfunc(EngFunc_RegUserMsg, name, -1) exactly as ARP_Core registers H9ArpHud
(ARP_Core.sma:1037) and sent with engfunc(EngFunc_MessageBegin, MSG_ONE, id_of_message, Float:{0.0,0.0,0.0}, id) (reliable). A
message is at most 188 bytes (H9VGUI_MSG_MAX). Little-endian as the engine: write_byte 0..255, write_short (the client reads it
SIGNED: send 0..32767 only), write_long (signed 32 bit; money 0..2147483647), write_string (ASCII, NUL-terminated, no '^'
translations, no '%'; the client prints them with %s but keep '%' out). Names <= 32 chars, descriptions <= 120, notes <= 120.
Send to protocol-6 clients ONLY. Rate: never more than 12 messages to one player in one frame; a snapshot of many messages is
sent staggered (one message per 0.02 s with a task, or fewer messages) so the reliable channel cannot overflow.

3.1 H9Inv  (server -> client).  First byte = op.
  op 1 OPEN   byte 1; byte flags (bit0 = over capacity, bit1 = unlimited cells, bit2 = the money is called credits (arp_h9_credits 1)); short slots (cells shown: `slots`, or the grid
              length when unlimited); long wallet; long bank; string name (the character's name); string job (the ARP job name or "")
              Sent in answer to "h9inv open" and by Item_Inventory. The client opens the window (cursor on) when it reads OPEN. A second OPEN
              while open refreshes the header. The server then sends the catalog entries the cells need and then the SLOTS.
  op 2 CATALOG byte 2; byte n; then n x { short kind; short stackmax; byte flags(0); string name }
              The item kinds the client has to know to draw cells. Kind ids are ARP item ids (1..N, by registration order: NOT stable
              across server restarts, which is why a name always comes with them). The client forgets the catalog at every map change,
              so the server sends the entries for every kind in the cells in answer to "open" (the client says "open" again after a
              map change). Pack as many entries as fit in 188 bytes.
  op 3 SLOTS  byte 3; short first; byte count; then count x { short kind; short n }   (kind 0 = empty cell, n 0). count <= 40.
              A full snapshot after OPEN is the cells 0..slots-1 in chunks of 40. A change while open sends only the changed cells
              (a chunk per contiguous run is fine). The server sends CATALOG entries for a kind the client has not been sent
              BEFORE the SLOTS that use it (track "catalog sent" per player; clear it on open).
  op 4 MONEY  byte 4; long wallet; long bank          (when either changes while the window is open, at most 4 per second)
  op 5 DESC   byte 5; short kind; string text         (the answer to "h9inv info <kind>": the item's description from ARP_RegisterItem,
              or "No description." ; also the INFO action's text)
  op 6 NOTE   byte 6; byte level (0 plain, 1 warning); string text   (one line for the window's status row: a refusal, "Your inventory
              is full", "That is in your exchange", ... The server ALSO prints the same text to chat as [ARP] ... when the window is not
              open; the client shows a NOTE in the window and does not echo it to chat.)
  op 7 CLOSE  byte 7                                   (the server shuts the window: death, jail, cuffs, map change, window off)
  op 8 RESERVE byte 8; short first; byte count; then count x { short n }   (the count of each cell that an open exchange reserves;
              sent when it changes and with an exchange's OPEN; 0 = free)
  Order after "h9inv open": OPEN, CATALOG..., SLOTS..., RESERVE (if an exchange is open).

3.2 h9inv  (client -> server).  A console command sent with pfnServerCmd:  h9inv <op> <args>
  open | close | tidy
  move <from> <to>           cells 0..slots-1
  split <cell> <n> [<to>]    to omitted or 255 = the first empty cell
  use <cell>
  drop <cell> <n>            n = the count shown for DROP ALL, 1 for DROP ONE and for a shift-drag out of the window, the whole
                             cell for a plain drag out of the window
  give <cell> <n> <player>   player = the entity index (1..32) of the target (the window does not send it any more: an exchange gives)
  show <cell> <player>       SHOW TO ...: the same list of players; no count, nothing moves (section 0, NEW show)
  info <kind>                kind id of the CATALOG
  x accept | decline | cancel | seal | unseal
  x offer <cell> <n> [<index>]   put n of the cell into the sender's offer, index 0..11 or 255 = first free (same kind already in the
                             offer: the counts add up as one entry; one entry per cell)
  x take <index>             take offer entry `index` back
  x money <amount>           my money offer (0..wallet; replaces the previous amount)
  Register with register_clcmd("h9inv", ...). EVERY argument is validated on the server (never trust the client: ranges, ownership,
  counts, the target's distance and line of sight, alive, not jailed). At most 10 commands per second per player, the rest are dropped
  silently. An invalid or refused command answers with a NOTE (level 1) and changes nothing.

3.3 H9Trade  (server -> client).  First byte = op.
  op 1 INVITE byte 1; byte seconds (left); string name        (a player pressed E on you; shows "<NAME> OFFERS YOU AN EXCHANGE")
  op 2 OPEN   byte 2; string partner; long wallet; byte cells (12); byte flags (bit0 = the money is called credits)
              (the exchange window opens for both)
  op 3 OFFER  byte 3; byte side (0 = mine, 1 = theirs); byte index (0..11); short kind; short n; string name
              one cell of an offer changed (kind 0 = emptied). `name` is the item's name for side 1 (the client may not know the
              kind), "" for side 0.
  op 4 MONEY  byte 4; byte side; long amount
  op 5 SEAL   byte 5; byte mine (0/1); byte theirs (0/1); byte lock (tenths of a second until MY seal may be pressed; 0 = now)
  op 6 CLOSE  byte 6; byte reason; string summary
              reason: 0 completed (summary = the result lines the client shows, e.g. "VOSS RECEIVES: Cigarette x10, Pizza x2|KANE
              RECEIVES: Beer x4, 150 credits" with '|' between the two lines; the money worded by inv_money), 1 you withdrew, 2 they withdrew, 3 declined, 4 the invite
              lapsed, 5 out of range, 6 you died, 7 they died, 8 they disconnected, 9 jailed or cuffed, 10 your inventory is full,
              11 their inventory is full, 12 the check failed (an item or the money changed), 13 map change, 14 busy, 15 timed out.
  The inventory messages (H9Inv) keep flowing while an exchange is open; RESERVE tells which cells are held.

3.4 H9Search  (server -> client, to a client that says _h9vgui 8).  First byte = op. The contract the two halves were written from is
D:\temp\wf\arpinv\DESIGN-M5.md; the plugin's own account is docs\ARP_INVENTORY.txt.
  op 1 OPEN   byte 1; byte flags (bit0 = his cells are over capacity); short cells (the length of his grid); string name (his, 32 at most)
              the window opens; a second OPEN while it is open replaces the header and starts his cells again (a search of another player, or the snapshot sent again)
  op 2 SLOTS  byte 2; short first; byte count (40 at most); count x { short kind; short n }
              his cells, kind 0 an empty cell: H9Inv SLOTS' layout, drawn the same. The names of the kinds are the catalog the client has from H9Inv: the server
              sends H9Inv CATALOG entries for the kinds the client lacks BEFORE the SLOTS that use them
  op 3 CLOSE  byte 3; byte reason: 0 you closed it, 1 he is no longer handcuffed or jailed, 2 he died, 3 he left (or his character was loaded again), 4 you moved away,
              5 you died or were handcuffed, or the server closed your own inventory window (H9Inv CLOSE follows), 6 the map is changing or the search was switched off
  op 4 NOTE   byte 4; byte level (0 plain, 1 warning); string text (120 at most): the answer to a take, in the row under the grids
  op 5 MONEY  byte 5; long wallet: his wallet, for a client that says _h9vgui 9. The window then has a row "HIS CREDITS/CASH n" and the buttons TAKE 10, TAKE 100 and TAKE ALL (never more than
              the wallet last told), which send h9inv s money <n>; with no MONEY sent there is no row. Dead buttons when the wallet is 0.
  THE WINDOWS CAN BE MOVED: the title bar of the inventory, exchange and search windows takes hold of the window, which follows the cursor (kept on the screen) and stays where it was put while the game runs.
  THE BOXES ARE BLENDED: the window's boxes are drawn with pfnFillRGBABlend (alpha), not pfnFillRGBA, which this engine draws additively (a dark box then darkened nothing).
  Order when a search opens: H9Inv OPEN, CATALOG and SLOTS of your own inventory if it is not open, H9Search OPEN, H9Inv CATALOG for his kinds, H9Search SLOTS. While
  it is open your own H9Inv messages keep flowing, and an H9Inv CLOSE closes the search with the window. The client's commands (through h9inv, ten a second):
  h9inv s take <cell> <n> (n of the cell <cell> of HIS grid) and h9inv s close. A take is never made by the client itself: the answer is H9Search SLOTS for his changed
  cell, H9Inv SLOTS for yours and an H9Search NOTE, and a take that cannot go on is answered by an H9Search CLOSE.

4. THE PICTURES
---------------

A tile shows its item's picture: a 32 x 32 frame of sprites/h9_arpicons.spr (HL sprite, SPR_ALPHTEST: palette index 255 is transparent, drawn
with SPR_DrawHoles) named in sprites/h9_arpicons.txt, one line a picture:
    Soda|64
    First Aid Kit|68
(the item's name as ARP_RegisterItem has it, exactly, a bar, the frame). The client reads both whenever the HUD's VidInit runs, a map's start or a restart of the video: the
inventory element's VidInit does it, and CHud::VidInit calls it by name (H9ArpInv_VidInit), because the H9 elements register through AddHudElem, are not members of CHud, and
CHud::VidInit does not walk the list of elements (Think, Draw and InitHUDData are walked). A name whose frame the sheet does not have is left out. An item that is not in the
text file is a monogram in a colour of its name. The tile grows with the screen so that a name has room under the picture: 96 pixels on a screen 1000 pixels high or more (three lines of the game's 19 pixel font
under the picture), 80 pixels from 700 pixels (two lines), and the 48 pixel tile of a smaller screen, or a font too tall for a line, holds the picture alone, the name
then being in the panel of the selected item (the status row in the exchange and the search). The name is broken at spaces into the lines the tile has, each line centred
under the picture and no wider than the tile less 3 pixels a side; a name that needs more lines, or a word wider than that, ends in "..." and is never cut off without a
sign. All 101 names the game ships fit whole on the 96 pixel tile (86 of them on the 80 pixel tile). The other player's offer in an exchange is drawn under the name the
server sent with it, so that its picture is found for an item this player's catalog does not hold.

Why a tile shows a monogram: cl_h9_arpinv_state prints "pictures:" first (the sheet is a sprite number with its frames, or NONE and why, and how many times it was read since the game
started: 0 means VidInit has not run), the room a tile has (for the picture, and how many lines of the name), the items the server has named that have no picture, the first tiles' frames, and for a name typed after it
(cl_h9_arpinv_state Flashlight) the frame, or that the list spells it otherwise (names are compared exactly) or has no such name. cl_h9_arpinv_test draws a window of
sixteen tiles for twelve seconds with no server and no item: fifteen names the sheet has and one it has not. python reTS\cl_dll\tests\check_arpicons.py checks the sheet as
the engine reads it and every name the plugins register against the list, and says which items have no picture.

The sheet has the 92 items of Apollo RP's plugins (ARP_TS, ARP_Items, ARP_HungerMod, ARP_TalkArea). The 31 weapons are rendered from their
world models in TS's models folder (w_*.mdl) with SMAK's software renderer, the weapon laid on the diagonal; Apollo RP gives every other
item the one backpack as its world model, so those are DRAWN, flat colours with a dark edge (modelling\arpicons\arpicons_art.py). Every
picture gets a thin light edge so that a dark thing shows on the dark tile. To make the sheet again, after changing a drawing or adding an
item (a drawn one goes at the end of LATER in arpicons_art.py; modelling\arpicons\README.txt says it step by step):
    node modelling\arpicons\bake_final.mjs modelling\arpicons\weapons_final.json modelling\arpicons\baked 32      (the weapons; once)
    python modelling\arpicons\make_arpicons.py [a tab separated list of item names to check against]
which writes modelling\arpicons\out\h9_arpicons.spr and .txt, modelling\arpicons\preview.png (a sheet to look at) and h9_arpicons_atlas.png
(what tools\render_arpinv.py draws the previews of the window with). With --install the two files are copied into sprites\, which the
build ships with the game: install them with the client.dll of 2026-11-10 (18:25 or later), which draws 32 x 32 frames with
SPR_DrawHoles; an older client centres frames as if they were 64 x 64 and draws them additively. A model of an item's own, when there is
one, takes the place of its drawing: render it the same way and list it in weapons_final.json.

H9's own weapons, the PPSh-41 and the laser rifle, are rendered from their world models (H9XASH\ts\models\h9\w_ppsh41.mdl and w_laserrifle.mdl; weapons_h9.json,
baked_h9\) and come last in the sheet (frames 92 and 93), so no earlier frame moved. Their names are "PPSh-41" and "Laser Rifle", and the
spellings PPSH-41, PPSh41, PPSH41, LASER RIFLE, Laser rifle and LaserRifle give the same frames. Apollo RP's ARP_TS registers both weapons as items of
those names (apollo-rp\ORIGIN.txt, 34), so a tile shows one as soon as a player has it; on a server whose h9_customweapons is 0 using the item gives it back.

The window's colours are H9's purple, or the colour the player gave the panels (cl_h9_vgui_r, _g, _b: docs\H9_VGUI.txt 4k); its warning, money and
stock colours (red, green, yellow) are not moved.

The same pictures stand above the notice of an item lying on the floor. The look-at line of a dropped item (arp_h9_itemhover) is the fourth HUD, the notice
banner; to a game that draws the HUD as panels and says _h9vgui 7 the server adds a line of the kind HUDK_ICON, "icon:<the item's name>", and the
panel (h9_arphud.cpp) draws that item's frame above the banner, centred, two or three times its size on a 720 or 1080 line screen (one at 480),
below the banner when the top of the screen leaves no room, fading with it. The panel reads the sheet itself (vgui_H9Panel.cpp, H9ItemPicture
and CH9PicturePanel: the frame's RGBA as a VGUI bitmap, kept for the life of the client). cl_h9_arphud_icon 0 hides it.

The same sheet also stands in the world, over the pack itself, for everybody who sees the pack (arp_h9_itemicon on the server, docs\APOLLO_RP.txt, 2, and apollo-rp\ORIGIN.txt, 28):
a camera-facing sprite of the item's frame, 19 units across, over the backpack. It needs no client change: the sheet is a sprite the engine draws, its type SPR_FWD_PARALLEL faces
the viewer, and the server precaches it (a client that lacks the file has it sent). A client of an older build shows it as well.

5. TESTS
--------

reTS\cl_dll\tests\test_arpinv.cpp checks the model and the clicks without the game: the messages decoded and refused (truncated, out of
range, no terminator, negative, huge), the layout at seven screen sizes with and without an exchange (every cell is found at its own centre,
the windows stay on the screen), select, double-click, the menu, split, drag (move, combine, split, drop, offer, take back), show,
the exchange (seal lock, money field, quick buttons, ESC), the search (its messages decoded and refused, the layout at seven screens and three grid lengths, select,
double-click, the buttons, the right-click menu and the TAKE box, a drag onto the inventory, ESC and the X, the wheel and the arrows, my own window beside it),
scrolling, the header's words (CREDITS with the credit sign and WALLET with $, at three screens), the picture of a tile at the layouts the game has (the 96 and 80 pixel tiles with the game's
19 pixel font and a smaller one, the 48 pixel tile that holds the picture alone, a font too tall for a line under the picture, the offers, the other player's offer under the
name the server sent), the names under the pictures (every name the game ships whole on the 96 pixel tile, most of them on the 80 pixel tile and the rest ending in dots, each
line centred), the reading of the picture list (IconList in
h9_arpinv_icons.h: comments, CRLF, a name too long, a list too long, a frame the sheet does not have; and the shipped sprites\h9_arpicons.txt against the shipped sheet), and
a check of the source that CHud::VidInit calls H9ArpInv_VidInit and that this reads the pictures.
Build and run: cl /std:c++17 /EHsc /I.. test_arpinv.cpp. The checks of the pictures and of the names read H9XASH\ts\sprites\h9_arpicons.txt and .spr and H9XASH\valve\gfx.wad
(the game's own font, its lump CREDITSFONT) and print SKIP when they are not beside the tests.
reTS\cl_dll\tests\render_arpinv.cpp and tools\render_arpinv.py draw the windows for twenty-seven situations (15 to 23 are the search: four screens, the menu,
the TAKE box, a drag, nothing on him, a long grid over capacity; 29 to 32 are the owner's 1920 x 1080 screen, an 800 x 600 one and an exchange, measured and cut with the game's own
font: the engine's creditsfont, 19 pixels high, read from valve\gfx.wad) into pictures with the very drawing the game uses (python tools\render_arpinv.py <folder of
the .json files>): the layout, not the glyphs.
The model was also checked against the server engineer's own strict decoders and client models, which were written from the contract alone (D:\temp\wf\arpinv-server\test5;
they are not in the repository): random valid H9Inv and H9Search traffic from an encoder written for the purpose, accepted by those decoders, replayed through
h9_arpinv_model.h by a small C++ program, agreed after every message (25 seeds: 33,390 messages, 1.9 million comparisons, none different; a value changed on purpose is found).

6. OWNER'S CHECKS (in a game, in order)
---------------------------------------

  1. On a roleplay map with the new game and server: say /inventory. The window comes up, the cursor with it, and stays up until the X, ESC
     or the key again (try each). Walk about with it up. Die with it up: it closes.
  2. Select a tile: its description appears. Double-click a food: it is eaten. Drag a tile to an empty cell, onto a cell of the same item
     (combine), onto another item (swap). SHIFT-drop on an empty cell: half is split off. SPLIT ... with a number.
  3. Drag a tile out of the window: it is dropped and a pack lies there; walk up to it and press E. Fill the cells and pick up one more
     item: "Your inventory is full".
  4. SHOW TO ... with a second player (and none near: "NO PLAYER NEAR"). Show a license or a card: the other player reads it. There is
     no GIVE TO in the window, in the menu or as a button. Every tile shows a picture (the guns look like the guns).
  5. Press E on a second player: he is offered an exchange. Accept (E at him). Put items and money in, seal, change something (both seals
     break, the button waits), seal both: EXCHANGE COMPLETE and the goods have changed hands; look at the server log. Cancel one with
     WITHDRAW, one with ESC, one by walking away.
  6. cl_h9_arpinv 0 (the console, then change the map): /inventory is the text menu again. arp_h9_vgui 0 on the server: the same for everyone.
  7. The weapons: in the server's console (or as an ARP admin) arp_createitems "<your name>" "PPSh-41" 1 and the same with "Laser Rifle": both tiles show
