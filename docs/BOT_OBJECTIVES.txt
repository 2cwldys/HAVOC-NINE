BOT OBJECTIVES -- how the bots play Assault, the Uplink and Capture the Flag, and TS's own capture game, and what the plugins publish for them
================================================================================================================================================

rcbot_mm.dll (rcbot-patch, CHANGE 11) plays the objectives of a mode while the mode's plugin runs, the round is live and
tsm_bot_objectives is 1. It reads what ts_assault, ts_uplink and ts_ctf write on their own entities: no message, no cvar
per value, no extra entity. This file is the contract. include\tsm_botobj.inc holds the helpers the three plugins share.
TS's own capture game (the briefcase, on the ctb_ and ctf_ maps) has no plugin: rcbot reads what the game gives out
itself (section 5).


1. THE SWITCH
-------------

  tsm_bot_objectives   1 (the default): the bots play the objectives of the claimed mode. 0: they only fight.
                       Registered by ts_assault, ts_uplink and ts_ctf (a cvar that exists keeps its value) and read by
                       rcbot once a second. A server whose plugins do not register it, or whose rcbot is the stock one,
                       has bots that fight. The value line stays commented out in ts_matchmode.cfg, like tsm_bot_powerups:
                       that file runs on every map load.
  tsm_enabled          rcbot plays nothing while the match mode is off.
  tsm_pres_mode        "assault", "uplink" or "ctf": which mode's fields the entities named tsm_cap carry (Assault points and
                       Uplink zones share the classname). The core sets it once a second.
  tsm_bot_stockobjectives   1 (the default): the bots play TS's own capture game where no match mode runs the map
                            (section 5). Registered by ts_matchmode, read by rcbot once a second; tsm_bot_objectives
                            and tsm_enabled do not enter into it, but tsm_enabled above 0 means a match mode runs the
                            map and the game is not played.

  With 0 the bots take no part: a bot that stands in an Assault point still counts there, as it always did; ts_uplink
  does not let a bot pick the uplink up; ts_ctf lets a bot take and return flags only while tsm_cf_bots is 1.
  A bot that carries something when the switch goes to 0 keeps it until it dies: a fake client sends no client
  command, so nothing can make it drop the uplink or a flag.
  Wave Survival, Mech Warrior, Siege and the maps TS plays as it ships publish nothing, and nothing changes there.


2. THE FIELDS
-------------

Sides are 1 and 2 (pev->team of a player is his side); 0 is nobody. A float of -1.0 means none. Nothing is written on a
player, and none of these fields reaches a client (reTS AddToFullPack copies no iuser, fuser, vuser or team).

  2.1 A capture point of Assault and a control zone of the Uplink: the ring, classname tsm_cap
      Exists for the map when the ring sprites are there (tsm_zone_ring_*.spr); without them there is no ring to read.

      team      the holder: 0, 1 or 2                                     (existing)
      fuser1    the radius across the floor, units                        (existing)
      iuser1    the point's or zone's number                              (existing)
      origin    the ring's middle, 2 units above the floor                (existing)
      iuser2    state. Assault: 0 quiet, 1 being taken, 2 contested (both sides inside, nothing moves).
                Uplink: 0 quiet, 1 the carrier is inside and his timer runs, 2 the carrier is inside with a living enemy
                of his side there (no timer). Both: 3 not in play (warmup, freeze, round end, match end).
      iuser3    the side taking it while iuser2 is 1; in the Uplink also the carrier's side while it is 2; else 0
      fuser2    progress. Assault: -1 to +1, side 1 holding it in full at +1, side 2 at -1, 0 neutral; it stays as it is
                when nobody is inside. Uplink: the share of tsm_cap_time the carrier has stood in it, 0 to 1
      fuser3    how far above or below the ring's floor a player still counts as in it: 128 (Assault), 96 (Uplink)
      vuser1    x: players of side 1 in play inside, y: players of side 2, z: 0. The numbers the capture uses (Assault:
                the nearest point takes a player who stands in several; Uplink: the first zone that holds him). Bots
                count like anyone.

  2.2 The uplink, classname tsm_uplink
      Exists only while a round is live.

      aiment    the carrier while it is carried, else 0 (authoritative; owner is the same)           (existing)
      origin    where it is                                                                          (existing)
      iuser2    1 at its post, never carried this round; 2 carried; 3 dropped where a carrier lost it
      iuser3    carried: the carrier's side. Dropped: the side whose carrier lost it. At its post: 0
      fuser2    dropped: the seconds before it flies home (tsm_uplink_return less the seconds it has lain); -1 when it is
                not dropped, or when tsm_uplink_return is 0
      vuser1    its post

      Only the carrier takes a zone: tsm_cap_time seconds inside, the whole time with no living enemy inside. Every zone
      whose team is not the carrier's side can be taken.

  2.3 A flag of Capture the Flag, classname tsm_flag
      One for each side; exists only while a round is live.

      team      whose flag it is (the side whose pad it stands on)                                    (existing)
      iuser1    0 at its pad, 1 carried, 2 dropped                                                    (existing)
      aiment, owner   the carrier while it is carried, else 0                                         (existing)
      origin    where it is: at its pad, or where it lies (its foot, the floor)                       (existing)
      iuser2    while it is carried: 1 if its carrier would score by reaching his pad now (his own flag at its pad, or
                tsm_cf_needhome 0), else 0. Always 0 in the other states
      fuser2    dropped: the seconds before it flies home (tsm_cf_return less the seconds it has lain); -1 otherwise, or
                when tsm_cf_return is 0
      vuser1    its pad: the floor where it stands and where its carrier turns the enemy's flag in

      The carrier of side f's flag is of side 3-f and scores at his own pad, within tsm_cf_pad_radius across the floor and
      72 units up or down of its floor. A flag is taken or returned by the nearest player within 48 units across the floor.

  2.4 Written when and how often
      The ring's state changes are written at once (every repaint), its counts every tick: Assault every 0.25 s, the
      Uplink every 0.1 s with the counts every 0.2 s. The uplink's fields are written at the instant it changes hands and
      every tick; the flags' every tick (0.1 s) and at the instant they change hands. A reader sees the last write; the
      server is single-threaded.
      The uplink and the flags are made and removed every round, and the engine reuses a freed edict: a reader looks them
      up by classname about once a second, and checks the classname before it trusts an index.


3. WHAT RCBOT DOES WITH THEM
----------------------------

Once a second rcbot reads the entities above and gives each bot a post (bot_objective.h; unit tests in rcbot-patch\test).
A quarter of each side, and any bot without a post, fights and roams as it always did. Bots that follow an Officer, the
citizens of a roleplay map and a bot held by the plugin play no objective.

  Assault   Two bots for a neutral point and three for the other side's (one more while an enemy stands in it); the bots
            already inside count, and so do the players who are no bot of the plan. A point nearly taken (a quarter of its
            bar left, nobody against) draws no more. A point of its side that the other side wears down or stands in is
            retaken before a neutral one, the sooner the further it is worn. A quiet point of its side is guarded only at
            the front.
  Uplink    The uplink lying at its post or dropped: the two nearest bots of each side walk onto it, unless it is about to
            fly home. The side that carries it: the carrier walks to the cheapest zone its side does not hold (the walk, a
            fixed cost for each enemy inside, more while the plugin says it is denied there) and stands in it; three bots
            escort it, a person's carrier as well, and one goes to the zone it is heading for and clears it. The other
            side: three bots hunt the carrier.
  Flag      The enemy flag at its pad or dropped: the two nearest bots fetch it. A carrier walks to its own pad and stands
            on it; two bots escort it. The side's own flag at its pad is guarded by two bots; dropped, the nearest bot
            returns it; carried by the other side, three bots hunt the carrier.

A carrier on its way to its zone or pad looks for no fight and follows no enemy until it is hurt (1.5 seconds) or has
arrived. A bot that gets nowhere in 5 seconds gives its post up for 25. rcbot paths by waypoint: a post with no waypoint
within 400 units is not given to anyone, and a ring with none inside is reached by the nearest and the last stretch walked
straight.


4. TESTING IT
-------------

  amx_tsm_assault, amx_tsm_uplink, amx_tsm_ctf   print the switch and each point's, zone's or flag's published fields
  server log                                     "[RCBOT OBJ]" lines: objective play on and off, and the first posts
  tsm_bot_objectives 0                           the bots fight as before


5. TS'S OWN CAPTURE GAME (the ctb_ and ctf_ maps)
-------------------------------------------------

TS plays a capture game of its own, the briefcase (docs\CAPTURE_THE_FLAG.txt, section 7): two ts_briefcase bases, one for each of two
teams, and a flag, the briefcase, that a team takes from the other's base and carries to its own. The match mode stays out of those
maps (tsm_enabled 0) and no plugin publishes anything: the game keeps a flag's state in its own private members. rcbot_mm.dll
(CHANGE 12) reads what the game does give out, and plays it with the posts of section 3's flags.

  5.1 What it reads
      ts_briefcase  the base: a brush, invisible. Its box says where it is. Its team is the map's key ownerteam (1 or 2, 0 neutral),
                    which rcbot takes from the map as it loads: nothing of it is on the entity afterwards
      ts_flag       a flag that lies loose (at its base, dropped, in flight); it exists only then. rendercolor is its team's glow:
                    125 165 210 the first team, 200 90 70 the second. A flag that is carried is no entity
      TSFlag        user message to everyone, four bytes: 0 a base seeded its flag / 1 it was taken / 2 it was dropped; the flag's id;
                    its team number; the carrier's edict index. A capture and a return send none of their own: the base seeds the
                    flag again, 0.25 s after a capture and 1 s after a return or an expiry, and that says 0
      ScoreInfo     user message to everyone, a player's score and his team number: the index of his team in the map's team list, plus
                    one. It is the only thing that says which side a player is on, since nothing stamps pev->team in this game
  Team numbers are 1 and 2 for a map of two teams; they are the sides of section 3. A player's side is what ScoreInfo gave him, and
  while the game is played a bot takes another player for an enemy when the numbers differ.

  5.2 When it plays
      The map has exactly two ts_briefcase entities, one with ownerteam 1 and one with ownerteam 2; tsm_enabled is 0 (or the cvar does
      not exist); tsm_bot_stockobjectives is not 0 (a server without ts_matchmode has no such cvar, which plays); no roleplay
      citizens walk. A neutral base, two for a team, or a third team is not played (the server log says so once), and a map run by the
      match mode (tsm_enabled 1) is read as section 2 says.

  5.3 What the bots do
      The posts and the restraint are section 3's (Flag): the two nearest bots of a side FETCH the other team's briefcase where it lies
      at its base or dropped; the bot that has it CARRIES it onto the middle of its own base and stands there; two bots ESCORT the
      carrier, a person's as well; two GUARD their own briefcase at its base; the nearest bot RETURNs it when dropped; three HUNT the
      carrier of it. A dropped briefcase lies 20 seconds (counted from its message) and one about to go home is not walked to. The
      brush scores by touch, and only while the carrier's own briefcase lies at its base (CTSBriefcase::CaptureTouch): with it away
      the carrier waits at the base, and the return and the hunt get it back.
      A bot is sent to take a briefcase only if it can: TS lets a player take one only with 20 free inventory slots, and sets his
      speed by the slots he has left (210 + 120 * free / 81, cut to a whole number), so a bot with a maxspeed of 200 to 238 is too
      heavy and is never sent for one. It may still return, escort and guard. A bot cannot drop a briefcase (a fake client sends no
      client command); it lets go by dying, or when a player of its side presses the use key on it (tsm_handoff 1): the game's drop_0 is
      sent for the bot, which gives up a flag only when it carries one, and the bot's touch of a loose flag is refused for tsm_handoff_secs
      (8) seconds, so that the player can take it.
      Check (the hand-off). Capture the Uplink with bots: when a bot of your side carries the uplink, press E on it from within about 96
      units: it drops where it stands (the chat says "handed over the uplink"), stays off it for 8 s, and you walk up and take it. The same
      with a flag in Capture the Flag, and with the briefcase on a ctb_ or ctf_ map (no chat line there: TS's own "dropped_briefcase" is in
      the server log). E on a bot that carries nothing, or on an enemy bot: nothing. tsm_handoff 0: nothing happens at all.

  5.4 Testing it
      server log   "[RCBOT BRIEF] capture game: base of team 1 at ... of team 2 at ..." and "[RCBOT OBJ] objective play is on: TS's
                   capture game, 2 flag(s) read" when the game is on; the first 40 TSFlag and ScoreInfo messages the bots saw
                   ("[RCBOT BRIEF] TSFlag: action 1, flag 3, team 2, carrier 5"); "[RCBOT BRIEF] N briefcase(s), owners ...: only one
                   base for each of two teams is played" for a map that is not played; "[RCBOT BRIEF] side 1: 3 of 4 bots carry too
                   much to take a flag ..." when half of a side is too heavy to be sent for one; and the "[RCBOT OBJ]" lines of
                   section 4
      the game's    triggered "aquired_briefcase", "dropped_briefcase" and "captured_briefcase" with the player, in TS's own log
      tsm_bot_stockobjectives 0   the bots are stock rcbot again, which on these maps takes every player for an enemy


6. WAYPOINTS
------------

rcbot walks only along a map's waypoints: rcbot\waypoints\ts\<map>.rcw. The visibility table beside it,
rcbot\visibility\ts\<map>.rcv2, is worked out by rcbot itself the first time the map loads, and again whenever the
waypoint count changes (a table of another size is ignored). A post where no waypoint stands is played badly or not
at all. What objective play (CHANGE 11, bot.cpp ObjAddRing, ObjAddUplink, ObjAddFlag) asks of them:

  ring        An Assault point or an Uplink zone: goal waypoints within 0.6 of its radius across the floor and within
              72 of its floor + 36 (no ladder, lift, jump, crouch, teleport, stunt or other special one). The bots
              walk to one and stand there; the 12 nearest the middle are kept. One is enough to play it, 3 to 6 let
              the up to 4 bots of a post spread. With none inside, the bots walk to the nearest waypoint within 400
              (one in sight first) and the last stretch straight, which fails at a corner; with none within 400 the
              post is given to nobody.
  uplink,     The nearest waypoint within 400 of the mark, one in sight first. A bot within 160 across the floor of
  flag, pad   its mark walks straight to it, so a waypoint within about 60 with a clear line is as good as a path.
  spawns      A bot that spawns makes for its nearest waypoint within 400: every spawn set (Assault: each tier)
              needs one.
  all         They must be on the bots' graph both ways: reached along the paths from the base spawns, and back.

HOW COVERAGE IS CHECKED. tools\waypoints\coverage.mjs measures each objective map as rcbot meets it. The objectives
come from what the plugins read (configs\tsm_assault_<map>.cfg, tsm_uplink_<map>.cfg, tsm_ctf_<map>.cfg, then the
map's own tsm_as_N, tsm_cap_N, tsm_uplink and tsm_flag_N entities), the waypoints from H9XASH\rcbot\waypoints\ts
(or --rcw-dir):

    node tools\waypoints\coverage.mjs [map ...]     one row an objective: waypoints inside, nearest one, reach, status

  OK      enough inside, reached from both bases       THIN   fewer than a ring wants
  OUTSIDE none inside, the last stretch walked straight    NONE   no waypoint within 400
  UNREACHED not reached from a base, or no way back    FAR    an item with no waypoint near it or in sight

HOW A MAP MAKER ADDS THEM. The map needs a .rcw first (SMAK's walker, export-map\waypoints.mjs); these tools add to
one that exists.
  1. node tools\waypoints\coverage.mjs <map>                        what is short
  2. node tools\waypoints\lay.mjs <map> --out <dir>                 writes <dir>\rcbot\waypoints\ts\<map>.rcw (never
     over the original) and <dir>\_added\<map>.txt: waypoints on walkable floor in each short ring, on each mark and
     beside far spawns, joined both ways along lines a player walks with room beside them, through no door and over
     no drop (a chain of waypoints where no short line reaches the graph). Nothing old is moved, removed or
     reflagged: the new waypoints are appended.
  3. node tools\waypoints\check.mjs <map> <file> --reach            each new waypoint and path checked on its own,
     and against SMAK's own walk of the map
  4. node tools\waypoints\coverage.mjs <map> --rcw-dir <dir>\rcbot\waypoints\ts       the table after
  5. Copy the .rcw to Half-Life\rcbot\waypoints\ts\ (build.bat deploys it to H9XASH, export.bat to export\) and
     delete the map's old H9XASH\rcbot\visibility\ts\<map>.rcv2. tools\waypoints\show.mjs <map> <objective> draws
     the floor and the waypoints around one objective.
  By hand, rcbot's waypoint editor (rcbot\rcbot_waypoints.txt) does the same: plain waypoints, 36 over the floor,
  joined both ways.

A ROOM ONLY A DOOR OPENS. A bot never opens a +USE door (spawnflag 256). h9_as_neuropolis's two tsm_eng_door doors
(Combat Engineer) seal Assault points 1 and 6 and their spawn sets. lay.mjs waypoints such a room inside and leaves
it unjoined, and coverage says UNREACHED (the post is tried, fails at once and is given up for 25 s).
lay.mjs --through-doors joins the room through the door instead: the bots then walk to the door, get nowhere and give
the post up, unless a person opens it.
