===============================================================================
 CHAT BUBBLES: WHAT A PLAYER SAID, DRAWN OVER HIS HEAD BY THE CLIENT
 (ts_floatchat.amxx's words for an H9 client; reTS cl_dll\h9_chatbubble.cpp)
===============================================================================

 Who this is for: whoever runs an H9 server (the switches), whoever builds H9's
 reTS client (the code), and any player who wants to change how it looks.

 The short version
   * ts_floatchat shows what a player said, to a client that cannot draw it
     itself, as a HUDMESSAGE whose place the server works out for every viewer
     ten times a second. That lags a fast turn, takes HUD channels (three
     people's words at most), and the words of several speakers run into each
     other on the screen.
   * An H9 client draws them itself. The server sends each line ONCE, as
     the H9Say user message; the client keeps each speaker's latest line, and
     every frame puts it in a box in H9's panel style over the head of the
     speaker its CROSSHAIR is on. The box is placed on the client's own screen
     with the client's own camera, so it never lags a turn, and since only the
     speaker aimed at has a box, lines cannot run into each other.
   * Every other client is sent the hudmessage. The typing-bubble SPRITE
     ("..." over a player writing) is the same for everyone.
   * A box never overlaps the typing sprite: while a speaker's sprite shows, his
     box rests over it (section 3). A player's own line is his own choice: it
     is sent to him when his client asks (cl_h9_chatbubble_own 1) or the server
     makes it everyone's (fc_self 1); by default nobody sees his own line.
   * Each part has its own switch: the server's h9_floatchat_* cvars and the
     player's cl_h9_chatbubble_* cvars (section 6). cl_h9_chatbubble_list
     prints every client switch and its value.

 1. WHO GETS WHAT
 ----------------
 A client says what it does with the userinfo key _h9chatbubble (the client
 sets it, as it sets _h9tts and _h9vgui). ts_floatchat reads it when a player
 joins and whenever his userinfo changes. The key's value carries two answers:
 its first character says whether the game draws the words itself, its second
 whether the player also wants his own line.

   value    who says it                                  the server sends
   0        cl_h9_chatbubble 0                           the hudmessage
   1        cl_h9_chatbubble 1, cl_h9_chatbubble_own 0   H9Say, once, when the
                                                         line is said
   1s       cl_h9_chatbubble 1, cl_h9_chatbubble_own 1   H9Say, and his own line
                                                         too (below)
   (none)   a Steam client, a game older than the key    the hudmessage
   other    2, 9, s, 01s, an empty value: no 1 first     the hudmessage

 H9Say needs h9_floatchat_bubble 1 as well; with it 0 every game is sent the
 hudmessage. Only a 1 in the first place counts; the s counts only straight
 after it, and only a small s (1S, 10 and 1x are "1"). A value is read to 3
 characters, so a longer one is cut. An older ts_floatchat tests the first
 character and nothing else, so it reads 1s as 1: a game that asks for its own
 line is still drawn for as before, it just is not sent the line; and an older
 client's 1 means what it always did. The own line is a letter after the 1 and
 not a key of its own because a game's userinfo is 256 bytes and full
 (h9_clientid was dropped by it once): the s is one byte more, sent only by a
 player who turned the own line on.

 A player who typed .bubbles (float chat off) is sent neither. A viewer is sent
 H9Say only while he is alive. A say_team line goes only to the speaker's own
 team (h9_floatchat_bubble_team 1, the default): the client keeps a line for as
 long as it stays, and a team line must not reach the other side's game to be
 held there. The hudmessage path has no team test: it shows a team line to
 everyone in sight.

 THE OWN LINE. A player cannot see his own head in first person, so his own
 line is the one box that is not over a head: it sits near the top of his
 screen (section 3). It is sent to him
   when fc_self is 1   whatever his game asks, to every player: as H9Say to a
                       game that draws the words, as the hudmessage to one that
                       cannot (it has no way to ask)
   when fc_self is 0   (the default) only if his game asked, with 1s: the
                       player's cvar decides. A game that asked while
                       h9_floatchat_bubble is 0 is sent it as the hudmessage
 The cvar is 0 by default, so by default nobody sees his own line. The client
 shows the own line it is sent, whatever its cvar says: the cvar is the request,
 fc_self 1 is the server's order.

 The server does not test distance or sight when it sends: the client does,
 every frame, with the range the message carries.


 2. THE MESSAGE: H9Say
 ---------------------
 Registered by ts_floatchat in plugin_precache, variable length, whatever the
 cvars say (so every game learns it at connect). Sent with fakemeta's
 EngFunc_MessageBegin, MSG_ONE_UNRELIABLE: a line lost to a bad packet is a
 line not shown, and a burst of chat never fills a reliable channel.

   byte    the speaker's slot, 1 to 128; 0 with a ttl of 0 takes every line down
   byte    flags: bit 0, the line was said in team chat; bit 1, it is an action: Apollo RP's /me <action>
           on a roleplay map (rp_ or tsrp_ in the map's name), sent as "*action*", which the client draws in red
   short   how long the line stays, in tenths of a second; 0 takes the
           speaker's line down
   short   how far away it shows, in units (fc_range)
   byte    the top of the speaker's head above his origin standing, in units
   byte    the same ducking
   string  the words, at most h9_floatchat_bubble_maxchars (12 to 150), never
           broken into lines (the client breaks them to fit its box)

 The head heights are what ts_floatchat reads from the speaker's model (the
 idle sequences' bounding box in models\player\<name>\<name>.mdl: 35 to 39 for
 a human, 48 for the Mech and the ED-209, standing; 23 to 26 ducking), rounded.
 The client takes the standing or the ducking one by the speaker's hull and
 stretches it by his scale.

 The words are what the player said and nothing added: control characters and
 doubled spaces gone, a line typed all in capitals set in sentence case, cut to
 the limit with "..." at a space when one is near. At most 188 bytes a message.

 A line comes down for every viewer before its time when its speaker dies,
 leaves the match, changes to a state the match mode does not show (cloaked,
 eliminated, spectating) or leaves the server: the plugin sends the slot's
 ttl 0. A viewer who types .bubbles is sent ttl 0 for slot 0.

 The client reads the message with its own limits whatever it says: at most 24
 messages a second, the words cleaned and cut to 160 bytes, a ttl of 30 seconds
 at most, a range of 64 to 16384 units, a slot in the player range.


 3. WHAT THE CLIENT DOES WITH A LINE
 -----------------------------------
 It keeps the latest line of each speaker (slot), with when it came and for how
 long. Each frame, in the HUD's draw (so the boxes are placed in the frame they
 are painted in; VGUI paints after the HUD), it:

   1. Drops lines whose time is up, and the lines the player does not want
      (cl_h9_chatbubble_team 0 drops team lines).
   2. For each speaker: needs his entity in this frame's packet (not out of the
      PVS), drawn (no EF_NODRAW), not cloaked (render mode set and amount 16 or
      less), not spectating, in front of the view, within the line's range.
   3. Aimed mode (cl_h9_chatbubble_aimed 1, the default): the speaker whose
      BODY the crosshair is on. The body is the rectangle from the point over
      his head to his feet, as wide as a quarter of its height on each side of
      his middle, widened all round by cl_h9_chatbubble_aim percent of the
      screen height (4 by default, 43 px at 1080p; 0 is the body alone). The
      speaker already shown keeps his box 1.6 times that and 2 px more, so it
      does not flicker at the edge. Where two bodies are both under the
      crosshair, the one whose middle is nearer wins. The crosshair is the
      middle of the screen, also in third person.
      Every-speaker mode (aimed 0): every speaker whose head point is on the
      screen, nearest first, at most 4 boxes.
   4. Line of sight: a trace from the eye (the one the aim names use) to the
      point over the head, glass ignored and players ignored (the trace goes on
      past a player), clear to 95 percent of the way. A speaker behind a wall,
      a door or the world gets no box, however near the crosshair. At most 8
      traces a frame.
   5. Draws a box for the first speakers that pass (see the placing below).

 PLACING. The box rests on the point 6 units over the top of the speaker's
 head (over the top of his typing sprite while that shows, below), centred on
 it, with a pointer from its bottom edge to that point. It is
 kept inside the screen (4 px from the sides), above the bottom band the H9
 panels keep clear (the chat and the HUD's boxes), beneath the top stack of the
 H9 panels (the notices, the match bar, the row of marks: H9Vgui_StackBottom,
 docs\H9_VGUI.txt 4c) and off the crosshair's box (6 percent of the width by
 4 percent of the height round the middle): when it would not fit where the head
 puts it, it moves, and the pointer goes (it points only from a box that sits
 on the point). Boxes of two speakers never overlap: the later one goes above.
 The player's own line (section 1) sits at the middle of the screen's width,
 0.30 of its height, the place the hudmessage gives it.

 THE TYPING SPRITE. While a player's chat line is open the server shows
 sprites\tsm_typing.spr over his head (a speech bubble with three dots): an
 entity ts_floatchat makes for him once and keeps, owned by him, hidden with
 EF_NODRAW when he is not writing (which keeps it out of the packet). Its frames
 are 128 x 96 pixels, centred on its origin, and it is drawn at fc_scale (0.20,
 so 19.2 units tall) with its origin fc_rise (24) units over his eyes. For a
 standing player (eyes 28 over his origin) its top is 28 + 24 + 9.6 = 61.6
 units over his origin, where a box resting on his head (36 + 6) would sit
 across it. So the box is lifted:
   * entity.cpp's HUD_AddEntity tells h9_chatbubble.cpp about each typing sprite
     it lets through (H9ChatBubble_NoteEntity), by the owner field of its
     entity state: the player it is over.
   * For each speaker with a line, Locate asks (TypingSpriteTop) whether his
     sprite still shows: the entity is still his and still that model (an
     entity number can be reused), it is in this frame's packet, it is not
     EF_NODRAW, and the player has cl_h9_chatbubble_typing 1.
   * If so the box rests 6 units over the sprite's top (its origin's height plus
     48 times its scale, a scale of 0 being 1 and one over 4 being 4) when that is higher
     than the point over the head, and never more than 64 units over that point
     whatever the server's sprite says.
 The line of sight and the aim are still measured to the point over the head,
 not to the lift. With no sprite showing the box rests exactly where it always
 did and stands still; it rises when the sprite comes and falls when it goes.
 The server takes the sprite down the moment a line is sent, so new words
 appear with no sprite under them. The lift is measured straight up in the
 world and the sprite faces the camera, so a steep look down at a speaker
 leaves a smaller gap between the box and the sprite than a level one.
 The server's own words for a game that cannot draw them (the hudmessage) rest
 the same way: 6 units over the sprite's top, 48 x fc_scale over its centre,
 while the sprite is drawn (words_anchor).

 FADE. A box comes up over 0.12 s when it begins to show, and with
 cl_h9_chatbubble_fade 1 fades out over the last 0.8 s of the line.

 LINGER. Whether a speaker's box shows is worked out afresh every frame: he must be in this
 frame's packet, on the screen, in sight, within range and (aimed mode) under the crosshair. One
 frame that fails any of these used to take the box down and bring it back fading in: a blink. A
 box that had shown for at least 0.15 s now stays where its speaker was for cl_h9_chatbubble_linger
 seconds (0.35) after he stops qualifying, following the world point it was over, and then fades
 out over 0.3 s; if he qualifies again meanwhile it carries on as if nothing happened. 0 takes a
 box down at once. A speaker who is hidden, cloaked, spectating or gone, a line whose time is up
 and the boxes while the HUD is off leave nothing behind. The range has a margin of its own: a
 box that is showing stays until the speaker is 12 percent past the range (fc_range, or
 cl_h9_chatbubble_range), so one standing at the edge does not blink.

 A line is kept until its time is up, whoever is aimed at: a line that arrives
 while another speaker is aimed at shows when its speaker is, if that is before
 its time is up.


 4. WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE
 ---------------------
 Everything is the H9 panels' own, so a box looks like a notice of the match
 bar (docs\H9_VGUI.txt, sections 2 and 9):
   art        sprites\tsm_banner.spr ("#banner"), the purple brushed-metal
              slab with the chamfered corners, the dark rim and the corner
              brackets, drawn through the panels' nine-slice (CH9Surface's
              art, 32 px corner slices, content box insets 22 18 22 10) at the
              notices' scale, (screen height / 480) x 0.45 x cl_h9_vgui_scale.
              It comes in three widths (the art's own, the middle, 30 percent
              of the screen) and a height by its rows, so for one screen size,
              scale and text size it makes at most 15 of the panels' art
              bitmaps (MAX_BITMAPS, 64, in vgui_H9Panel.cpp, is shared with the
              menus and the notices; the client keeps every size it makes). A
              single row is a slim banner, as a notice is.
   font       the textscheme's "H9 Text 1" to "H9 Text 4" (Square 721 Bold
              through ts\resource\fonts\fonts.txt), by cl_h9_chatbubble_scale;
              "H9 Flash 1" for the largest size. A scheme file without them
              gives the stock schemes at the same steps, as the panels do.
   colours    the words in the scheme's "Primary Button Text" colour (223 213
              245, the lavender); the heading and the pointer in the lit purple
              (182 145 234, the art's bracket and rail-lit colour, the panels'
              titles) or the speaker's team colour; the rule under a heading in
              the rail's purple (133 98 178), as a menu's title has it.
   heading    cl_h9_chatbubble_name 1: the speaker's name above the words in the
              panels' title style: the lit colour, centred, a one-pixel rule in
              the rail's purple under it.
   team       cl_h9_chatbubble_teamcolor 1: with a team game on (the GameMode
              byte not 0), the name and the pointer take the speaker's team
              colour from the table the scoreboard, the team box and the flag
              icons use (iTeamColors): team 1 (MSR) blue 125 165 210, team 2
              (Insurgent) red 200 90 70, 3 yellow, 4 green; no team or another
              mode: the lit purple. The frame itself is the art and stays
              purple. There is no role colour: no existing panel has one.
   words only cl_h9_chatbubble_box 0: no art and no box; the words in the same
              colours with a one-pixel black shadow, as a notice without art.
   art missing  the panels' plain dark box (16 10 24) with a rim in the rail's
              purple, as every H9 panel falls back.
 Text is drawn as the engine's VGUI font draws it, byte by byte: a multi-byte
 UTF-8 character shows as its bytes, as it does in the scoreboard and the
 menus. The wrap and the cut never split one.


 5. THE CASES
 ------------
   Speaker behind a wall or a door    no box (the line of sight), whoever is
                                      aimed at; his line is kept and shows if
                                      he comes into sight before its time
   Viewer dead, eliminated, spectating  no box; the server sends H9Say only to a
                                      living viewer, and the client draws only
                                      while the HUD's own status does (not dead
                                      for the HUD, not spectating)
   Speaker dead, gone, cloaked, out of the match
                                      the plugin takes his line down; the client
                                      shows nobody who is not drawn
   A line while someone else is aimed at  kept; shows when its speaker is aimed
                                      at, until its time is up
   Two speakers on the crosshair      the nearer to the middle of the body
   Text at the screen's edge          the box is kept 4 px inside it; the
                                      pointer stays on the head
   A long line or name                wrapped to the box's width (30 percent of
                                      the screen, 40 for the largest text) and
                                      cut to cl_h9_chatbubble_lines lines with
                                      "..."; a name wider than the box is cut
                                      with "..."; a word wider than the box is
                                      broken between characters
   UTF-8                              never split; drawn byte by byte (above)
   A speaker who leaves               the plugin sends ttl 0 for his slot; a new
                                      player of that slot never inherits a line
   Map change, new server             every line is dropped (InitHUDData)
   The top of the screen              the box stays beneath the stack of notices,
                                      the match bar and the row of marks, and
                                      inside the safe area of the panels
   HUD hidden, scoreboard, intermission  no box (nohud, the scoreboard,
                                      the intermission); the boxes hide within
                                      0.3 s of the HUD stopping its draw
   Scoped or zoomed                   the tolerance is in pixels, so a zoomed
                                      view aims as finely as it looks
   The typing sprite up and words over it
                                      the box rests over the top of the sprite,
                                      not over the head; the hudmessage's words
                                      the same
   A line sent as the chat line closes  the plugin takes the sprite down as the
                                      line is said: the words come under none
   The sprite hidden or not wanted    no lift (cl_h9_chatbubble_typing 0, float
                                      chat off, the speaker gone): the box rests
                                      over the head
   A speaker who left, his sprite with him
                                      the plugin removes the sprite; the entity
                                      is out of the packet (and may be another
                                      thing by now): no lift
   His own sprite, in third person    over his head; his own line is at the top
   An own line nobody asked for       not sent; a line that is sent is shown


 6. THE SWITCHES
 ---------------
 Each does one thing. The client's are saved cvars (FCVAR_ARCHIVE); a changed
 default reaches new players only. cl_h9_chatbubble_list prints them all.

 SERVER (ts_floatchat; fc_enabled 0 turns everything off, and the existing fc_*
 and h9_floatchat_maxchars are in COMMANDS.txt):
   h9_floatchat_words 1        0: no words over heads for any client
   h9_floatchat_hud 1          0: a client that cannot draw the words itself is
                               sent none
   h9_floatchat_typing 1       0: the typing bubble sprite is never shown
   h9_floatchat_bubble 1       0: no client is sent H9Say; all get the hudmessage
   h9_floatchat_bubble_hold 0  seconds a line stays for the clients that draw it
                               (1 to 30); 0: fc_hold + 0.04 a character
   h9_floatchat_bubble_maxchars 96   most characters of a line sent (12 to 150)
   h9_floatchat_bubble_team 1  1: a say_team line goes to the speaker's team
                               only; 0: to everyone, as say goes
   fc_range, fc_hold           as for the hudmessage; the range and the hold (when
                               h9_floatchat_bubble_hold is 0) apply to H9Say too
   fc_self 0                   1: every player is sent his own line, whatever his
                               game asks (H9Say, or the hudmessage to a game that
                               cannot draw the words); 0: only a player whose
                               game asks, with cl_h9_chatbubble_own 1, is
   fc_red/green/blue, fc_y_tweak, h9_floatchat_maxchars, fc_scale, fc_rise
                               the hudmessage's colour, height and limit (not
                               the box's), and the sprite's size and height: the
                               box and the hudmessage's words rest over the
                               sprite wherever these put it

 CLIENT:
   cl_h9_chatbubble 1          master: 0 leaves the words to the hudmessage
   cl_h9_chatbubble_aimed 1    1 only the speaker aimed at, 0 every one in view
   cl_h9_chatbubble_aim 4      aim tolerance, percent of the screen height (0-25)
   cl_h9_chatbubble_range 0    farthest a line shows, units; 0: the server's
   cl_h9_chatbubble_box 1      1 the panel art, 0 the words alone
   cl_h9_chatbubble_name 0     1 the name as a heading
   cl_h9_chatbubble_teamcolor 1  1 the team's colour on the name and the pointer
   cl_h9_chatbubble_pointer 1  1 the pointer to the head
   cl_h9_chatbubble_fade 1     1 fade out over the last 0.8 s
   cl_h9_chatbubble_linger 0.35  seconds a box stays when its speaker is lost for a moment, then fades
   cl_h9_chatbubble_lines 3    most lines of words in a box (1 to 5)
   cl_h9_chatbubble_hold 0     seconds a line stays (1 to 30); 0: the server's
   cl_h9_chatbubble_scale 1    text size, 0.5 to 2: 0.55, 0.7, 0.9 (normal), 1.4
   cl_h9_chatbubble_team 1     1 shows lines said in team chat
   cl_h9_chatbubble_own 0      1 asks the server for your own line, shown near the
                               top of the screen; the server's fc_self 1 sends it
                               to everyone. Needs cl_h9_chatbubble 1.
   cl_h9_chatbubble_typing 1   0 hides the server's typing bubble sprite (the
                               client leaves it out of the frame by its model)
   cl_h9_chatbubble_list       prints these and their values
   cl_h9_chatbubble_test <words>  the words over the player the crosshair is on
                               (a player in view within the aim tolerance, at
                               least 4 percent), else over you, for 10 seconds:
                               tries the look with no server plugin
   _h9chatbubble               userinfo: 0, 1 or 1s (section 1); written at most
                               once in 2 seconds (the engine ignores a userinfo
                               update that follows another within 0.6 s and
                               reads the key at its next resend, about a second
                               later), with the latest values of the switches


 7. LIMITS AND SAFETY
 --------------------
 A server is untrusted: the client's limits are its own. At most 24 H9Say
 messages a second are read; a line is cleaned (controls to spaces, runs of
 spaces to one, never inside a multi-byte character), cut to 160 bytes and held
 at most 30 seconds; a speaker has one line (a newer one replaces it); at most
 4 boxes show; a box is wrapped to at most 5 lines; a text with no word in it is
 dropped. Nothing a message says can run a command, move the cursor or take a
 key: the boxes are VGUI panels that never take the mouse or the keyboard
 (CH9Surface). The line of sight is tested by the client, so H9Say shows no
 one through a wall; but the text of a line reaches a game within range, whatever
 stands between (a modified client could read it), which is why a team line goes
 to the team only.


 8. THE WEB CLIENT
 -----------------
 The same cl_dll sources are compiled for the browser: h9_chatbubble.cpp uses
 only the engine's API (GetEntityByIndex, pTriAPI->WorldToScreen, the event
 API's trace, the cvars, the VGUI panels of vgui_H9Panel.cpp), with no
 Windows-only call, so the web client's client.dll draws the boxes as the
 native one does (its VGUI fonts are the browser's canvas, Arial: the text is
 not Square 721 there, as docs\H9_VGUI.txt 3d says of every panel). No engine
 patch changes, so xash3d-h9-patches\WEB_PARITY.json has nothing to add. The
 web client gets it with the client.dll of its next build-webclient.cmd.


 9. WHAT WAS NOT CHECKED IN A GAME
 ---------------------------------
 The plugin compiles (amxxpc, no warnings) and the client sources pass a
 syntax-only compile (cl /Zs, the build's flags and include paths); nothing was
 run in a game. Not seen: that the trace flags behave as read (PM_STUDIO_IGNORE
 and PM_GLASS_IGNORE with the event API's EV_PlayerTrace), that WorldToScreen is
 right inside the HUD's draw (the marks and the team dots use it there), that
 the body rectangle fits every model, the look of the box at every resolution
 and size, the placing near the screen's edges and the stack, and the number of
 art bitmaps made. Also not seen: that the typing sprite reaches HUD_AddEntity
 with its owner and its height where the lift reads them, that the box clears
 it at every distance and look (it is lifted straight up, so a steep look down
 leaves less gap), the rise and fall as the sprite comes and goes, that the
 server sees 1s and keeps it through the engine's userinfo flood penalty, and
 that the own line shows and the hudmessage words clear the sprite. Section 10
 is a check to make in a game.


10. A CHECK IN A GAME
---------------------
   1. Join a server running the new ts_floatchat with the new client.dll. In
      the console, cl_h9_chatbubble_list: every switch, _h9chatbubble 1.
   2. With a second player (or a bot's say through the console), say a line.
      Aim at the speaker: a purple banner box with the words rests over his
      head, a pointer under it. Turn fast: the box stays on his head. Aim
      away: it goes. Aim back within the line's time: it returns.
   3. A long line wraps to three lines and ends in "...". Say one more line
      from a third player while the second is aimed at: nothing runs into it;
      aim at the third and his line is there.
   4. Stand a wall between you: no box. Open a door or step out: the box.
   5. Die, or be eliminated: no box. The speaker dies: his line goes.
   6. cl_h9_chatbubble_name 1: the name over the words in the team's colour.
      _box 0: words alone with a shadow. _aimed 0: every speaker in view.
      _scale 0.7 and 1.4: smaller and larger text. _typing 0: the "..." sprite
      over a player writing is gone. _team 0: say_team lines are not shown.
   7. cl_h9_chatbubble 0: the hudmessage words return at once (the userinfo
      key changes), and cl_h9_chatbubble 1 puts the box back.
   8. On a server with h9_floatchat_bubble 0, or in a client of another kind,
      the words show as a hudmessage.
   9. cl_h9_chatbubble_test hello there: the words over the player under the
      crosshair, else over you, with no server plugin.
  10. Resize the window (or change cl_h9_vgui_scale) and look at the box again;
      look at the console for "panel sizes drawn" if boxes lose their art.
  11. The sprite: a second player says a line, then opens his chat line again
      while it shows: his box rises over the "..." sprite, never through it, and
      falls when he sends or cancels. Sending a line takes the sprite down as the
      words appear. cl_h9_chatbubble_typing 0: no sprite, no lift.
  12. The own line: cl_h9_chatbubble_list shows cl_h9_chatbubble_own 0 and
      _h9chatbubble 1. Say something: no box near the top of your screen.
      cl_h9_chatbubble_own 1: the list shows _h9chatbubble 1s within 2 seconds;
      say something: a box near the top. Flick the cvar 0 1 0 1 quickly: the
      list ends on 1s. A server with fc_self 1 sends you your line whatever you
      set; with a game of cl_h9_chatbubble 0 and fc_self 1 you get the hudmessage.
