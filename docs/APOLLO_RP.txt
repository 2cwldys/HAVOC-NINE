APOLLO RP ON HAVOC 9 -- what it is, what a player sees, what it needs
======================================================================

Apollo RP (ARP) is a roleplay plugin set for AMX Mod X: a wallet, a bank, jobs with salaries, items, shops, jails and
a HUD. H9 carries it whole in apollo-rp\ (apollo-rp\ORIGIN.txt says where it came from and lists every change H9 made
to it, items 1 to 41), builds fourteen of its plugins for The Specialists and lists them in plugins.ini, ARP_Core first.


1. WHERE IT RUNS
----------------

  * Only on a roleplay map: a name that starts with rp_ or tsrp_, or holds _rp_ or _tsrp_, in any case. H9's are
    h9_tsrp_hive and h9_tsrp_newbyzantium. On any other map ARP_Core logs one stand-down line and nothing else of
    ARP runs: no HUD, no commands, no menus. ts_matchmode stands down on the same maps (tsm_enabled 0).
  * build.bat builds and deploys it with the other plugins (also on its own: build.bat apollo), syncs the plugins'
    includes, and copies apollo-rp\base\addons\amxmodx\configs\arp into the game's configs\arp.


2. WHAT A PLAYER SEES
---------------------

  * THE HUD, at the left of the screen, refreshed every second or two: the title (arp_hud_title in
    configs\arp\arp.cfg, where #hostname# puts in the server's name, so name the server for its city), the wallet,
    the bank, the payday countdown, the salary and the job, and the lines other plugins add: jail status, the zone, the
    blood alcohol level. There are four HUDs: the first is this, the second is the needs (hunger; below), the third is the
    out-of-character chat (arp_ooc 2) and the fourth the short notices (what you look at, ammo loaded up). Their places
    and colours are arp_hud1_x, _y, _r, _g and _b in configs\arp\arp.cfg (the second to fourth HUDs likewise). No bot is
    sent one. It comes in two forms.
    - As PANELS, to a player whose game draws H9 VGUI and whose cl_h9_arphud is 1 (the default): each HUD is a panel on
      H9's purple art, the first with the title as its heading and the lines under it, label and value, the third and
      second the same with their first line as the heading (a need is a row with a bar, below), the fourth a banner that
      stays three seconds. A panel is shown only while it has lines, takes the size its lines need (a long line is
      wrapped), starts under the notices' stack and the HUD's own corner text (the first panel also under TS's weapon
      bar, below), and is hidden with the scoreboard, a menu opened over it, the buy menu, nohud
      and the intermission; cl_h9_vgui_scale sizes it as it does every H9 panel. The server sends it in the user
      message H9ArpHud, reliably, when a HUD changes and every arp_hud_refresh seconds (10) otherwise; the game keeps
      what it has until something else comes. The text HUD's colours (arp_hudN_r, _g, _b) are not used: the panels are
      H9's purple, or the colour the player gave them (cl_h9_vgui_r, _g, _b). The dead or spectating player's panel says so (the same "You are currently dead/spectating" line). A player who is shown the credit sign
      (section 9) gets, in the text of the lines, the single byte A2 (the cent sign) in place of each "$" that a digit follows.
    - WHERE THE PANELS SIT. In the safe area of the screen (clear of the chat and TS's HUD along the bottom), each at the
      nearest of three columns and three rows to the place its HUD has (arp_hudN_x and _y). A panel of the top row starts
      32 pixels down at the left (104 at the right, clear of the round time and the kill feed; under the notices' stack in
      the middle), and the panels of a column stack. The top panel of the LEFT column also stands under TS's weapon
      select bar, the five columns of the weapons carried at the top left of the screen, which is drawn in pixels of its
      own (cl_showequipment 1 to 3 draws it faint, 0 hides it until the select opens): the panel starts 8 pixels below the
      lowest weapon of the columns it covers. The bar is measured as it is drawn with the select open (kung fu's place
      counted, though the faint bar leaves it out), shut or open, so with cl_showequipment 1 to 3 the panel does not move
      when the select opens or shuts, and slides (a fifth of a second) only when the weapons carried change; with
      cl_showequipment 0 it slides down when the select opens and back when it shuts. With so many weapons, or so low a
      screen, that the panel cannot stand under the bar, it is held inside the safe area, over the bar's lower rows. While
      the select is open (the bar drawn in full, with its headers and the cursor's name) that panel is also drawn smaller, a
      step down the font ladder and three quarters of the art's scale, and has its own size again when the select shuts
      (cl_h9_arphud_shrink 0 keeps it as it is). The other panels do not look at the bar.
    - As the AMX Mod X TEXT HUD (a hudmessage, as before), to every other player: an older game, a game with
      cl_h9_arphud 0 or cl_h9_vgui 0, a server with arp_hud_panel 0 or h9_vgui 0.
    Each player chooses with cl_h9_arphud and can switch every panel (cl_h9_arphud_left, _top, _side, _notice), every
    kind of line of the first (_title, _wallet, _bank, _payday, _salary, _job, _status, _extra), the needs
    (cl_h9_arphud_needs) and the plaque (cl_h9_arphud_plaque) off alone; cl_h9_arphud_list prints them. The server's arp_hud_panel 0 gives everyone the text
    HUD. arp_hudstate, from the server's console or an ARP admin's, says who is sent which and why, and the last lines
    each was sent (COMMANDS.txt).
  * THE NEEDS, at the top right: the second HUD holds a line for each need that is on, for a living player whose
    character has loaded. ARP has two, hunger and thirst (ARP_HungerMod; arp_hunger_enabled and arp_thirst_enabled, 1 in the plugin
    and in the shipped arp.cfg): the text "Hunger: 42%" and "Thirst: 18%", 0 (fed, quenched) to 100, and from 90 the lines "Starving"
    (hunger: no salary, as in stock ARP) and "Dehydrated" (thirst is only a meter) under them. A player with panels sees a panel headed "Needs" at the top right (arp_hud2_x 1.0 and _y 0.0 put it
    there) with a row for each need: its label, a bar and the number. The bar fills to the value and is green at 0,
    yellow half way to 75 and red from 75 up (very hungry: the speed is x 0.75), with a mark at 75 and a mark at 90; the
    number is red from 75; "Starving" and "Dehydrated" are red rows under the bars. A player with the text HUD has the same
    words as the second HUD's text, flush right at the top of the screen, in its colour (arp_hud2_r, _g, _b): hunger no
    longer stands among the lines at the left. Nothing is shown of a need that is off, for a dead or spectating player or
    before the character has loaded, and cl_h9_arphud_needs 0 hides the panel. The panel is made for more than one
    need. ARP_HungerMod keeps two, Hunger and Thirst (arp_hunger_enabled, arp_thirst_enabled, both 1 in the shipped arp.cfg); a plugin that
    has another need adds its line to the second HUD from its HUD_Render handler with ARP_AddNeedItem(id, "Label", value) (include\ApolloRP_HUD.inc: the line kind HUDK_NEED,
    the text "<label>: <0 to 100>%"), and every panel shows one more row, with the same bar and colours, and the text
    HUD one more line. A line of that kind whose text is not in that form is drawn as text.
  * A dropped item names itself: a player who looks toward an item lying on the floor, from within 120 units, reads its name and the number of
    them in the fourth HUD (the notice banner for a player with panels, the centre text for any other): "Soda x3", and under it "Press use
    (default e) to pick up" from within 50 units, where use picks it up. The item's box is too small for the crosshair to hit, so the look is a
    cone of about 14 degrees from the eye, tried four times a second; the item nearest the middle of it wins, and nothing shows while the crosshair
    is on a player, an NPC or a door (they name themselves). The same for the items a map has put on the floor (ARP_Core.sma, H9_AimedItem, GetMsg).
    A game that says _h9vgui 7 and has the HUD as panels is also sent the item's picture, a line of the kind HUDK_ICON ("icon:<the item's name>"),
    and draws it above the notice (the sheet sprites/h9_arpicons.spr: docs\ARP_INVENTORY_WINDOW.txt, 4; cl_h9_arphud_icon 0 leaves it out).
    arp_h9_itemhover (1 by default) switches it: 0 is stock Apollo RP, which says nothing of a dropped item.
  * WHAT A PLAYER LOOKS AT, THE PLAQUE (arp_h9_plaque, 1 by default). A property door, an NPC and a player each say what they are, within 100 units, as the look-at line always
    did. ARP_Core's GetMsg builds it each time the look-at runs and sends it in the notice HUD. A player with panels (arp_hud4_type 2, which the shipped arp.cfg sets) sees a
    PLAQUE: a plate of one width on H9's purple art in the notice's place (the middle of the screen, low, clear of the crosshair), its heading in upper case with a rail under it,
    then a label and a value for each field, the affiliation and the status as badges, a restriction in red and a hint in the dim colour.
    - A PROPERTY DOOR. The establishment (the property's external name) is the heading. OWNER: the owner's name, with his job in brackets while he is in the game and has one
      (Unemployed is none); STATE PROPERTY when there is no owner and no price; FOR SALE and the price when there is a price and no owner (a property its owner has put up
      shows his name, and FOR SALE with the price under it). AFFILIATION, from the property's access letters: the police letter (arp_police_access, a) or g (the Lord
      Minister's own) is the MINISTRY OF DEFENSE, the medical letter (arp_medical_access, b) the MEDICAL DIVISION, both "Ministry of Defense / Medical Division", the admin letter
      alone ADMINISTRATION, no letter CIVILIAN. STATUS: SEALED when the property is locked, OPEN when it is not. RESTRICTED, in red, says who may when the property is locked and
      the player has neither a key of it, nor the property, nor a letter in common with its access (the test the use key makes): MINISTRY OF DEFENSE, MEDICAL DIVISION, THE LORD
      MINISTER, AUTHORISED PERSONNEL, or KEYHOLDERS for a property that names no letter. With a price the hint "Say /buy to purchase" ends it. A door of no property says nothing.
    - AN NPC. Its name is the heading, then ROLE (SHOP, BANK, DOCTOR, GUN SHOP or ATM, from the handler its plugin gave it), the owner and affiliation of the property its
      npcs.ini block names with a property line (none of H9's does), and the hint "Press use (default e) to use".
    - A PLAYER (arp_hover_message, 1). His name, JOB (his rank) when he has one, and AFFILIATION from the access letters of his job by the same rule (a Cadet to a Lord Commander
      are the Ministry of Defense, the M- ranks the Medical Division, an Administrator Administration, anyone else Civilian). A player who is not alive is not described.
    - STABILITY. A player who keeps looking at the same thing is left alone for a quarter of a second at a time, and the core sends the same lines again only every 1.5 seconds; every
      door of one property gives the same lines, so looking from one part of a door, or from one door of a property to another, changes nothing on the screen. A plaque that changes
      (another property, an owner who has just bought) is drawn again in place and does not fade out between. It stays 2.5 seconds after the last message and then fades. The game
      shows nothing of it to a dead or spectating player, with the scoreboard, a menu at the left, the buy menu, nohud and the intermission, as every panel.
    - OTHER GAMES. A game that draws no panels and a player with cl_h9_arphud 0 get the text HUD's fourth line, and every player gets the text in the centre of the screen when
      arp_hud4_type is 1: the stock lines (the name, "Owner:", "Price: $N. Say /buy to purchase.", "Status: Locked" or "Unlocked"; "Name:" and "Job:" for a player) with an
      "Affiliation:" line before the status or after the job. A game of H9's that does not know the plaque (an older client.dll) draws its lines as plain lines of the notice,
      each of which reads on its own ("Owner: State property"). The player's cl_h9_arphud_plaque 0 does the same on a game that has it. arp_h9_plaque 0 is stock Apollo RP: the
      text it always had, no Affiliation line and no plaque.
    - THE DATA. docs\ARP_CONTENT.txt, section 9, is the properties of H9's two maps (each door by its name in the map; a property has all the doors of its building, which show one
      plaque and share one lock). A door the map did not name cannot be bound by the file.
  * No names over players (arp_h9_hidenames, 1 by default; ts_matchmode): TS draws the name of the player the crosshair is on, red for anyone there
    being no sides on a roleplay map. An H9 game leaves those names out while Apollo RP is in use (the H9Look message, docs\H9_VGUI.txt 4j), so a
    player is known only by what Apollo RP says of him; 0 keeps TS's names. cl_h9_aimplaque 0 draws them regardless.
  * Welcome lines in chat when a player is in game, and the chat commands (/menu, /items, /911 and the rest).
  * MENUS, once there is something to show. A menu is built from content: /items lists what a character carries (as the inventory window for a game that has it, section 9),
    /menu lists the entries plugins add to it (ARP_H9Menu adds Inventory, My money and job, Jobs on this map and Commands), a shop, the bank, an ATM, the doctor, the gun shop and the phone are
    NPCs, a job or a fine is an offer from another player, the jail menu needs a job that may jail. A map with no
    NPCs, no items and no jobs but Unemployed therefore opens none: /items says there is nothing, /menu says it is
    empty, and the HUD, the welcome lines and the chat commands are all a player gets. The content of H9's two maps
    is described in ARP_CONTENT.txt (the Ministry of Defense ranks as jobs, the items, the shops and where the NPCs
    stand).
  * AS PANELS. A player running H9's client sees ARP's menus as H9 VGUI panels (docs\H9_VGUI.txt, 1e); cl_h9_arpvgui 0
    gives him ARP's text menus instead, for ARP only, and the server's h9_vgui 0 gives every player the text menus. A
    client that does not send the setting gets the panels. Every other player gets the classic text menus. arp_h9_vgui (1 by default) is the
    server's switch for ARP's panels alone (its menus, the inventory window): 0 gives every player the classic text menus, whatever his game.
  * DOORS AND BREAKABLES. arp_goddoors 1 (the default) makes every func_door and func_door_rotating unbreakable. arp_godwindows
    (0 by default) would do the same for every func_breakable (manhole covers, vents, glass, crates), so those can be broken
    unless a server sets it to 1. Both are in configs\arp\arp.cfg.


3. A CHARACTER, ITS KEY AND THE HAVOC9.ORG ACCOUNT
--------------------------------------------------

  A character (wallet, bank, job, items, keys, the property it owns) is kept in the server's own ARP database under a
  key, the authid its rows were made with. H9XASH makes an authid per server (ID_ and an MD5 of the player's id and
  the server's IPv4 address), so a server that changes address would have made every player a new character. A player
  whose game is linked to a havoc9.org account and proves it (h9_presence, h9_presence_challenge 1) keeps his: the
  table arp_ids ties each authid to its account, and a join under another authid finds the character through it.
  Details, limits and going back: apollo-rp\ARP_IDS.txt. arp_account_wait (30 seconds in configs\arp\arp.cfg; 0 turns
  the account out) is how long a player no row knows waits for the proof, the HUD saying his account is loading.
  The database is the server's: another server has its own and starts the player fresh, unless the two share one.


4. THE DATABASE
---------------

  data\sqlite3\arp.sq3 in the game folder, made and filled by the plugins; it is a live file (never in git). Tables are
  made with CREATE TABLE IF NOT EXISTS at every start (arp_ids too: nothing existing is altered or dropped). Values
  that reach SQL are quoted (ARP_SqlEscape, with the SQL module's own quoting) and an authid has 35 characters of room.
  Back it up with the server stopped: copy the file. A map's own .sql file in configs\arp\maps\<map>\ is run by ARP_Core
  itself at every start of that map, after the tables are made and before the jobs, properties and doors are read, so its
  rows are there in the same map load (SQLite; the cvar arp_mapsql_autoload, 1, in arp.cfg, turns it off). Only a file in
  which every statement is  INSERT OR IGNORE INTO arp_jobs, arp_property or arp_doors VALUES (...);  on a line of its own is
  run (the rule: docs\ARP_CONTENT.txt, section 2). The log has one line for each start; a file the rule refuses is left to
  the console command arp_config, entry 3, and the log says which line and why.


5. ON XASH3D
------------

  * AMX Mod X's client_disconnect fires on H9XASH (the amxmodx_mm.dll change 7, amxmodx_mm.dll.patch), so ARP saves and
    clears a player when he leaves.
  * The SQLite module loads through the plugins' own #pragma loadlib.
  * ARP makes no assumption about a Steam id: ID_ authids pass every test it has.
  * AMX Mod X's TrieClear removes every key of a trie, the "" key too. ARP's trie lists (include\celltravtrie.inc) keep the list of
    their keys under that key, so TravTrieClear puts it back (ORIGIN item 22); a trie cleared any other way takes no key again.
  * The SQL module hands the answers of threaded queries that finished in one frame over last in, first out. Nothing in ARP may
    depend on the order of two answers (ORIGIN item 23; the loads count their answers, g_GotInfo).
  * client_disconnect is deprecated in this AMX Mod X (the compiler says so for six plugins) and still called, next to
    client_disconnected: the plugins keep it.


6. TO CHECK ON A ROLEPLAY MAP (nothing here has been run in a game yet)
------------------------------------------------------------------------

  1. Start on h9_tsrp_hive with the live database backed up. The AMX Mod X log has no ARP error and no "stand-down" line
     for the map; amx_plugins lists the fourteen ARP plugins running.
  1a. The same log has one line "[ARP] map SQL: 267 statements, 0 errors, jobs now 26, properties now 91, doors now 155" for the
     start (the file's 26 jobs, 90 properties and 151 doors, and the jail's property and four doors the database has already; the same line at every later start
     of the map: nothing is added twice), and arp_joblist lists 26 jobs, Unemployed first, in the order of the map's .sql file. h9_tsrp_newbyzantium the same,
     its jobs table made by the start, with 149 statements, properties now 30 and doors now 93.
  2. Join: within a few seconds the HUD is at the top left (title, wallet, bank, payday, salary, job) and two ARP
     welcome lines are in chat. h9_overflow.log gets no new "Datagram overflowed" line for a bot.
  2a. With H9's client: the log has "[ARP] HUD: <name> is sent the HUD panel: _h9arphud is 1" within a few seconds of the
     join and then "[ARP] HUD panel: first update sent to <name> (HUD 1: ...)"; arp_hudstate lists him as PANEL with the
     last lines sent. The HUD is a panel at the top left. cl_h9_arphud 0 gives him the text HUD (arp_hudstate says TEXT HUD
     within two seconds), arp_hud_panel 0 on the server gives everyone it, and each cl_h9_arphud_* set to 0 takes that part
     off. cl_h9_arphud_list says what the game holds and why. A player on any other client is a TEXT HUD in arp_hudstate.
  3. With content: /items and /menu show their entries, each NPC opens its menu, as H9 panels with cl_h9_arpvgui 1 and as
     text menus with 0 (the other H9 panels unchanged), and h9_vgui 0 on the server gives text menus to everyone.
  3a. arp_menu (or say /menu) opens the ARP Client Menu with four entries: Inventory (runs say /items), My money and job (a chat
     line with wallet, bank, job, salary and the payday), Jobs on this map (arp_joblist) and Commands (arp_help). Leaving the
     game and joining again brings the items back (say /items), and /items lists what was bought at a shop NPC.
  4. Earn money, leave and join again: it is there. Change the map: it is still there.
  5. Identity, on a COPY of the database: after the proof arp_ids holds the authid with the account; set one arp_users
     authid to ID_OLD and add (ID_OLD, the account) to arp_ids: joining with that account loads the old character
     once the proof is in. arp_account_wait 0: no wait and no arp_ids writes.
  6. On a map that is not a roleplay one: the stand-down line, and no ARP traffic.
  7. The inventory and the exchange of goods (section 9): docs\ARP_INVENTORY_CHECKS.txt.

8. JOBS AND SETUP BY MAP
------------------------

  WHAT IS SHARED AND WHAT IS A MAP'S OWN
    * Shared by every roleplay map of a server (one ARP database): the characters (wallet, bank, items) and the account table
      arp_ids.
    * A map's own, always: its NPCs (configs\arp\maps\<map>\npcs.ini), jail cells (jailmod.ini), zones (zones.ini),
      robberies (rob.ini), and its properties, doors and keys (the tables carry the map's name).
    * A map's own when you configure it: its JOBS (below). Without that, jobs are shared: one table, arp_jobs.
    H9's two roleplay maps (h9_tsrp_hive, h9_tsrp_newbyzantium) have jobs of their own, so the Ministry of Defense ranks are
    theirs alone: a server that runs its own maps next to them is not made to use them.

  HOW A MAP GETS ITS OWN JOBS
    1. Put a file arp.ini in the map's folder, configs\arp\maps\<map>\arp.ini (the same folder as npcs.ini). ARP reads it
       after the server's configs\arp\arp.ini, so anything in it wins on that map. One line is enough:

           arp_table_jobs "arp_jobs_my_city"

       The name is the jobs table of that map: letters, digits and underscores only, made at the first start (ARP adds the
       job Unemployed to a jobs table that has none). A good name is arp_jobs_<mapname>; H9's maps use exactly that
       (apollo-rp\base\addons\amxmodx\configs\arp\maps\h9_tsrp_hive\arp.ini). A line that starts with ; is a comment.
    2. Put the map's jobs into that table, either way:
         * a console command, as an admin, on that map:   arp_addjob <name> <salary> <access letters>
           (arp_joblist lists the jobs of the map, arp_setjob <player> <job> gives one);
         * or an SQL file configs\arp\maps\<map>\<map>.sql, written against the DEFAULT table names (arp_jobs, arp_property,
           arp_doors): ARP runs it by itself at every start of the map and swaps in the map's own table names, so the file
           needs no change when the map has its own jobs table. One statement to a line, each one of this form, which does
           no harm when it is run again:
               INSERT OR IGNORE INTO arp_jobs VALUES ('Cadet',25,'<access letters>');
           (the whole rule: docs\ARP_CONTENT.txt, section 2). A file that breaks it, any file with arp_mapsql_autoload 0 and
           any file on MySQL (which needs INSERT IGNORE) is run by an admin with the console command arp_config, menu entry
           "Load Map SQL Data"; what that adds is read at the next map load.
    3. Start the map (the table name and the file are read when the map loads). The jobs show in arp_joblist and in the job HUD.

  OTHER WAYS TO SET IT UP
    * Several maps with the same jobs: give them the same table name in their arp.ini (arp_table_jobs "arp_jobs_msr" in each).
    * Every map the same, the default: no arp.ini in the folder, or none that names a jobs table: the shared arp_jobs.
    * A different default for the whole server: change arp_table_jobs in configs\arp\arp.ini (it is the shared table's name).
    * More of a map's own: arp_table_users and arp_table_items are read from a map's arp.ini the same way (separate
      characters and items for that map). H9's maps do not use them, and nothing here has been run in a game.

  WHAT A PLAYER GETS, AND A RANK ON ANOTHER MAP
    A character carries one job name. On a map whose jobs table has it, he has the job (salary, powers, the HUD line). On a
    map whose table has no job of that name he is Unemployed there, with a chat line saying his job is not offered on this
    map, and the job he came with is kept: it is written back as it was when he leaves, unless he takes another job on that
    map. So a visit to another map does not cost him his rank, and he has it again where it is offered.

  LOOK AT IT
    The jobs tables are in the database (data\sqlite3\arp.sq3): arp_jobs, arp_jobs_<map> ... (name, salary, access). The
    jobs of the characters are in arp_users (jobname). Open a COPY of the file with any SQLite tool to read them; back the
    file up with the server stopped before any edit by hand.

9. THE INVENTORY AND THE EXCHANGE OF GOODS
------------------------------------------

  ARP_Inventory (ORIGIN items 29 to 33, 35 and 36) arranges a player's items in cells and draws them in a window. docs\ARP_INVENTORY.txt is the full text (the rules,
  the hooks a plugin can use, the protocol) and docs\ARP_INVENTORY_CHECKS.txt says what to look at in a game.

  * CELLS AND STACKS. A player has arp_h9_inv_slots cells (32; 8 to 96; 0 is no limit) and each holds one kind of item, up to arp_h9_inv_stack of it (20;
    configs\arp\inventory.ini names a limit for an item, 1 for one that does not stack). The truth stays ARP's: the count of each kind (the table
    arp_items, unchanged) is what a player owns, and the cells are its arrangement for as long as he is in the game, made when his character loads: kinds in
    item order, full stacks and then the remainder. He MOVES a cell to another, COMBINES stacks of one item (as many as fit, the rest stays), SWAPS two
    kinds, SPLITS a stack and TIDIES; an action (use, drop, give, show) acts on the cell it was made from.
  * THE WINDOW. A player whose game has it (_h9vgui 6) is given the inventory window by /items, /inventory and "h9inv open" in place of the text menu,
    also when his inventory is empty (the grid, with nothing in it). Every other player keeps the text menu, and the cell rules, which are the server's,
    apply to him as well. Its right-click menu is USE, DROP ONE, DROP ALL, GIVE TO ..., SHOW TO ... and INFO, and each one runs ARP's own path (the events
    Item_Use, Item_Drop, Item_Give and Item_Show, the item's handler, the g_ItemUse mutex), so a plugin that hooks an item event keeps working. Dragging a
    tile out of the window drops it.
  * FULL. The use key on a pack on the floor takes what fits and leaves the rest in the pack; a shop, a bank, a doctor or a gun shop refuses before it takes
    the money; a give is all or nothing; an add nobody asked about (a reward, an admin command) falls at the player's feet. A limit lowered while the
    server runs takes nothing from a player: he keeps his cells, and is over capacity until he is back under the limit.
  * DROP. The pack is made first and the items leave the player after it, so nothing is destroyed. (ARP_Core made them leave first, and made no pack at all
    when arp_performance was 10 or less.)
  * SHOW TO. The item is shown to a player within 150 units and in sight: the Item_Show event first, then ARP's chat lines; the other player also gets a
    banner ("<NAME> SHOWS YOU", the item, its description) and the shower a note in the window. Nothing moves.
  * THE EXCHANGE OF GOODS. A player presses the use key on another player and offers him an exchange; when he accepts (the use key on the first, or ACCEPT), both see
    a window with an offer each: up to 12 entries (a cell and a count) and an amount of money from the wallet, never the bank. The cells offered are held (nothing
    can use, drop, give, move or merge them), ANY change by either side breaks BOTH seals and the seal button is dead for 3 seconds, and when both have sealed the
    server checks it all again (the counts, the money, the fit of both inventories on copies) and swaps atomically, or closes with the reason and moves nothing.
    Never with a bot, a player who is cuffed or in jail, a game without the window, or a player already in an exchange. It breaks off on range
    (arp_h9_trade_range 250), death, disconnect, jail or cuffs, the map changing and an idle time (arp_h9_trade_timeout 120). Each swap is logged with both
    names and authids, the items, the money and the map.
  * THE SEARCH OF A HANDCUFFED PLAYER. A player presses the use key on a player who is handcuffed or in jail (ARP_JailMod's H9_Restrained): a window opens on his screen, a game
    that says _h9vgui 8, with the other player's cells beside his own, and what he takes goes from the one's inventory to the other's at once: as much as fits, nothing made or
    lost, one log line for each take, the other player told in chat. It ends when the cuffs come off, the other dies or leaves, the searcher goes beyond arp_h9_search_range (150),
    dies, is handcuffed or closes his window. One searcher at a time for a player, never while either is in an exchange. A plugin can veto it (the event Player_Search: "police
    only", a cooldown). A game that says less than 8 gets "That player cannot make an exchange now."; arp_h9_search 0 is that for everybody.
  * CREDITS. arp_h9_credits (1) words the money of the window and of this plugin's messages as credits ("1,250 credits"); 0 words it as cash ("$1,250").
    The wallet and the bank are ARP's: only the words change.
  * THE CREDIT SIGN. With arp_h9_credits 1 a player whose game says _h9vgui 7 (the client with its inventory window wanted) is shown the cent sign instead of the "$" of
    every amount that any Apollo RP plugin prints for him: in chat, console and centre text, in the HUD panel, in the H9 menus (the shops' prices, the bank, the ATM) and in
    the result lines of an exchange. A player whose game says less, a bot, every log and the text menus and hudmessages keep "$"; with 0 every plugin sends what stock ARP
    sends. A message hook cannot reach another plugin's chat line (AMX Mod X sends it straight to the engine), so include\ApolloRP_Credits.inc redirects every client_print
    of an Apollo RP plugin through arp_client_print; docs\ARP_INVENTORY.txt, 7, says where each kind of text is converted.
  * THE ARRANGEMENT IS KEPT. Where a player put his items is stored in a table of its own, arp_h9_cells (one row for each character: the cells as text with the item names),
    written a few seconds after the cells last changed and with ARP's own save of the character, and laid over what he owns when his character loads: counts that are lower are
    clipped, items he no longer has are left out, the limits of the day are obeyed, and whatever he owns that no saved cell took is added to his cells. A missing or unreadable row
    or a database that is down leaves the packed cells. arp_h9_inv_persist 0 packs at every join and writes nothing. The counts (arp_items) stay ARP's.
  * STOCK ARP. arp_h9_inv_slots 0 refuses nothing; arp_h9_inv_window 0 or arp_h9_vgui 0 gives every player the text menu; arp_h9_inv_persist 0 packs the cells at every join; arp_h9_search 0
    leaves a handcuffed player alone; with the plugin not loaded or paused the core answers none of its hooks and is stock Apollo RP. No row of any ARP table changes and there is no migration; the one table the plugin
    adds is made when it is first needed.
