NOTICE -- the third-party parts of H9's Kokoro text to speech (the folder ts\h9tts of the game)

This folder holds what the client's Kokoro voices speak with. None of it is H9's own work except voices.txt, chains.txt, the program h9kokoro64.exe
and the line pack; the rest, and what those are made from, is credited here. The licence text of each part is in the file named beside it.

KOKORO-82M, the text to speech model and its voices
  Made by hexgrad (https://github.com/hexgrad/kokoro, https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M), Apache License 2.0: LICENSE-kokoro-apache-2.0.txt.
  The model file (model\model_quantized.onnx) is the 8-bit ONNX version of the model from the Hugging Face repository onnx-community/Kokoro-82M-v1.0-ONNX
  (revision 1939ad2a8e416c0acfeecc08a694d14ef25f2231, file onnx/model_quantized.onnx, 92,361,116 bytes, unchanged). The voice files (voices\*.bin) are
  the style files of that repository, unchanged; voices.txt blends some of them (a weighted mean of their style vectors).
  The model card of Kokoro-82M lists the audio the model was trained on, among it recordings under CC BY licences (Koniwa tnc, CC BY 3.0; SIWIS, CC BY 4.0).
  Credit: "kokoro-82m text to speech model and voices - hexgrad (Apache 2.0)".

MISAKI, the pronunciation data and the algorithm of the G2P
  g2p\en_us.h9lex is made from the files misaki/data/us_gold.json and us_silver.json of misaki 0.9.4 (https://github.com/hexgrad/misaki, hexgrad, Apache
  License 2.0: LICENSE-misaki-apache-2.0.txt; sha256 dc414872... and de8f67be... of the two files). The files were CHANGED: converted to a binary lexicon (the
  case variants folded, the symbols stored as vocabulary ids); no entry was changed. g2p\en_us.h9lt (letter-to-sound trees) was trained on those two files and
  is derived from them. The part of the helper that turns text into phonemes (tools\h9kokoro64\src) follows the algorithm of misaki/en.py (Apache-2.0) and
  was written anew in C++; its tests compare it with a Python reference line by line.
  The upstream project does not document where the entries of its lexicons come from. A check against CMUdict (Carnegie Mellon University, BSD style
  licence, "we request that you acknowledge its origin") found that the gold lexicon agrees with it where both have a word, so: pronunciations partly derived
  from CMUdict (Carnegie Mellon University) is the credit that fits.
  Credit: "the misaki phoneme dictionaries - hexgrad (Apache 2.0)".

ONNX RUNTIME, the program that runs the model
  Microsoft (https://github.com/microsoft/onnxruntime), MIT licence: bin\LICENSE-onnxruntime-mit.txt, with its list of third-party components in
  bin\ThirdPartyNotices-onnxruntime.txt. bin\onnxruntime.dll and bin\onnxruntime_providers_shared.dll are the files of the release 1.31.1 for Windows x64,
  unchanged. Credit: "run by onnx runtime - microsoft (MIT)".

NOT PART OF IT
  espeak-ng (GPL) is not used and not shipped: words the lexicon does not have are done by the letter-to-sound trees. spaCy and num2words are not used: the
  tokenizer is rules and the number words are written out in the helper.
